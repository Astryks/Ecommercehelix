## Day detail

### Day 1: Write who your product is for

*Topic: Who it is for · Stage 1 · 10 minutes · SOP 16*

**Why it matters.** If you try to sell to everyone, your ads and pages speak to no one. One clear sentence about your buyer makes every later step easier.

**How to do it.**

1. Finish this sentence: 'My product is for [person] who wants [result] but struggles with [problem].'
2. Keep it under 25 words.
3. Read it to a friend. Ask them to say it back in their own words.
4. If they get it wrong, make the sentence simpler and try again.
5. Save the final sentence. You will use it in your ads and on your home page.

**Want me to do it for you?** Not for this one. It is quick to do yourself and you learn the most by doing it.

### Day 2: Check that people already want it

*Topic: Is there demand? · Stage 1 · 15 minutes · SOP 16*

**Why it matters.** The cheapest test is the one you run before spending on ads. If people already search for, talk about and buy products like yours, you are in a good place.

**How to do it.**

1. Search Google for what you sell. Write down 3 competitors that come up.
2. Read 20 reviews of those competitors. Note what people love and what they complain about.
3. Search TikTok or Instagram for the product type. Do people post about it?
4. Give your idea a score from 1 to 5 for demand. Under 3? Look at how to make the product or offer more different.

**Want me to do it for you?** Not for this one. It is quick to do yourself and you learn the most by doing it.

### Day 3: Work out what one sale really costs you

*Topic: Your cost per sale · Stage 1 · 10 minutes · SOP 02*

**Why it matters.** A sale can lose money even at a good price. You need to know every cost that comes with one order before you set prices or spend on ads.

**How to do it.**

1. Write your product cost (what you pay the supplier for one unit).
2. Add packaging and the shipping you pay to send it.
3. Add payment fees. Use about 3% of the price if you are not sure.
4. Add the total. This is your cost per sale before ads.
5. Price minus this cost is what you have left to pay for ads and keep as profit.

**Want me to do it for you?** Work out your cost per sale from your prices (Free plan, after you approve).

**See the effect.** Update your numbers each morning. Over the next 7 days, watch: Profit at the top of your dashboard.

### Day 4: Check your price leaves room for ads

*Topic: A price that leaves profit · Stage 1 · 10 minutes · SOP 04*

**Why it matters.** New stores often price too low and then cannot afford ads. A simple check now saves months of selling at a loss.

**How to do it.**

1. Take your price and your cost per sale from yesterday.
2. Aim for a price of at least 3 times your product cost. Many small stores need this to cover ads and still make money.
3. If you are below that, look at bundles, a higher-value version, or a cheaper supplier.
4. Write down the most you could pay in ads for one sale and still break even: price minus cost per sale.

**Want me to do it for you?** Not for this one. It is quick to do yourself and you learn the most by doing it.

**See the effect.** Update your numbers each morning. Over the next 7 days, watch: Profit at the top of your dashboard.

### Day 5: Write your one-line promise

*Topic: Your promise · Stage 1 · 10 minutes · SOP 04*

**Why it matters.** People decide in seconds. A clear promise tells them what they get and why it is better, before they scroll away.

**How to do it.**

1. Write one line: the result your customer gets, not the features.
2. Add why it is different, in plain words. For example: 'Soft as cotton, dries in an hour.'
3. Cut any word a 12-year-old would not understand.
4. Put the line at the top of your home page and under your product title.

### Day 15: Set your test budget and your stop rule

*Topic: Test budget · Stage 3 · 10 minutes · SOP 06*

**Why it matters.** Your first campaign is a test, not a bet. Decide how much you can afford to learn with, and when to stop, before you start.

**How to do it.**

1. Pick a total test budget you can afford to lose, for example $150 to $300.
2. Split it over 7 to 10 days. That is often $20 to $30 a day.
3. Write your stop rule: if one ad spends twice your break-even cost per sale with no sale, turn it off.
4. Write your keep rule: if an ad gets sales at or under your break-even cost, keep it running.

**Want me to do it for you?** Not for this one. It is quick to do yourself and you learn the most by doing it.

**See the effect.** Update your numbers each morning. Over the next 7 days, watch: Ad cost per sale (CPA) compared with your break-even.

### Day 20: Read what your test is telling you

**Why it matters.** Even without many sales, the numbers show where the problem is: the ad, the page, or the offer.

**How to do it.**
