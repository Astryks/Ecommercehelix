# Daily email sequence: founder story sources (checked 20 Oct 2026)

The 30-email daily sequence lives in `src/lib/email/sequence.ts`. Lesson emails are drawn from the Helix Playbook lessons they link to (`docs/playbook`). The eight founder stories use only the facts below. Prices are in US dollars.

| Email | Claim used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sara Blakely (Spanx) | Sold fax machines door to door for seven years; cut the feet off her pantyhose to wear under white pants; launched Spanx in 2000 with US$5,000 savings; no business degree; heard "no" from manufacturers for two years; put Spanx by every register at Neiman Marcus and was allowed to keep doing it | Stanford GSB: https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/sara-blakely-start-small-think-big-scale-fast |
| Phil Knight (Nike) | Ran for coach Bill Bowerman at Oregon; in 1964 they shook hands, put in US$500 each and ordered 300 pairs as Blue Ribbon Sports; Knight sold shoes from the trunk of his green Plymouth Valiant; the Nike brand came later | Nike history page (archived 2014): https://web.archive.org/web/20140515080658/http:/nikeinc.com/pages/history-heritage |
| Ben Francis (Gymshark) | Pizza Hut earnings at 19; started in his parents' garage; he and Lewis Morgan bought a sewing machine and screen printer; his mum sent sewing videos; 2013 expo with no ad money, gave free product to fitness YouTubers; sales rose from about US$450 to US$45,000 a day (per Forbes) | CNBC, 26 May 2023: https://www.cnbc.com/2023/05/26/gymshark-ceo-ben-francis-from-pizza-hut-sewing-kit-to-billionaire.html |
| Kevin Plank (Under Armour) | Hated sweat-soaked cotton shirts as a college football player; 1996; grandmother's basement; a garment maker made 500 shirts; drove them to colleges in his car trunk; first sale 200 shirts at US$12 each; US$17,000 sales in 1996 | TIME: https://time.com/archive/6668005/tight-skivvies/ ; Under Armour company history: http://investor.underarmour.com/company-history/1996-1998 ; CNBC 2019: https://www.cnbc.com/2019/10/22/under-armour-ceo-kevin-plank-steps-down-as-ceo-how-he-got-here.html |
| Warby Parker | Four business-school friends; glasses could cost over US$700; first planned US$45, but cost of goods doubled; research showed US$100 felt fair; launched February 2010; 20,000-person waitlist within four weeks | Wharton Magazine, 10 May 2013: https://magazine.wharton.upenn.edu/digital/neil-blumenthal-shares-the-warby-parker-story/ |
| Bombas | David Heath and Randy Goldberg read that socks are the most requested clothing item in homeless shelters; two years developing the sock; one pair purchased = one pair donated; goal of 1 million pairs in 10 years reached in 2.5 years | Sourcing Journal / WWD, 8 Mar 2019: https://wwd.com/footwear-news/shoe-industry-news/bombas-qanda-heathgoldberg-1238731174/ |
| Emily Weiss (Glossier) | Founded Into The Gloss in 2010, known for profiles of women's beauty routines; the team launched Glossier in 2014, "inspired by the people who use them" | Into The Gloss, About: https://intothegloss.com/pages/about/ |
| Yvon Chouinard (Patagonia) | 1957, bought a used forge and taught himself blacksmithing; shop in a chicken coop in his parents' backyard in Burbank; forged two pitons an hour, sold for US$1.50 each; sold gear from the back of his car; lived on fifty cents to a dollar a day for weeks at a time; Patagonia came later (1973) | The Guardian, 15 Sep 2022: https://www.theguardian.com/global/2022/sep/15/yvon-chouinard-the-existential-dirtbag-who-founded-and-gifted-patagonia ; Commoncog case: https://commoncog.com/c/cases/building-patagonia/ |

Lesson-email facts come from the linked lessons: carts left behind (about 7 in 10, Lesson 9.11, citing Baymard: https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate), product cost 20% to 40% (Lesson 1.2), free shipping about 20 to 30% above AOV (Lesson 9.11), the 90-day stock example (Lesson 14.12), the 30% target example (Lesson 1.5), the dog-lead example (Lesson 29.2).
