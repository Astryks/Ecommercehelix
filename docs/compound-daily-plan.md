# The Helix Compound Plan

**How to grow an online store a little every day, and let the gains multiply.**

This is the operating system behind Ecommerce Helix. The app turns it into three tasks a day, but it also works on paper. Every number below is a starting default. Helix tunes it to your store once it has your data.

---

## 1. Why daily, and why it compounds

A store's profit comes from a handful of levers that **multiply**:

```
Revenue        = Sessions x Conversion rate x Average order value
Gross margin   = Revenue x (1 - Variable cost ratio)
Daily profit   = Gross margin - Ad spend - Daily share of fixed costs
```

Because the levers multiply, small gains on several levers at once beat a big gain on one.

**Worked example (a $30k/month store):**

| | Before | After 90 days of small gains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sessions / month | 10,000 | 11,000 (+10%) |
| Conversion rate | 2.0% | 2.2% (+10%) |
| Average order value | $150 | $165 (+10%) |
| Revenue per visit | $3.00 | $3.63 |
| Revenue | $30,000 | $39,930 |
| Variable costs (40%) | $12,000 | $15,972 |
| Fixed costs | $6,000 | $6,000 |
| Ad spend | $9,000 | $9,900 (+10%) |
| **Profit** | **$3,000 (10%)** | **$8,058 (20%)** |

Three 10% improvements lift revenue by 33%. Profit goes up 2.7 times, because fixed costs do not grow with sales.

**The 1% rule of thumb.** If a habit makes the business 1% better each week, the gains compound to about 68% over a year (1.01^52). If small daily actions improve one lever by 1% every few days, you get about 1.35x in 30 compounding steps and about 2.45x in 90 (1.01^90). Real gains do not stack forever. A lever saturates once it reaches a healthy benchmark. That is why Helix keeps moving you to the **next weakest lever**, not the one you already fixed.

**What compounds best:**
- Winning ads and hooks (each test teaches the next brief).
- Email subscribers and flows (built once, they earn every day).
- Reviews, UGC and proof (each one lowers the cost of the next sale).
- Site improvements (every visitor from every channel benefits).
- Cost reductions (every future order is more profitable).
- Your own decision log (you stop repeating mistakes).

## 2. The daily loop (15 to 30 minutes)

Do it at the same time every day, ideally before you open email or Slack. Helix shows it as the **Today** screen: one scorecard card and up to three tasks. On a light day there may be only one task. That is a good sign.

### Step 1. Check the scorecard (3 minutes)

Helix fills this in automatically once Shopify and your ad accounts are connected. Without connections you type five numbers.

**Checklist**
- [ ] Yesterday's revenue, orders and sessions.
- [ ] Yesterday's ad spend per channel (Meta, Google, TikTok, other).
- [ ] Yesterday's estimated contribution profit (revenue minus variable costs minus ad spend minus the daily share of fixed costs).
- [ ] **MER%** (total ad spend ÷ total revenue) for the last 3 days and for the month to date, each against your target.
- [ ] Revenue per visit (RPV), conversion rate (CR) and average order value (AOV) versus your 30-day average.
- [ ] New-customer orders versus returning-customer orders.

**Read it like this**

| 3-day MER | Month-to-date MER | Today's stance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On target | On target | **Push.** Scale winning campaigns by up to 20% today, light tidy-up only. |
| On target | Off target | **Careful push.** Scale 10 to 20% on the best campaigns, regular tidy-up, check what went wrong earlier in the month. |
| Off target | On target | **Hold.** No budget increases. Regular optimisation. Look at what changed in the last 3 days. |
| Off target | Off target | **Defend.** Cut spend by up to 20% a day, aggressive optimisation, run the full diagnosis (SOP 03). |

> Your target MER is not a guess. It falls out of your economics:
> **Target MER% = 100% - variable cost % - fixed cost % - target profit %.**
> Example: variable 40%, fixed 15%, target profit 15%, so target MER is 30%.

**Red flags that override everything else (fix today):**
- Revenue or sessions dropped more than 40% versus the same weekday last week, with no planned reason. Check the site is up, checkout works, tracking fires and ads are delivering.
- A best seller went out of stock. Pause or redirect ads pointing at it.
- An ad account is disabled or a payment failed.
- An email or SMS is about to go out with a broken link or wrong discount.

### Step 2. One ad action (5 to 10 minutes)

Pick **one** from the list. Helix chooses for you based on the stance above.

- [ ] **Scale:** raise budget by up to 20% on the campaign with the best cost per purchase that still has low frequency.
- [ ] **Cut:** turn off the ad that has spent at least your "bad" cost-per-purchase threshold with no purchase, or that has a bad cost per purchase **and** high frequency.
- [ ] **Refresh:** if a campaign has no strong ads left and fewer than 30% of its original ads still perform, load a new batch from your build campaign.
- [ ] **Launch a test:** put a new batch of 4 to 8 ads (testing one variable) live in your testing campaign.
- [ ] **Google check:** add 5 negative keywords from the search-terms report, or fix one Merchant Center product error.
- [ ] **Do nothing on purpose:** if a campaign is in learning or was changed in the last 3 days, leave it alone. Write "held" in the log.

**Rules that keep you safe**
- Never move a campaign budget more than 20% in a day.
- Never judge an ad before it has spent about 1 to 1.5 times your target cost per purchase (unless it is a very high-priced product. Then use early signals: click-through rate, cost per click, add-to-carts).
- After a budget step-up, give the new level 10 to 14 days before you judge MER. It usually gets worse first.
- Do not add ads to a campaign that is winning. Adding ads resets learning.

### Step 3. One site or conversion action (5 to 10 minutes)

Pick the next item from your **Site Fix List** (Helix builds this from the audit and keeps it ranked by expected impact ÷ effort).

Examples of a single daily site action:
- [ ] Add one missing reason-to-buy line under the product title on your best seller.
- [ ] Replace the main top image with a mobile-first 4:5 version.
- [ ] Add 3 photo reviews to the top of a product page.
- [ ] Write one FAQ answer for the most common pre-sale question this week.
- [ ] Fix one broken link, slow image or confusing menu item.
- [ ] Check one test: is the A/B test at enough visitors to call? If yes, call it and log the result.
- [ ] Walk through checkout on your phone. Note one friction point.

**Rule:** if RPV is below your benchmark, site and offer actions come **before** new ad spend. Ads only fix ad problems.

### Step 4. One retention action (5 minutes)

- [ ] Check yesterday's flow revenue and pop-up sign-ups. Anything at zero that should not be?
- [ ] Approve or edit the next scheduled email (Helix drafts it).
- [ ] Reply to two reviews, or send one review request to a recent happy customer.
- [ ] Add one email to a flow that is missing a step (welcome, browse, cart, checkout, post-purchase).
- [ ] Tag one customer insight from support tickets or reviews in your research log.

### Step 5. One learning (3 minutes)

- [ ] Read today's one-card tip or trend from the Helix **Trends** feed (a format, hook or platform change), or
- [ ] Answer Helix's daily question ("What did customers ask most this week?"), or
- [ ] Write one line in your decision log: what you changed, why, and what you expect to happen.

The decision log is the most underrated compounding asset. In 90 days it becomes your own playbook.

## 3. Day-of-week themes

The daily loop stays the same. The theme decides which "one action" Helix picks first and which bigger task (30 to 60 minutes) it suggests for the week.

| Day | Theme | Bigger task suggested this day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | **Numbers and budget** | Weekly scorecard review, set this week's spend range, pick the one priority for the week. |
| Tuesday | **Creative** | Write or approve next week's creative briefs. Review last week's test results. |
| Wednesday | **Launch** | Launch the new ad test batch. Load refresh batches where needed. |
| Thursday | **Site** | Start or call one site test. Ship one landing page improvement. |
| Friday | **Retention** | Send the weekly email campaign. Review flow performance and list growth. |
| Saturday | **Customer** | Read reviews, support tickets and competitor ads for 20 minutes. Log 3 insights. |
| Sunday | **Rest** | Scorecard glance only. Helix only alerts on red flags. |

## 4. The weekly rhythm (60 to 90 minutes, Monday)

**Weekly scorecard (15 min)**
- [ ] Revenue, profit, MER%, RPV, CR, AOV, new-customer orders: this week vs last week vs 4-week average.
- [ ] Spend and cost per purchase by channel and by funnel layer.
- [ ] Email: flow revenue, campaign revenue, list growth, unsubscribes.
- [ ] Stock: any product under 4 weeks of cover, or over 6 months?

**Creative testing cadence (20 min)**
- [ ] Grade last week's test batch (keep, iterate, kill). Write one sentence on why the winners won.
- [ ] Brief the next batch: about 70% iterations of proven ideas, 30% new ideas.
- [ ] Batch size by daily campaign spend: up to $100/day means 4 to 8 ads; $100 to $500 means 8 to 12; over $500 means 12 to 15.
- [ ] Keep about 70 to 80% of spend on proven campaigns and 20 to 30% on testing.

**Budget review (10 min)**
- [ ] Set this week's daily spend range from the stance table.
- [ ] Check the funnel split (default: cold 30 to 50%, mixed 30 to 40%, warm 20 to 30% of Meta spend).
- [ ] Google: review search terms, add negatives, check the share of spend on brand vs non-brand.

**Offer review (10 min)**
- [ ] Is the main offer still clear and competitive? Did a competitor start a sale?
- [ ] Any upcoming date in the next 6 weeks that needs a plan (SOP 15)?
- [ ] Are the cart tiers (free shipping, gift, discount) still set at sensible spend levels for current AOV?

**Plan the week (10 min)**
- [ ] Pick **one priority** for the week (for example "launch the post-purchase flow" or "build a landing page for the bundle").
- [ ] Helix breaks it into daily chunks that fit inside the loop.

## 5. Monthly rhythm (2 hours, first working day of the month)

- [ ] **Economics review.** Variable cost ratio, fixed cost ratio, MER%, net profit % for the month. Compare to targets. Run constraint diagnosis (SOP 03) if any ratio is outside its band.
- [ ] **Cost sweep.** List every subscription and app. Cancel one you do not use. Ask one supplier or 3PL for a better rate.
- [ ] **Customer value.** Repeat purchase rate, 90-day and 12-month customer value by first-order month (cohorts). Is new-customer value rising or falling?
- [ ] **Product fit snapshot.** For each product: ad spend vs return last month. Products with high spend **and** good return get more creative next month.
- [ ] **Promo calendar.** Confirm the next 90 days of launches, sales and key dates. Book creative and stock.
- [ ] **Stock and cash.** Weeks of cover by product, upcoming purchase orders, 3-month cash forecast. Move about 25% of the month's profit into a tax account if that suits your structure (check with your accountant).
- [ ] **Roadmap check.** Review what Helix has done and what it suggests unlocking next.

## 6. Quarterly rhythm (half a day)

- [ ] **Six-week priority cycles.** Choose one or two big priorities for the next six weeks (for example "launch Google Shopping", "rebuild the welcome flow", "fix shipping costs"). Block 8 to 12 hours a week for them.
- [ ] **Pricing and offer.** Test a price change, a new bundle, a guarantee, or free-shipping threshold.
- [ ] **Hiring and ops.** What will break if revenue doubles? Plan roles 3 to 6 months ahead. Hire for outcomes, test with a paid work sample.
- [ ] **Channel review.** Add a channel only when current channels are profitable and you have creative to feed it.
- [ ] **Peak planning.** If a big season is within 4 months, start stock, creative and list-building plans now.
- [ ] **Year view.** Update the revenue target and back-solve stock and marketing budgets.

## 7. Thresholds and decision rules

These are Helix defaults. They are starting points, not laws. Helix adjusts them by category (fashion, beauty, home, high-ticket) and by your own history.

### 7.1 Business health bands

| Metric | Healthy | Watch | Act now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Net profit % | 10 to 20%+ | 0 to 10% | Below 0% |
| MER% (ad spend ÷ revenue) | 20 to 35% | 35 to 45% | Above 45%, or above your computed target for 7+ days |
| Variable cost ratio (product, shipping, packaging, fees ÷ revenue) | Under 40% | 40 to 50% | Above 50%. Above 60% makes paid ads close to impossible. |
| Fixed cost ratio | 10 to 20% | 20 to 30% | Above 30% (but growth often fixes this) |
| Shipping and fulfilment ÷ revenue | Under 10% | 10 to 15% | Above 15% |
| Payment fees | About 2 to 3% | | Above 3.5% |
| Revenue per visit | $2.50 to $4 (category dependent) | $1 to $2.50 | Below $1 |
| Meta outbound click-through rate | 1 to 1.5%+ | 0.5 to 1% | Below 0.5% |
| Pop-up sign-up rate | 6 to 10% | 3 to 6% | Below 3% |
| Flow open rate / click rate | Above 35% / above 1% | | Below 25% / below 0.5% |
| Campaign unsubscribe rate | Under 0.3% | 0.3 to 0.5% | Above 0.5% |
| Repeat purchase rate (12 months) | Above 25% | 15 to 25% | Below 15% (do not build a loyalty program yet) |
| Weeks of stock cover (top sellers) | 6 to 16 weeks | 4 to 6 or 16 to 26 | Under 4, or over 26 |

### 7.2 "Is it the ads or the site?"

```
MER too high?
├─ First: any temporary shock? (stockout, sale hangover, competitor sale, season, tracking break)
│     yes -> fix or wait it out, do not rebuild anything
├─ Revenue per visit below benchmark?
│     yes -> it is a SITE / OFFER problem
│            ├─ AOV low  -> bundles, cart tiers, post-purchase upsell (SOP 12)
│            └─ CR low   -> clarity, proof, risk reversal, offer work (SOP 04, 11)
└─ RPV fine?
      -> it is an AD problem
         ├─ Outbound CTR < 0.5%  -> creative / message / offer in the ad (SOP 07, 08)
         ├─ CTR fine, cost per click high vs RPV -> CPMs high: audience saturation,
         │                                          frequency, season (SOP 06)
         └─ Engaging ads with poor purchase cost -> landing page does not match the ad (SOP 11)
```

### 7.3 Ad decision rules (Meta defaults)

Helix grades every ad that has spent enough to judge. It uses two numbers: **cost per purchase (CPP)** over the last 4 days and **frequency** over the last 7 days.

- Good CPP threshold = AOV x target MER x layer multiplier.
- Bad CPP threshold = 2 x good threshold.
- Layer multipliers: Cold 2.0, Mixed 1.25, Warm 1.0, Hot 0.75. Cold audiences look expensive because attribution undercounts them. Compare cold ads only with other cold ads.

| Layer | Frequency is fine | Frequency is high |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold (new people) | Under 1.4 | Over 1.8 |
| Mixed (mostly new, some warm) | Under 1.8 | Over 2.0 |
| Warm (visitors, engagers, subscribers) | Under 2.0 | Over 2.4 |
| Hot (cart and checkout, past buyers) | Under 2.4 | Over 3.0 |

| Grade | Rule | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Star** | Good CPP, fine frequency | Give it more budget. Copy it into other campaigns ("take it on tour"). Brief iterations. |
| **Steady** | Good CPP but high frequency, or middling CPP with fine frequency | Keep it running. Prepare a refresh. |
| **Passenger** | Middling CPP and rising frequency | Watch. Turn off when a new batch is ready. |
| **Drain** | Bad CPP, or spent the bad threshold with zero purchases | Turn off today. |

**Scale and kill rules**
- Scale vertically (same campaign) by up to 20% a day when both 3-day and month-to-date MER are on target.
- Scale horizontally (new campaign, new audience, new country) when vertical scaling stops working or you are at 20% steps already.
- After any step-up, hold 10 to 14 days. If MER does not settle back toward target, step back down and diagnose.
- The worst case of a scale test is the extra spend. Decide that number before you start.
- Ads with huge reach at low frequency are worth more than their CPP suggests. Prefer them when scaling.

### 7.4 Google decision rules

- Breakeven ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin after variable costs. Example: 60% margin means breakeven ROAS of 1.67 (167%). Set target ROAS above breakeven by the share of profit you want to keep.
- Accept a lower ROAS on campaigns that bring mostly new customers.
- Do not make big changes to a new Performance Max campaign in its first 2 weeks.
- Judge a new campaign after about 2 weeks of learning plus 4 weeks of data, at no less than about $50/day.
- Review search terms weekly. Add negatives. Keep brand search separate and modest.

### 7.5 Site test rules

- Change one thing at a time. Split traffic 50/50.
- Call a test only at about 95% confidence, or after 2 full weeks with no clear winner (keep the simpler version).
- Fix speed only if it saves at least 1 to 2 seconds.
- A cheap template change that you ship this week beats a custom redesign next quarter.

### 7.6 When to stop scaling and fix

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Profit above 10% and ratios healthy | Scale. Buy every customer you can profitably serve. |
| Fixed costs high, everything else fine | Scale carefully. Growth spreads fixed costs. Cut obvious waste but keep what you need to grow into. |
| Variable costs high | Stop scaling. Fix product cost, shipping, packaging, pricing, AOV or product mix first. Scale cannot fix this. |
| MER high | Diagnose with 7.2 before touching ads. |
| Everything is borderline | Fix MER first, then variable costs, then scale. |

## 8. The 90-day roadmap by store stage

Helix places you in a stage from the audit and your revenue. Each stage has three 30-day phases with exit criteria. You can move up early if you hit them.

### Stage 0: Pre-launch (no sales yet)

**Focus:** a product people want, an offer that is clear, and a store that converts the first visitors.

| Days | Do | Exit criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 30 | Write the one-line promise and the top 10 reasons people would not buy (SOP 04, 16). Set up cost drivers and target margin (SOP 02). Build the store on a fast standard theme. Write product pages that answer every objection. Install pixel, conversions API and analytics. Set up the welcome pop-up and welcome flow. | Store live, tracking verified, welcome flow live, cost sheet done. |
| 31 to 60 | Post organic short-form video 3 to 5 times a week to learn which angles get attention. Build a bank of 10 to 15 ad-ready videos. Seed product to 20 to 50 creators or friends for first reviews and UGC. Collect emails with a launch list. | 10+ creative assets, 10+ reviews, 200+ launch list subscribers. |
| 61 to 90 | Launch with a planned event (hype emails, launch-day offer). Start Meta with one broad cold campaign plus one warm campaign at a budget you can afford to lose while learning. Daily loop starts. | First 50 orders, CR and RPV baseline measured, first creative test graded. |

### Stage 1: Under $10k a month

**Focus:** find one profitable way to get customers. Do not spread across channels.

| Days | Do | Exit criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 30 | Daily scorecard live. Fix the top 5 audit issues on the site. Five core email flows live (welcome, browse, cart, checkout, post-purchase). One Meta structure: one cold campaign, one warm campaign. Weekly test batch of 4 to 6 ads. | RPV measured against benchmark, flows live, two test batches graded. |
| 31 to 60 | Sharpen the offer (risk reversal, proof, bundle). Build one landing page for the best ad angle and test it against the product page. Start a review-request flow. | One winning ad angle with repeatable CPP within target. Pop-up sign-up rate above 3%. |
| 61 to 90 | Scale the winner in 20% steps. Add cart tiers for AOV. Start a weekly email campaign. Consider Google Shopping (feed-only) only if Meta is profitable and the feed is clean. | Positive contribution profit for 4 straight weeks. MER at or under target. |

### Stage 2: $10k to $100k a month

**Focus:** systemise creative and spread fixed costs by growing.

| Days | Do | Exit criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 30 | Full Meta structure across cold, mixed, warm and hot layers. Daily grading. A build campaign for staging new ads. Google: feed-only Shopping or PMax plus a separate brand search campaign. Monthly economics review. | Funnel split within ranges. Search terms reviewed weekly. Monthly review done. |
| 31 to 60 | Creator and UGC pipeline: brief 4 to 10 creators a month. 70/30 iterate vs new. Site testing program: one test running at all times. Email: 1 to 2 campaigns a week with segmentation. | Creative velocity of 8+ new ads a week. One site test called per fortnight. |
| 61 to 90 | Plan the next big sale or launch (SOP 15). Demand planning for stock (SOP 17). Consider a second country or channel if profitable. Hire or outsource the first repeatable role (editor, VA, customer service). | 90-day promo calendar set. Stock cover in band. One role delegated with a written SOP. |

### Stage 3: $100k+ a month

**Focus:** protect margin, build the team, and plan peaks a year ahead.

| Days | Do | Exit criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 31 to 60 | Advanced creative: dedicated test campaign, partnership ads with creators, catalog video, new formats from the Trends feed. Google: margin-tier campaigns, new vs returning segmentation, Demand Gen tests. Loyalty only if repeat rate is above 15 to 20%. | Two new channels or formats tested with clear results. |
| 61 to 90 | Org chart for the next 12 months. Outcome-based job descriptions. Six-week priority cycles. A full-year plan built in advance (revenue target, with stock and marketing worked back from it). | Year plan signed off. Next two hires planned with work-sample tests. |

## 9. Common failure modes Helix watches for

| Failure | What it looks like | Helix response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chasing platform ROAS | Ads Manager says 4x but the bank account is flat | Switch the scorecard to MER and contribution profit. |
| Fiddling | Budgets and ads changed every day, campaigns never leave learning | "Do nothing on purpose" task. Lock changes for 3 days after edits. |
| Scaling a broken store | Doubling spend while RPV is under $1.50 | Block scale suggestions until the site fix list moves RPV. |
| Creative drought | No new ads for 3+ weeks, frequency climbing, CPP rising | Refresh batch reminder, brief generator, Trends feed ideas. |
| Same note every time | All ads use one angle or one format | Pillar balance check (teach, prove, feel, stand for). |
| Over-discounting | Store-wide sale every month, customers wait for sales | Limit big sales to 3 or 4 peaks a year. Suggest gift-with-purchase or bundles instead. |
| Email neglect | Flows built years ago, no campaigns, list going cold | Flow health check. Weekly campaign draft. |
| Sale hangover panic | Revenue drops after a sale and ads get rebuilt | Expect it. Hold structure, warm up evergreen spend gradually. |
| Stockout from success | Scaling sells out a best seller weeks early | Weeks-of-cover alert linked to ad budget suggestions. |
| Tracking breaks silently | Two "primary" purchase conversions, pixel currency mismatch | Daily tracking sanity check against Shopify orders. |
| Founder bottleneck | Every task waits for the owner | Weekly delegation suggestion with a ready-to-send SOP. |
