# Module 4: Brand, content and community

**Outcome:** a clear brand that people recognise, content pillars for each channel, and a plan to build community and reach.

---

## Lesson 4.1: The brand core

Answer five questions on one page:
1. **Who is it for?** Two or three customer profiles (avatars) with their goals, frustrations and where they spend time.
2. **Why choose us?** Brand promise in one sentence.
3. **How do we sound?** Three personality words and three words we never use.
4. **What do we stand for?** Two or three values you can prove with actions.
5. **What is the story?** Why the brand exists, in under 100 words.

Brands without a clear core compete on price and discounts. Brands with one rely less on discounting because trust does the selling.

## Lesson 4.2: Customer profiles

For each profile: age range, life stage, the job they hire your product for, top 3 objections, words they use, where they buy, what they compare you with. Build them from reviews, surveys and support tickets (Module 16 in SOPs), not imagination.

## Lesson 4.3: Brand guidelines

A short guide anyone (freelancer, creator, AI tool) can follow:
- [ ] Logo versions and clear space.
- [ ] Primary and secondary colours with codes.
- [ ] Fonts and sizes.
- [ ] Photo and video style (light, framing, props).
- [ ] Voice examples: good and bad.
- [ ] Do and don't examples for ads, emails and social.
Recognition comes from repeated, consistent exposure.

## Lesson 4.4: Content pillars by channel

Choose 4 to 6 pillars, for example product, education, proof, community, promotion, brand story and values. Weight them differently by channel:

| Pillar | Instagram | TikTok | Email |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product | High | Low | Medium |
| Education | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| Community and story | Medium | High (where reach comes from) | Low |
| Promotion | Low | Low | High (people subscribe to hear first) |

Write your own weights, then plan a month of posts against them.

## Lesson 4.5: Communication calendar

1. Mark promotional dates first (launches, sales, key gifting days).
2. Add product stories and education around them.
3. Fill gaps with community and proof.
4. Always ask permission before reposting customer content.

## Lesson 4.6: Organic content that also feeds ads

- Post short-form video 3 to 5 times a week.
- Treat organic as free creative testing: posts that hold attention become ad candidates.
- Use trial or test posting features where platforms offer them to try ideas on non-followers.
- Engage in comments within the first hour.
- Realistic output for a small team: 3 to 5 short videos a week, batched in one or two filming sessions.

## Lesson 4.7: Community

- Give customers a place and reason to talk (private group, events, challenges, user-generated content features).
- Celebrate customers in content and make them the focus.
- Use community questions to find new content ideas and objections.
- Community works best with some repeat behaviour; for very low repeat categories, focus on reviews and creators instead.

## Lesson 4.8: Personal brand and PR

- A visible founder adds trust and gives content an endless source: opinions, behind the scenes, lessons.
- PR: pitch stories (founder journey, data, trends), not products. Reuse press logos and quotes as proof in ads and on site.

## Lesson 4.9: Influencer amplification

Gifting at scale, then pay the best performers for usage rights, then run their content as partnership ads (Module 5 and 7).
