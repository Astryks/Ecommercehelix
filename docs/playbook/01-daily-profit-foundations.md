# Module 1: Daily profit foundations

**Outcome:** every morning you know if yesterday made money. You also know the few numbers that guide every other choice in this course.

**Time:** 60 to 90 minutes to set up once, then 2 minutes a day.
**Related SOPs:** [02 Scorecard](../sops/02-scorecard-and-unit-economics.md), [03 Constraint diagnosis](../sops/03-constraint-diagnosis.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Grow (keep more of every sale, then scale). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** Each morning you check one number: did yesterday make a profit?
>
> **Why it matters:** Sales can go up while you lose money. Profit is what pays you, so every other choice starts here.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Write down your costs once: product cost, shipping, payment fees and monthly bills.
> 2. Each morning, type yesterday's sales, orders and ad spend into Helix (2 minutes).
> 3. Read the profit number and the one line that tells you what to do today.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Profit:** Usually means contribution profit: what is left from sales after product cost, shipping, payment fees, discounts, refunds and ads. Net profit also takes off fixed costs.
> - **Revenue:** The money customers paid for orders. Helix uses net sales: after discounts and refunds, without GST or VAT. It is not what you keep.
> - **Break-even ROAS:** The ROAS where an order makes $0 profit after its costs. Below it, ads lose money.
> - **MER:** Helix shows it as all ad spend divided by all sales, as a percentage. MER 25% means $25 of ads for every $100 of sales. Many tools flip it to sales divided by ad spend, so 25% shows as 4x.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 1.1: Why revenue is the wrong scoreboard
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Revenue (all the money customers paid you) feels like success. But it does not tell you what you kept. A $5,000 day can still lose money once you pay for ads, products and shipping. Ad platforms also each claim the same sale, so their totals add up to more sales than you really had. This lesson gets you a real starting point so you can see the gap for yourself.

**Step 1.** Find last month's sales in your store.

1. In Shopify, click **Analytics**, then **Reports**.
2. Click **Total sales over time**.
3. Set the date at the top to **Last month**.
4. Write down the **Net sales** number (sales after discounts and refunds).

**Expected result:** You have one dollar figure for last month's net sales written in a note or on paper.

**Watch out:** Use the figure without GST, VAT or sales tax. That tax belongs to the government, not to you. Shopify's Net sales already leaves tax out.

**Step 2.** Add up last month's ad spend from every platform and write down the total. In Meta, go to **Ads Manager > Campaigns**, pick last month and read the **Amount spent** column. In Google Ads, read **Campaigns > Cost**.

**Expected result:** You have one total ad spend figure for last month.

**Step 3.** Guess how much profit you made last month. Write it down as one dollar number before you do any maths.

**Expected result:** A guessed profit figure sits next to your sales and ad spend.

**Step 4.** Save the three numbers somewhere easy to find. A note on your phone works, or the notes field in Helix Settings.

**Expected result:** You can find the note later. At the end of this module you check your guess. You compare it with your real contribution profit (sales minus order costs and ads). You also compare it with net profit (what is left after bills).

## Lesson 1.2: Your cost drivers
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Cost drivers are your average costs, and they are what turn sales into profit. Get them roughly right once and every profit number in Helix becomes trustworthy. You set them up in about 30 minutes and check them every 3 months.

**Step 1.** Work out your **product cost %**. If your plan has it, use **Analytics > Reports > Gross profit by product** (fill in "Cost per item" first). Or take your top 10 sellers. Divide each one's landed cost (price paid plus freight and duty) by its selling price.

**Expected result:** You have one percentage. For many online stores it sits somewhere between 20% and 40%.

**Watch out:** Use prices without GST, VAT or sales tax. Include freight and duty, not just the factory price.

**Step 2.** Work out your **shipping and packing cost per order**. Take last month's bills from your carrier or warehouse (a 3PL is a company that stores and ships for you). Divide by the number of orders you shipped.

**Expected result:** You have a dollar amount per order, for example $9.

**Step 3.** Add **packaging per order** (boxes, mailers, tape, inserts) by dividing last month's packaging spend by orders.

**Expected result:** You have a second per-order figure, often $0.50 to $2.

**Step 4.** Find your **payment fee %**. In Shopify, go to **Settings > Payments**, click **Manage** under Shopify Payments and read **Standard rates**. Or divide last month's total fees (on your payouts page) by sales.

**Expected result:** You have a percentage, often about 2% to 3.5% once the 30 cent fee per order is counted. It depends on plan, country, card mix and buy now pay later (see [Shopify: payout fees and card rates](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/getting-paid-with-shopify-payments/view-payouts/pay-periods-and-fees)).

**Step 5.** List every **fixed cost** for one month. Include staff and helpers, a fair wage for yourself, software and apps, rent, insurance and accounting. Add loan interest and any marketing that is not ad spend (photo shoots, free product for creators, agency fees).

**Expected result:** You have one monthly total for fixed costs.

**Watch out:** Include your own wage even if you do not pay it yet. Leaving it out makes the business look healthier than it is.

**Step 6.** Save your fixed costs in Helix.

1. Open **Your numbers**, then **Targets and fixed costs**.
2. Type your monthly total into **Fixed costs per month**.
3. Click **Save targets**.

**Expected result:** The Dashboard's net profit now takes off a daily share of your fixed costs. That share is your monthly total divided by 30.4.

<!-- do:numbers -->

### Good to know

**Variable costs: they grow with every order:**

| Cost | How to work it out |
| --- | --- |
| Product cost % | What one item costs to land in your warehouse (the product, plus freight to you, plus import duties), divided by its selling price. Average it across what you sell. If your sales mix changes a lot, weight it by units sold in the last 90 days. |
| Fulfilment and shipping per order | What your warehouse or 3PL (a company that packs and ships for you) charges to pick and pack, plus postage. Take away what customers pay you for shipping. |
| Packaging per order | Box or mailer, tissue paper, inserts. |
| Payment fees | What Shopify Payments, Stripe or PayPal charge. If you do not know, use 2.6%. Your payment reports show the real figure. |
| Other per-order costs | Marketplace commission, a free gift with each order, shipping protection you pay for, buy now pay later fees. |

**Common questions**

1. **Is my own wage a cost?** Yes. Put a fair wage for yourself into fixed costs, even if you do not pay it yet. If you leave it out, the business will look healthier than it is.
2. **Do I include GST, VAT or sales tax?** No. Use sales and costs without the tax you collect and pass on to the government.
3. **How do I handle returns?** Use sales after refunds (net revenue). If you get lots of returns, add a returns cost per order. That is the return postage plus any stock you have to throw away.
4. **What about tariffs and duties on orders sent overseas?** Add them as a per-order cost for that country. Or keep a separate scorecard for each country.
5. **I make my products myself.** Your product cost % includes materials and the labour for each unit. Production staff on a salary go in fixed costs.
6. **I sell wholesale and retail.** Keep online retail separate. Give wholesale a fair share of the fixed costs.
7. **My costs will change soon.** Save each version of your costs with a start date. That keeps your past numbers accurate.
8. **I cannot get an exact average product cost.** Start with a sensible estimate from your top 10 sellers. A rough number you use every day beats a perfect number you never use.
9. **What happens to product cost during a sale?** Product cost % goes up when you give a discount. The price drops, but the cost stays the same. Use a separate cost setting for sale periods.
10. **I have several stores or regions.** Keep one scorecard per store or currency, then look at them combined.

## Lesson 1.3: The daily scorecard
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** The scorecard is a short daily record of the numbers that decide profit. In Helix it lives in **Your numbers**, and the quick version is the profit box on **Today**. You type in a handful of numbers and Helix works out the rest, so you never do the maths yourself.

**Step 1.** Each morning, open Shopify **Analytics** and set the date to **Yesterday**. Note **Gross sales**, **Orders**, **Discounts**, **Returns** and **Sessions** (a session is one visit to your store).

**Expected result:** You have five numbers for yesterday, taking under a minute.

**Step 2.** Note yesterday's ad spend for each channel: Meta **Amount spent**, Google **Cost** and TikTok **Cost**. If Meta is connected in Helix, skip it, because Helix brings it in every morning.

**Expected result:** You have one spend figure per channel you advertise on.

<!-- do:meta-connect -->

**Step 3.** Add yesterday's numbers in Helix.

1. Open **Today** in Helix.
2. In **Update yesterday**, type yesterday's sales, orders and ad spend.
3. Click **Save and show my profit**.

Want to add every cost too? Use **Your numbers > Fill in a day** instead.

**Expected result:** The profit box shows yesterday's profit with a plain-English line, and the Dashboard updates.

<!-- do:numbers -->

**Step 4.** Add the last 1 to 2 months in one go.

1. Open **Your numbers** and find **Import CSV**.
2. Click to download the template.
3. Open it and paste one row per day.
4. Save the file.
5. Click **Import** and pick the file.

**Expected result:** The Dashboard shows trends from day one instead of a single day.

**Watch out:** Dates must be in YYYY-MM-DD format, for example 2026-10-05, or the row is skipped.

### Good to know

**Helix then works out the rest for you:**

| Number | What it means | How it is worked out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variable costs | Costs that came with yesterday's orders | Revenue x product cost % + orders x per-order costs + revenue x payment fee % |
| Contribution profit | What is left after order costs and ads | Revenue - variable costs - ad spend |
| Net profit (estimate) | What the business really earned | Contribution profit - one day's share of fixed costs |
| MER % | How much of your sales went on ads | Ad spend ÷ revenue |
| VCR | How much of your sales went on order costs | Variable costs ÷ revenue |
| FCR | How much of your sales went on fixed bills | Fixed costs ÷ revenue |
| RPV | How much each visit is worth | Revenue ÷ sessions |
| Conversion rate | How many visits turn into orders | Orders ÷ sessions |
| AOV | Average order value | Revenue ÷ orders |
| Cost per visit | What ads paid for each visit | Ad spend ÷ sessions |
| Blended CAC | What ads paid for each new customer | Ad spend ÷ orders from new customers |

## Lesson 1.4: Healthy ranges
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A number on its own means little until you know whether it is healthy. These ranges tell you at a glance what is fine, what to watch and what needs action. Helix colours each one green, amber or red for you.

**Step 1.** Open **Dashboard** in Helix and pick **Month** so you are looking at the last 30 days.

**Expected result:** You see metric cards with a coloured dot on each.

<!-- do:growth -->

**Step 2.** Compare your net profit % (net profit divided by net sales) with the table below. A common guide: 10% or more is healthy, 5 to 10% is thin, below 5% is a warning sign.

**Expected result:** You know which band your profit sits in.

**Step 3.** Compare your MER with your target MER from Lesson 1.5. MER is all ad spend divided by net sales. Then compare your VCR with the table. VCR is product, shipping, packaging and fees divided by net sales.

**Expected result:** You know whether ads or order costs are the bigger squeeze.

**Step 4.** Write down the one number furthest outside its healthy range.

**Expected result:** You have a single number to work on first, which Module 2 shows you how to fix.

### Good to know

| Number | Healthy (a common guide) | Warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net profit | 10 to 20% of sales | Below 5% |
| MER % (ads as a share of sales) | 20 to 35%. It falls as more customers come back and buy again. | Above 45% |
| VCR (order costs as a share of sales) | 30 to 45% | Above 50%. Above 60% makes growing with ads very hard. |
| FCR (fixed bills as a share of sales) | 10 to 20% | Above 30% |

## Lesson 1.5: Your target MER and break-even numbers
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** These numbers tell you how much you can spend on ads and still make money. They are the line you should not cross with any ad choice. Once Helix knows your costs, it works them out for you. Doing it by hand once helps you trust them.

**Step 1.** Work out your target MER: 100% minus VCR, minus FCR, minus the profit % you want. FCR is your fixed costs divided by net sales. Example: 100 - 40 - 15 - 15 = 30%.

**Expected result:** You have a target MER, for example 30%. That means you can spend $30 on ads for every $100 of sales, across new and returning customers.

**Step 2.** Type that number into **Your numbers > Targets and fixed costs > Target MER (%)**. Then click **Save targets**.

**Expected result:** Helix rates your MER green at or under this target on every screen.

<!-- do:numbers -->

**Step 3.** Work out break-even ROAS: 1 divided by (1 minus VCR). ROAS (return on ad spend) is sales from ads divided by ad spend. With VCR at 40%, 1 / 0.6 = 1.67.

**Expected result:** You know the line. Any channel that brings back less than $1.67 for each $1 of ads loses money on every sale. That is before fixed costs.

**Step 4.** Work out break-even CPA: AOV times (1 minus VCR). CPA is the ad spend for one sale, and AOV is your average order value. With AOV $100 and VCR 40%: $60.

**Expected result:** You know the most you can pay for one sale before it loses money.

**Step 5.** Calculate target CPA as AOV times target MER. In the example: $100 x 30% = $30.

**Expected result:** You have a simple three-zone rule. Under $30 per sale is good. $30 to $60 means watch it. Over $60 means the ad loses money on every sale.

**Watch out:** Helix shows break-even ROAS and CPA on the Dashboard from your real numbers. If your hand maths differs a lot, check your cost drivers in Lesson 1.2.

## Lesson 1.6: A 2-minute morning routine
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Two minutes each morning stops small problems from becoming expensive ones. You read one number, choose how hard to push today, and check nothing is broken. Over weeks, the decision log becomes your best teacher.

**Step 1.** Open **Today** in Helix and read the **Profit yesterday** number and the sentence under it.

**Expected result:** You know if yesterday made money and how it compares with your 7-day average.

<!-- do:numbers -->

**Step 2.** Compare your MER for the last 3 days and this month with your target MER. Then pick one plan. **Push:** under target with good profit, so spend more. **Careful push:** close to target, so spend a bit more and watch. **Hold:** at target, so keep spend the same. **Defend:** over target, so cut the weakest ads and fix the cause.

**Expected result:** You have one word for today's stance.

**Step 3.** Do a quick emergency check.

1. On your phone, open your store.
2. Add a product to the cart and go to checkout. You don't need to pay.
3. In Meta Events Manager, check yesterday's purchases are showing.
4. Check your best seller is in stock.

**Expected result:** You have checked that your site, checkout, tracking and stock all work. Or you have found the one thing to fix first.

**Watch out:** Sometimes reported sales drop but Shopify orders look normal. That usually means tracking broke, not that the ads stopped working.

**Step 4.** Write one line in your decision log: what you chose and why. For example: "Hold: MER 31% vs 30% target, waiting on new ads".

**Expected result:** Your log has a dated entry you can look back on next week.

## Lesson 1.7: Sales, costs and profit in plain English
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Most new owners look at sales and feel rich, then wonder where the money went. Sales is not what you keep. Once you can see every layer that comes off a sale, you know which lever to pull.

**Sales** (also called **revenue**) is what customers pay you. In Helix it is shown without GST, VAT or sales tax, because that money belongs to the tax office. From there, each cost takes a layer off:

1. **Product cost (COGS):** making or buying the item.
2. **Freight and duty:** getting it from the factory to you. Product cost plus freight and duty is your **landed cost**. Sales minus landed cost is **gross profit**, and as a share of sales it is **gross margin**.
3. **Payment fees:** what Shopify Payments, PayPal or buy now pay later keep. On Shopify's Basic plan, Australian stores pay 1.7% plus 30 cents on local cards. US stores pay 2.9% plus 30 cents. Overseas cards and other plans differ (see [Shopify: AU pricing](https://www.shopify.com/au/pricing) and [Shopify: US pricing](https://www.shopify.com/pricing)).
4. **Packaging:** mailer, box, tissue and inserts.
5. **Shipping to the customer:** the postage you pay. What is left now is **profit before marketing**, the money one order leaves to pay for ads and bills.
6. **Marketing:** ads and other marketing. What is left now is **contribution**.
7. **Fixed costs:** Shopify and app subscriptions, wages, rent and software, spread over your orders. What is left is **net profit**.

![Where a $60 sale goes](../../public/guides/helix-0-profit-waterfall.svg)

**Worked example: a $60 product made overseas.** It costs $12 to make and $3 to ship in with duty. So the landed cost is $15 and you keep 75% after product cost. Payment fees take about $1.35, packaging $1.50 and postage $9, leaving $33.15 before marketing. Ads cost $15 per order, leaving $18.15. Shopify, apps and other bills of $580 a month over 200 orders is $2.90 an order. Net profit is **$15.25 an order (25%)**, or about $3,050 a month. If ads crept up to $33 an order, every sale would lose money once bills are counted. Sales would look the same.

**Step 1.** Write down one product and its price. Next to it, write a cost for each of the seven layers in this lesson. Guess if you have to. The layers are product, freight and duty, payment fees, packaging, shipping, marketing and fixed costs.

**Expected result:** You have a rough cost for every layer of one order.

**Step 2.** Take the layers away from the price, top to bottom, and circle the biggest one.

**Expected result:** You know your rough profit per order and the single biggest cost eating it.

**Watch out:** Free shipping is not free. If you do not charge for postage, the whole postage cost is a layer you pay on every order.

## Lesson 1.8: Set up your profit dashboard
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Every number Helix shows you later depends on knowing your costs. That includes yesterday's profit, break-even and how much you can spend on ads. Five minutes here makes every other screen honest.

**Step 1.** Enter the costs of one typical order in **Profit setup**.

1. Open **Profit** in Helix.
2. Pick your best seller (or an average order if you sell many products).
3. Type its selling price.
4. Type each cost: product, freight and duty, packaging, shipping to the customer, payment fees.

**Expected result:** The waterfall on the right shows your profit per order and your break-even numbers.

<!-- do:profit -->

**Step 2.** Enter your monthly costs: your Shopify or website plan, every app subscription, and any wages, rent or software. Then enter how many orders you get in a usual month, or your goal if you are just starting.

**Expected result:** You can see your net profit for a month at that number of orders.

**Step 3.** Enter marketing per order (last month's ad spend divided by orders). Tick "Use my monthly costs as fixed costs" and save.

**Expected result:** Profit setup is saved and your dashboard uses your real fixed costs.

**Watch out:** Check your app list in Shopify settings. Forgotten subscriptions of $10 to $50 each often add up to hundreds a month.

## Lesson 1.9: How much to spend on marketing
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Spend too little and the store never grows. Spend too much and every sale loses money. A share of sales gives you a sense check, and your own margin tells you the hard limit.

**Your hard limit comes from your margin, not from a rule.** Take profit before marketing as a share of sales. That is your **break-even MER**: marketing at that share of sales leaves exactly zero. **Break-even ROAS** is 1 divided by it. In the $60 example, profit before marketing is $33.15, so break-even MER is 55% and break-even ROAS is 1.81. Your **break-even cost per purchase** (break-even CPA, as in Lesson 1.5) is the same $33.15. If you pay more than that in ads to win one order, you lose money on it.

**Guides by stage (guides, not rules):**

- **Before your first sales or under about $5,000 a month:** a percentage target means little yet. Set a small test budget you can afford to lose, like $20 to $50 a day for two weeks. Judge each test by its cost per purchase against your break-even cost per purchase.
- **Growing (about $5,000 to $80,000 a month):** total marketing is often about 10% to 30% of sales. It depends on your margin and how far along you are. Newer brands buying growth sit near the top; brands with lots of returning customers sit lower.
- **Scaling (over about $80,000 a month):** many brands sit around 15% to 25% of sales on all marketing. Spending more is fine while your contribution margin stays above zero and returning customers grow. Those customers pay back the cost of winning them.

High margin products can afford a higher share; low margin products cannot. Always leave room between your marketing share and your break-even for fixed costs and profit.

**Step 1.** Open **Profit setup** and read your break-even MER, break-even ROAS and break-even cost per purchase.

**Expected result:** You know the marketing share at which you stop making money.

**Step 2.** Choose your target MER (ads as a share of sales).

1. Pick a number well below your break-even, for example break-even 55%, target 25%. The gap pays your fixed costs and your profit.
2. In Helix, open **Your numbers > Targets and fixed costs**.
3. Type it in **Target MER (%)** and click **Save targets**.
4. No sales yet? Write down a fixed daily test budget instead, for example $20 a day.

**Expected result:** You have a target MER, or a fixed test budget, written down.

**Step 3.** Open **Growth** and compare your marketing share with the guide band for your size.

**Expected result:** You know if you are under, inside or over the usual range. You also know what that means for your margin.

**Watch out:** Count all marketing, not just Meta. Google, TikTok, influencers, codes paid to creators and agency fees all count.

## Example

A candle store had this day:

- Revenue $1,800, 30 orders, 900 sessions, $520 ad spend.
- Product cost 28%. Fulfilment $9 per order. Packaging $1.20 per order. Payment fees 2.6%.
- Fixed costs $9,000 a month.

Working it out step by step:

1. Variable costs = 1,800 x 0.306 (product cost + fees) + 30 x $10.20 (shipping + packing). That is $551 + $306 = $857. That is 48% of sales (VCR 48%).
2. Contribution profit = 1,800 - 857 - 520 = $423.
3. Fixed costs per day = $9,000 ÷ 30.4 = $296. Net profit is about $127, or 7% of sales.
4. MER = 520 ÷ 1,800 = 29%. RPV = $2.00 per visit. AOV = $60.

**What it shows:** MER (ads) looks fine. The real squeeze is VCR at 48%. Shipping costs $9 on a $60 order, which is 15% of every sale. The fix is cheaper shipping and bigger orders, not ads.

## Lesson 1.10: Track your numbers in a spreadsheet
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Writing your numbers down every day is how you see trouble early. A simple sheet does the maths for you, so you only type what happened.

**Step 1.** Download the Helix tracking sheet.

1. Open [the sheet download](/downloads/helix-metrics-tracker.xlsx).
2. Save it somewhere you can find it, like your Desktop.

**Expected result:** You have a file called helix-metrics-tracker.xlsx.

**Step 2.** Open it in the app you like.

- Excel or Numbers: double-click the file.
- Google Sheets: go to sheets.google.com and open a blank sheet.
- Click **File**, then **Import**.
- Click **Upload** and pick the file.

**Expected result:** You see a tab called **Start here** with short instructions.

**Step 3.** Click the **Daily** tab at the bottom.

**Expected result:** You see yellow boxes (you type here) and blue boxes (they work themselves out).

**Step 4.** In the first yellow row, type yesterday's date in **Date**.

**Expected result:** The date shows like 2026-10-12.

**Step 5.** Type yesterday's sales in **Sales**. In Shopify **Analytics**, use **Net sales** for yesterday, not Total sales (that includes tax and shipping).

**Expected result:** A number shows in the Sales box.

**Step 6.** Type yesterday's orders in **Orders** and visits in **Visits**. Both are on the same Shopify **Analytics** page (visits are called **Sessions**).

**Expected result:** Two more numbers are filled in.

**Step 7.** Type what you spent on ads yesterday.

1. Meta: in Ads Manager, find **Amount spent** for yesterday. Type it in **Meta ad spend**.
2. Google: in Google Ads, find **Cost** for yesterday. Type it in **Google ad spend**.

**Expected result:** The blue **Total ad spend** box now shows the two added together.

**Step 8.** Type your costs for yesterday.

1. Type what the products cost you in **Product cost**.
2. Type what you paid to ship in **Shipping cost**.
3. Type card and PayPal fees in **Payment fees**. Not sure of any number? Use the one from your Helix **Profit** page.

**Expected result:** The blue **Profit** box fills in. Green means you made money. Red means you lost money.

**Step 9.** Read the blue boxes.

- **ROAS** (return on ad spend): sales for each $1 of ads.
- **Conversion rate**: how many in 100 visits bought.
- **AOV** (average order value): what one order is worth.
- **MER** (marketing efficiency ratio): ad spend as a share of sales. Lower is better.

**Expected result:** You can say, in one sentence, whether yesterday made money.

**Step 10.** Each Monday, open the **Summary** tab.

**Expected result:** You see your totals for every day you filled in, including total profit.

**Watch out:** Only type in the yellow boxes. If you type over a blue box, download a fresh copy and paste your yellow rows back in.
