# Module 21: Brand and social presence

**Outcome:** your Instagram, TikTok and Facebook profiles look like a brand people can trust, match what your ads promise, and turn curious ad viewers into followers, subscribers and buyers. You have a posting schedule you can keep and a simple way to reach more people for free.

**Related:** [Module 4 Brand, content and community](04-brand-and-content.md), [Module 19 Creators, UGC and video ads](19-creators-ugc-and-video-ads.md), [Module 11 List and audience growth](11-list-and-audience-growth.md), [SOP 16 Customer research and reviews](../sops/16-customer-research-and-reviews.md)

## Lesson 21.1: Why your profile is part of every ad
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Many people who see an ad do not click it; they tap your name to check you out first. If they find three old posts, no reviews and a broken link, they leave. A strong profile makes every ad dollar work harder, for free.

**Step 1.** Open your Instagram, TikTok and Facebook profiles on your phone as a stranger would (logged out or in a private window).

**Expected result:** You see each profile the way an ad viewer does.

**Step 2.** For each profile, check it does three jobs: proves you are real, shows what you sell and gives a clear next step.

**Expected result:** You have a list of which job each profile fails.

**Step 3.** Fix the biggest gap on each profile using the checklist in Lesson 21.2.

**Expected result:** Every profile does all three jobs.

## Lesson 21.2: Profile checklist (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook)
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** A profile is a mini landing page. Fourteen checks on each platform make sure it builds trust and matches what your ads promise.

**Step 1.** Go through every row of the checklist in the table below on Instagram.

**Expected result:** Each Instagram item is ticked or fixed.

**Step 2.** Repeat the checklist on TikTok and Facebook.

**Expected result:** All three profiles pass the checklist.

**Step 3.** Check that the product, offer and look in your current ads also appear on each profile (for example "free shipping this week").

**Expected result:** Ads and profiles tell the same story, so trust does not drop when people tap through.

### Good to know

| Item | What good looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Handle and name | The same handle on every platform. The name field includes a word people search ("Linen clothing for hot summers"), not just the brand. |
| Profile photo | Your logo or brand mark, readable at a tiny size, the same on every platform. |
| Bio, line 1 | What you sell and who it is for. |
| Bio, line 2 | Your proof or what makes you different (rating, number of customers, where it is made, guarantee). |
| Bio, line 3 | A reason to tap now (new release, free shipping amount, gift guide). |
| Link | Goes to a fast mobile page with 3 to 5 clear options: best sellers, new arrivals, offer sign-up, reviews. Add UTM tags so you can track visits. |
| Category and contact | A business account with a category, email and shop buttons turned on. |
| Shop | Product catalog connected so you can tag products in posts (where available). |
| Highlights (Instagram) | 4 to 7 saved story groups with simple matching covers: Reviews, Best sellers, How to use, Shipping and returns, Behind the scenes, Press, FAQ. |
| Pinned posts | 3 pinned: your best-performing product video, your strongest proof (reviews or a customer story), and your brand story or current offer. |
| Recent posts | Something posted in the last 7 days. No gaps of months. |
| Comments | Replied to within a day. Answer questions publicly so others see the answer. |
| Facebook page | Same bio and link, a current cover image, reviews managed, and the page connected to your ad account. |
| TikTok | Bio link turned on (not every account can yet), playlists for series, pinned videos. |

## Lesson 21.3: What to post: content pillars for social
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Content pillars are the topics you always post about. Four pillars cover social, and a rough mix stops your feed from feeling like one long ad. They build on [Lesson 4.4](04-brand-and-content.md#lesson-44-content-pillars-by-channel).

**Step 1.** Set your four pillars: proof (reviews, customer photos, press, numbers), education (how to use and choose, myths, care), story (founder, behind the scenes, values) and product and offer (launches, restocks, bundles, sales).

**Expected result:** You have four named pillars.

**Step 2.** Plan next month at roughly 40% education and proof, 30% story and community, 30% product and offer.

**Expected result:** Your plan follows the mix.

**Step 3.** Fill gaps with ideas that never run out: reviews and comments, support questions ("You asked, we answered"), calm answers to negative comments, and a recurring series.

**Expected result:** You have more ideas than posting slots.

## Lesson 21.4: Posting cadence you can keep
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Cadence means how often you post. Being consistent beats posting a lot, and batching makes it easy to keep up for 12 weeks.

**Step 1.** Pick the cadence in the table below that matches your stage.

**Expected result:** You have a weekly posting target per platform.

**Step 2.** Batch your work: film once a week, then schedule the whole week in Meta Business Suite or a scheduler.

**Expected result:** A week of posts is scheduled in one sitting.

**Step 3.** Make every post good enough to run as an ad later, with most of the value in the first quarter of the video and key points said twice.

**Expected result:** Your best free posts can become ads, partnership ads or Spark ads.

### Good to know

| Stage | Instagram | TikTok | Facebook | Stories |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Just starting | 3 posts a week | 3 to 5 a week | Share your Instagram posts | Most days |
| Growing | 4 to 5 a week (mostly Reels) | 1 a day | 3 a week | Every day |
| Established | Every day | 1 to 3 a day | Every day | Every day, plus a broadcast channel |

## Lesson 21.5: Growing organic reach
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Organic reach means people who see your posts without you paying. Seven tactics grow it, from trial reels to replying fast.

**Step 1.** Use trial reels or test posts to show a reel to non-followers first, check after 24 hours and share winners with everyone.

**Expected result:** You test hooks without cluttering your feed.

**Step 2.** Say your keywords out loud and put them in on-screen text and captions ("linen shirt for hot weather").

**Expected result:** Your posts show up in social search.

**Step 3.** Remake the first three seconds of your best free posts with new hooks ([Lesson 19.8](19-creators-ugc-and-video-ads.md#lesson-198-hooks-that-win-the-first-three-seconds)) and post again.

**Expected result:** Proven posts get a second life.

**Step 4.** Make collab posts with creators and partner brands, and reply to every comment in the first hour.

**Expected result:** Your content reaches partners' audiences and gets early engagement.

**Step 5.** Start a broadcast channel or close group for your keenest fans, and answer questions helpfully in forums where customers ask for recommendations (say who you are, never spam).

**Expected result:** Fans get early access and new customers find you off-platform.

## Lesson 21.6: Social proof and reviews on social
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Social proof on social media turns curious visitors into buyers. Reviews, customer posts and kind replies to complaints all count.

**Step 1.** Turn your best reviews into an Instagram highlight, a carousel and a video where you read them out.

**Expected result:** Reviews are visible on your profile.

**Step 2.** Ask happy customers to tag you, then reshare their posts to Stories with permission and save them to a highlight.

**Expected result:** Customer posts fill a highlight.

**Step 3.** Keep shop ratings healthy where you sell through social shops, and reply to bad reviews publicly and kindly before fixing the problem privately.

**Expected result:** Future customers see a calm, helpful reply.

## Lesson 21.7: Community, not just audience
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** People buy from brands they feel part of. Five habits turn followers into a community.

**Step 1.** Run a poll letting customers help decide something (a colour, name or flavour).

**Expected result:** Customers have a say in a decision.

**Step 2.** Feature customers as the faces of a collection.

**Expected result:** Real customers appear in your marketing.

**Step 3.** Run a small event, meetup or live session, and reward your biggest fans with early access, gifts or a VIP group.

**Expected result:** Your keenest fans feel looked after.

<!-- do:flow-vip -->

**Step 4.** Share feedback you acted on ("You asked for bigger sizes. Here they are.").

**Expected result:** Customers see their voice changes things.

## Lesson 21.8: Social shops
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Instagram, Facebook and TikTok shops let people buy without leaving the app. A few checks before you switch one on prevent bad scores and lost orders. See also Lessons 12.6 and 12.10.

**Step 1.** Connect a clean catalog with one price per product and correct stock levels.

**Expected result:** The shop catalog matches your store.

**Step 2.** State shipping times and returns clearly in the shop settings.

**Expected result:** Buyers know what to expect, protecting your shop score.

**Step 3.** Check which store features do not work in the in-app checkout (for example thank-you page offers), check seller fees, and confirm in-app orders flow into your store and email platform.

**Expected result:** In-app orders are profitable and land in your normal systems.

## Lesson 21.9: The social presence audit (what Helix checks)
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Helix checks your social profiles in its regular audit, using public information plus account insights once you connect. Knowing the checks lets you fix things before the audit flags them.

**Step 1.** Read the checks in the table below.

**Expected result:** You know what Helix looks at.

**Step 2.** Open **What to fix** in Helix to see any social findings.

**Expected result:** You see flagged items with evidence.

<!-- do:insights -->

**Step 3.** Fix one flagged item this week.

**Expected result:** Your next audit shows one less issue.

### Good to know

| Check | Helix flags it when |
| --- | --- |
| Bio says what you sell | No product or category word in the name or bio |
| Link works on mobile | The link is missing, broken or slow, or goes to a general home page with no UTM tags |
| Recent activity | No post in the last 14 days |
| How often you post | Fewer than 2 posts a week over the last 4 weeks |
| Highlights | Fewer than 3, or no reviews or shipping highlight |
| Pinned posts | None pinned, or pinned posts older than 6 months |
| Social proof | No review or customer content in the last 10 posts |
| Matches your ads | Products or offers in your live ads are not visible on the profile |
| Comment care | Questions left unanswered for more than 48 hours |
| Same name everywhere | Different handles or names across platforms |
| Shop | Catalog connected but no products tagged in posts, or the shop rating is dropping |

## Self-check

1. What three jobs does a profile do?
2. What should your three pinned posts be?
3. Why say your keywords out loud in videos?
4. Your ad promotes a bundle that is nowhere on your profile. Why does it matter?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Prove you are real, show what you sell, give the next step.
2. Your best-performing product video, your strongest proof, and your brand story or current offer.
3. Platforms read speech, captions and on-screen text for social search and recommendations.
4. Many people check your profile before buying. A mismatch lowers trust and sales.
