# Module 11: TikTok, Pinterest, SEO and AI search

**Outcome:** you know when and how to add channels beyond Meta and Google. You also know how to be found in Google search and in AI helpers.

**Related SOP:** [10 TikTok and emerging channels](../sops/10-tiktok-and-emerging-channels.md)

## Lesson 11.1: TikTok readiness
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** TikTok can bring cheap attention. But only once you have videos that work there and tracking in place. Getting ready first stops you paying to learn what free posts could teach you. Only start TikTok ads once Meta and Google are profitable.

**Step 1.** Post free TikTok videos 3 to 5 times a week for a month. Learn what works for you.

**Expected result:** You know which video styles get views and comments.

**Step 2.** Save the 10 to 15 best videos that are good enough to run as ads.

**Expected result:** You have a bank of ad-ready videos.

**Step 3.** Make a TikTok Business Center and an ad account. Install the TikTok pixel through the TikTok app in Shopify (it reports views, carts and sales).

**Expected result:** TikTok Events Manager shows Purchase events coming in. Older setups may call it Complete payment (see [TikTok: updated standard events](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-adopt-tiktoks-updated-standard-events)).

**Step 4.** Make a simple view in TikTok Ads Manager.

1. Click **Custom columns**.
2. Tick: spend, CPA (ad cost for one sale), add to cart and cost per add to cart. Also tick CTR and CPC (cost per click). Also tick 2-second and 6-second views, and 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% watched.
3. Save it.

**Expected result:** One saved view shows every number you need.

## Lesson 11.2: Structures by spend
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** TikTok structures depend on spend, just like Meta. Too many campaigns on a small budget starves each one.

**Step 1.** Find your daily spend band in the table below and set up the campaigns listed for it.

**Expected result:** Your TikTok account matches your budget.

**Step 2.** Choose campaign budget for steady running (TikTok shares money between ad groups). Choose ad group budget when you need control over each test.

**Expected result:** Each campaign uses the budget type that suits its job.

### Good to know

| Daily spend | Setup |
| --- | --- |
| About $100 | One purchase campaign, plus a small campaign aimed at an earlier step (view content or add to cart). The earlier step gives TikTok more data to learn from. |
| About $200 | Add a catalog ads campaign that shows products from your store's catalog. |
| About $300 or more | Add a traffic campaign to a landing page, or a video-view campaign, for cheap reach. Test each creative style separately. |

## Lesson 11.3: Spark ads and creators
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Spark ads run normal TikTok posts as ads, yours or a creator's. They keep the post's likes and comments. Creator videos are the main fuel for TikTok ads (Module 6).

**Step 1.** Ask the creator for an authorisation code for each post. On the post, they tap **...**, then **Ad settings**, turn on ad authorisation and generate a code.

**Expected result:** You have a code for each post you want to run. Each code works for one post only.

**Step 2.** Run the creator's post as an ad.

1. In TikTok Ads Manager, create an ad.
2. Choose to use a TikTok post (a Spark ad).
3. Paste the code the creator sent you.

**Expected result:** The creator's post runs as an ad with its likes and comments.

## Lesson 11.4: Optimising TikTok
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** TikTok ads can tire within days, so they need closer watching than Meta. Early signs warn you before cost per sale gets worse.

**Step 1.** Check TikTok every day or two. Watch for early warning signs. Cost per click goes up, cost per add to cart goes up, or fewer people watch the videos.

**Expected result:** You spot tired ads before CPA rises.

**Step 2.** Turn off ads that eat budget with a poor cost per sale.

**Expected result:** Spend moves to working ads.

**Step 3.** Did cost per sale go up after you turned off an earlier-step campaign (like add to cart)? Turn it back on.

**Expected result:** TikTok gets the data it needs again.

**Step 4.** When things work, grow one move at a time.

1. Make new hooks for your best ad.
2. Try catalog ads.
3. Offer bundles to raise order size.
4. Add a new campaign only after these.

**Expected result:** Growth comes from new creative and campaigns, not just more budget.

**Step 5.** Start TikTok at about 10% of your total ad budget (a rule of thumb). Judge it by your total MER on the Dashboard (total ad spend as a share of total sales).

**Expected result:** You see TikTok's effect on the whole store.

## Lesson 11.5: TikTok search
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Many people use TikTok like a search engine, typing things like "best gift for new dad" or "how to stop frizzy hair". Videos that clearly answer a search can keep being found for months, long after a normal post stops getting views.

**Step 1.** Type your product type into the TikTok search bar and write down the suggestions that appear as you type. Also note the "Others searched for" phrases shown on related videos, when TikTok shows them.

**Expected result:** A list of about 10 real search phrases, in your buyers' own words.

**Step 2.** Pick one phrase per video. Say it in the first sentence of the voice-over.

**Expected result:** TikTok can hear what the video is about from the first line.

**Step 3.** Put the same phrase in the on-screen text and in the caption, as a normal sentence. Add 3 to 5 hashtags that match it.

**Expected result:** The phrase appears in sound, text and caption.

**Step 4.** Answer the question properly, and show your product doing the job. Keep it short and get to the answer fast.

**Expected result:** Someone who searched that phrase gets a real answer, not just an ad.

**Step 5.** After a week, open the video's analytics and look at where views came from. Search is listed as its own traffic source.

**Expected result:** You know which phrases bring search views. Make more videos for the phrases that work.

## Lesson 11.6: TikTok Shop
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** TikTok Shop lets people buy without leaving TikTok. Its ads run as GMV Max campaigns, which aim for total sales (GMV). It also has affiliate creators and live shopping. Commission and fees can eat margin, so check the maths first.

**Watch out:** TikTok Shop runs in the US, UK, and parts of Europe, Asia and Latin America. It is not yet in Australia (October 2026). Since July 2025, GMV Max is the only TikTok Shop ad type (see [TikTok: GMV Max for Shop ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/gmv-max-migration-tiktok-shop-ads)).

**Step 1.** Work out your margin per order after TikTok Shop's commission and fees.

**Expected result:** You know your profit per order on TikTok Shop.

**Step 2.** If margin holds up, list your top 5 products and invite affiliate creators.

**Expected result:** Creators can sell your products from their videos.

## Lesson 11.7: Pinterest
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Pinterest pins keep getting found for months, much longer than social posts. People plan what to buy there well ahead of time. It rewards steady posting and early seasonal planning.

**Step 1.** Add fresh pins every week with search words in titles and descriptions, on boards by theme and season.

**Expected result:** Your pins show up in Pinterest search.

**Step 2.** Connect your catalog to run shopping ads, starting with the **Consideration** objective.

**Expected result:** Pinterest ads show your products with prices. Pinterest suggests moving to the **Sales** objective at about 50 conversions a week (see [Pinterest: Performance+](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/performance-plus)).

**Step 3.** Plan seasonal ads 2 to 3 months early.

**Expected result:** Your seasonal pins are live while people plan.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 4.** Measure over a longer time, and count sales after views and saves as well as clicks. Compare with your total MER (total ad spend as a share of total sales).

**Expected result:** You judge Pinterest fairly for its slower decisions.

## Lesson 11.8: SEO basics
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Search brings people who are already looking for what you sell, and you do not pay per click. Most of it comes down to a few basics done once and checked each month: the right page for each search, clear titles, tidy links and a sitemap Google can read. Lesson 11.9 then builds on this with keyword research, helpful articles and mentions on other sites.

**Step 1.** Match each kind of search to the right kind of page. A search for a type of product ("linen shirt men") wants a collection page. A search for one exact item wants that product page. A question ("how to wash linen") wants a helpful article or an FAQ.

**Expected result:** You have a short list of your main searches, each paired with one page on your store that answers it.

**Step 2.** Find the words people really use, for free.

1. In Google Search Console, open **Performance** and read the **Queries** list: the searches that already show your store (see [Search Console: Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553)).
2. Type your main product words into Google and note the suggestions that drop down as you type.
3. Read the **People also ask** questions on the results page.

**Expected result:** You have 5 to 10 real search phrases for each main collection and product.

**Watch out:** Use the phrases where they read naturally. Repeating a word over and over does not help and reads badly (see [Google: SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)).

**Step 3.** Write a clear title and description for each key page in its **Search engine listing** box in Shopify.

**Expected result:** Your home page, main collections and best sellers each have a unique title under about 60 characters, with the main phrase near the start, and a description of about 150 to 160 characters that says what the page offers (see [Google: title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) and [Google: snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)).

**Step 4.** In the same **Search engine listing** box, set the URL handle to a few plain words, like /products/linen-shirt-sand.

**Expected result:** Every handle is short and readable (see [Google: URL structure](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/url-structure)).

**Watch out:** When you rename or delete a page, send the old address to the best new one, or you lose the visits it has earned. Tick **Create a URL redirect** when you change a handle, or add one in **Content > Menus > View URL redirects** (see [Shopify: URL redirects](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/menus-and-links/url-redirect)).

**Step 5.** Describe your images. Click each product image in Shopify, then **Edit alt text**, and write what the photo shows in a few words, like "sand linen shirt, front view".

**Expected result:** Every product photo has alt text, which helps image search and people using screen readers (see [Google: images in search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images)).

**Step 6.** Link your own pages together. Link from each collection to its best products, from each product to its collection and one related product, and from articles to the products they mention. Use words that say where the link goes, not "click here".

**Expected result:** Every important page can be reached in a few clicks from the home page (see [Google: crawlable links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable)).

**Step 7.** Send your sitemap to Google. Shopify makes one for you at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. In Search Console, open **Sitemaps**, type sitemap.xml and click **Submit**.

**Expected result:** The Sitemaps report shows "Success" (see [Search Console: Sitemaps report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7451001)).

**Step 8.** Fix broken links and missing pages. In Search Console, open **Pages** and look for pages marked "Not found (404)". Redirect each one to the closest live page, or fix the link that points to it.

**Expected result:** No important page shows "Not found" (see [Search Console: Page indexing report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7440203)).

**Step 9.** Add a short FAQ to your best product pages: the 3 to 5 questions customers ask before buying (size, care, delivery, returns), answered in plain words.

**Expected result:** Each best seller answers its top buying questions on the page.

**Step 10.** Check your product details show in Google. Most Shopify themes add structured data (code that tells Google the price, stock and rating) for you. Paste a product page address into Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results). If it finds no product details, ask your theme's support or a developer to add them; do not paste code you do not understand.

**Expected result:** The test finds valid product details on your product pages (see [Google: product structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product)).

**Step 11.** Write for people, which also helps AI search. Answer real questions clearly, keep facts like prices, sizes and delivery times the same everywhere, and say who made the page. Google's AI features use the same basics as normal search, so there is no special trick (see [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Lesson 11.10 covers AI shopping helpers.

**Expected result:** Your key pages answer real questions in plain words, with facts that match across your store.

**Step 12.** Keep a 20-minute monthly routine.

1. Search Console **Performance**: note clicks and the top 10 searches, and find pages shown often but rarely clicked. Rewrite their titles and descriptions.
2. **Pages** report: fix any new "Not found" pages with a redirect.
3. Add titles, descriptions and alt text to any new products and collections.
4. Add or improve one FAQ or helpful article.

**Expected result:** A short note each month with clicks, top searches and the fixes you made.

## Lesson 11.9: SEO step by step
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** SEO (search engine optimisation) means getting found in Google without paying for ads. It takes months to build but keeps paying. Five steps cover most of it.

**Step 1.** Find the words people search when they want to buy.

1. List buying searches for each category, like "buy linen shirt" or "best linen shirt".
2. In a keyword tool, look up how many people search each one. Also check how hard it is to rank.
3. Keep about 10 per category.

**Expected result:** Each category has a list of target keywords.

**Step 2.** Fix your pages. Each page needs one main heading (H1), clear subheadings (H2, H3) and a page title. It also needs a meta description, the summary under your link in Google (about 150 to 160 characters). Build collection pages around a keyword, link between your own pages, and add alt text to images.

**Expected result:** Every key page has a keyword-led title, meta description and one clear H1. Google sets no meta description limit, but longer ones get cut off (see [Google: control your snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)).

**Step 3.** Write helpful content that matches your keywords: buying guides, comparisons and how-tos.

**Expected result:** You publish at least one helpful article a month.

**Step 4.** Check the technical side in Google Search Console. You want fast phone pages, structured data (code that tells Google price, rating and stock) and a sitemap sent in. Also no broken links and no copy pages.

**Expected result:** Search Console shows your sitemap as "Success" and no page errors you can fix.

**Step 5.** Get mentioned on other sites through press, partnerships and brand mentions.

**Expected result:** Other sites link to you.

## Lesson 11.10: AI search and agentic commerce
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** More shoppers now ask AI helpers (AI chat assistants and AI search) what to buy. Some helpers can even shop for them. Clear facts, reviews and clean product data decide whether they recommend you.

**Step 1.** Make your brand facts the same everywhere (site, marketplaces, social bios) and grow your reviews.

**Expected result:** AI helpers find the same facts everywhere and plenty of proof.

**Step 2.** Check your robots.txt lets trusted AI tools read your site. Keep product data clean, and add FAQ sections and clear shipping and returns pages.

**Expected result:** AI tools can read and quote your store accurately.

**Step 3.** Keep your product feed connected so your store can show up inside AI shopping assistants. Shopify lists eligible stores in AI chat apps by default under **Sales channels > Agentic** (see [Shopify: agentic storefronts](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/agentic-storefronts)).

**Expected result:** Your products are eligible for AI shopping results.

**Step 4.** Get talked about on forums, review sites and videos. Track visits from AI helpers in Google Analytics (referral sources).

**Expected result:** You know how much traffic AI assistants send.

## Lesson 11.11: Instagram shopping
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Instagram shopping lets people tap a product in a post. It takes them straight to it on your store. Since September 2025, Facebook and Instagram shops send buyers to your own website to check out ([Meta help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1314349509894768)). It is free once your catalog is connected.

1. In Shopify, add the **Facebook & Instagram** sales channel from the Shopify App Store.
2. Click **Connect account** and connect your Facebook Page, business portfolio, Instagram account and catalog.
3. Accept the terms and click **Finish setup** (see [Shopify: set up Facebook and Instagram](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/facebook-instagram-by-meta/setup)).

**Expected result:** Meta reviews your shop before it goes live. This can take a few days or longer.

**Step 2.** Turn on catalog sync from your store so prices and stock stay current.

**Expected result:** Price and stock changes in Shopify flow to your Meta catalog by themselves.

**Step 3.** Tag products in your posts and Reels.

**Expected result:** Shoppers can tap to see and buy products.

## Lesson 11.12: Start a YouTube channel that keeps selling
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** A post on most apps fades in a day. A good YouTube video can be found in search for years. People who watch a full "how to" video already trust you before they visit your store.

**Step 1.** List 10 questions your customers ask before they buy. Use reviews, emails and the search box in YouTube itself (type a word and read the suggestions).

**Expected result:** 10 questions real people search for, like "how to choose a running backpack".

**Step 2.** Pick the 3 questions that link best to your products. Each will be one video.

**Expected result:** A plan for your first 3 videos.

**Step 3.** Write each title as the question or the result people want. Keep it under about 60 characters (a rule of thumb), so it is less likely to be cut off.

**Expected result:** 3 clear titles, like "How to Pick a Running Backpack (3 Easy Checks)".

**Step 4.** Film each video on your phone. Answer the question in the first 30 seconds, then show more detail. Show your product as one helpful example, not a hard sell.

**Expected result:** 3 videos of 4 to 10 minutes that teach something useful.

**Step 5.** Make a thumbnail for each video: one face or product, big and bright, with 3 words or fewer.

**Expected result:** Thumbnails you can read on a small phone screen.

**Step 6.** In the description, put a link to the product page with a tracking tag (UTM, a label that tells your reports where a visit came from).

**Expected result:** You can see sales from YouTube in your store reports.

**Step 7.** Post one video every 2 weeks for 3 months. In **YouTube Studio > Analytics**, check which titles get the most clicks and the longest watching time.

**Expected result:** You know which topics work, and you make more like them.

**Watch out:** Results are slow. Most channels take months to grow. Judge it after 3 months, not 3 days.

## Lesson 11.13: Organic Pinterest: boards and keywords
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Pinterest works like a search engine for ideas. A good pin can bring visitors for months without paying for ads. The key is using the words people type, and keeping your boards tidy so Pinterest knows what you sell.

**Step 1.** Change your Pinterest account to a free business account and claim your website in **Settings > Claimed accounts**.

**Expected result:** Your pins show your logo and Pinterest gives you visitor numbers.

**Step 2.** Type your main product into the Pinterest search bar and write down the suggested words that appear under it.

**Expected result:** A list of 15 to 20 words and phrases real shoppers search for.

**Step 3.** Create 5 to 10 boards, each named with one of those search phrases, like "Linen summer dresses" rather than "My faves".

**Expected result:** Every board has a clear, searchable name and a one-line description that uses the same words.

**Step 4.** For each product, make 2 to 3 tall pins (2:3, like 1000 x 1500 pixels) with a different photo or text on each.

**Expected result:** Each product has a few fresh pins, not one copy pinned many times.

**Step 5.** Write each pin title and description with a search phrase in plain words, and link the pin to the exact product page.

**Expected result:** Clicks land on the product, not your home page.

**Step 6.** Post a few fresh pins every week, and post seasonal pins 6 to 8 weeks before the season.

**Expected result:** Your pins are already being found when people start planning.

**Step 7.** Once a month, open **Analytics** and sort your pins by outbound clicks.

**Expected result:** You know your top 5 pins, so your next pins can copy their style.

## Lesson 11.14: TikTok 101 (start here if you have never used TikTok for selling)
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** TikTok is a scrolling app built on short, real-looking videos. You can sell there in four ways. Knowing them stops you paying for ads before you know what people like.

**Step 1.** Learn the four ways. **Organic** means free videos you post. **TikTok Shop** lets people buy inside the app ([TikTok: sell on TikTok Shop](https://www.tiktok.com/business/en-US/tiktok-shop-seller)). **Ads Manager ads** are paid videos. **Spark Ads** pay to push a real post, yours or a creator's ([TikTok: about Spark Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/spark-ads)).

**Expected result:** You can say which of the four are free and which are paid.

<details><summary>More detail: the four ways side by side</summary>

| Way | Free or paid | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Organic | Free | Learning what people like |
| TikTok Shop | Free to list, fees on each sale | Buying without leaving the app |
| Ads Manager ad | Paid | Sending people to your store |
| Spark Ad | Paid | Pushing a post that already works. Likes and follows stay on the post |
</details>

**Step 2.** Learn the content style. Good TikToks look like normal posts, not ads. Film on a phone in 9:16. Show the product in the first 2 seconds. Use a person talking, a demo or a before-and-after.

**Expected result:** You know a simple phone video often beats a polished ad here.

**Step 3.** Start with free posts. Post 3 to 5 videos a week for a month before you pay for ads.

**Expected result:** You know which 2 or 3 videos got the most views and comments.

**Step 4.** Before you start ads, tick off this list. You have a TikTok Ads Manager account ([TikTok: set up an Ads Manager account](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/create-tiktok-ads-manager-account)). Your business details are added. The TikTok pixel is connected through the TikTok app in Shopify.

**Expected result:** Every box is ticked. Account reviews usually take under 24 hours.

<details><summary>More detail: account and payment</summary>

- Sign up with email or phone, then add your country, industry, legal business name, time zone and currency.
- Use your exact legal business name. A mismatch can stop the account being approved.
- Add billing details and a payment method, then click **Go to Ads Manager**.
</details>

**Step 5.** In TikTok Ads Manager, click **Create** with the **Sales** goal. Call it something like "2026-11 Sales First".

**Expected result:** A new Sales campaign with a clear name is open. Menus change often, so look for the same words nearby.

**Step 6.** Set a daily budget you are happy to lose while learning, like $20 to $30 a day. Leave the audience on TikTok's suggestion.

**Expected result:** The ad group shows a small daily budget and a broad audience.

**Step 7.** Pick your best free video as a **Spark Ad**. Add your product page link and a short call to action like **Shop now**.

**Expected result:** The preview shows your real post with a button to your store.

**Step 8.** Click **Submit**. Then leave it alone for 3 days.

**Expected result:** The ad is approved and spending. You have not changed anything yet.

<details><summary>More detail: after you launch</summary>

- **First 24 to 72 hours:** check it is approved and spending, and that the pixel records add-to-carts.
- **Do not** change the budget or audience for 3 days.
- **Common first-time mistakes:** making ads that look like TV ads, skipping free posts, a missing pixel, a too-narrow audience and stopping after one day.
</details>

**Step 9.** Stuck? Search YouTube for "TikTok Ads Manager tutorial for beginners 2026" or "how to run Spark Ads 2026". Pick videos from the last 6 months, because menus change.

**Expected result:** You find a recent video that matches your screen.

**Watch out:** Take a screenshot of where you are stuck. Ask the Helix coach or another AI assistant for the next click. **Next:** TikTok readiness and setup in Lesson 11.1.

## Lesson 11.15: Other channels 101 (Pinterest, SEO, YouTube, email, SMS and marketplaces)
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Meta, Google and TikTok are not the only places to be seen. Some channels are free but slow. Some you own, so no app can take them away. This lesson explains each one simply and says when to add it.

**Step 1.** Learn Pinterest. People use it like a search engine for ideas. You save **Pins** (pictures with a link) to **boards** (themed folders). Clear titles with the words people search help them get found ([Pinterest: create a board](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/create-a-board)).

**Expected result:** You know Pinterest suits products people plan for, like home, fashion, gifts and food.

**Step 2.** Learn SEO, which means showing up in Google for free. Google finds pages through links. Clear page titles, a short summary and good photos with alt text help it understand you ([Google: SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)).

**Expected result:** You know SEO is free but slow. Google says changes can take a few weeks to months to show.

**Step 3.** Learn YouTube. It is the place for longer how-to, review and demo videos. Shorts are its 9:16 short videos. Paid YouTube ads run through Google Ads, for example Demand Gen (see Google 101, Lesson 10.14).

**Expected result:** You know YouTube suits products that need showing or explaining.

**Step 4.** Learn email and SMS. These are **owned** channels. Once someone signs up, you can reach them again without paying for each view.

**Expected result:** You know to start collecting emails from day one, before anything else on this list.

**Step 5.** Learn marketplaces. Amazon and Etsy already have shoppers, but they charge fees and own the customer. Etsy suits handmade, vintage and craft items. Amazon suits everyday products people search by name.

**Expected result:** You know a marketplace can add sales, but the buyer belongs to the marketplace, not you.

**Step 6.** Pick when to add each one. Use the list in **More detail** below. Add one new channel at a time.

**Expected result:** You have one next channel written down, and a date to start it.

Pinterest recommends at least 25 keywords per ad group ([Pinterest: keyword targeting](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/keyword-targeting)).
</details>

**Step 7.** Stuck? Search YouTube for "Pinterest for business tutorial 2026", "Shopify SEO for beginners 2026" or "Pinterest Ads Manager tutorial 2026". Pick videos from the last 6 months, because menus change.

**Expected result:** You find a recent video that matches your screen.
