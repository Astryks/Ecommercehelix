# Module 24: Payments, fraud and chargebacks

**Outcome:** you know what each way of paying really costs you, and you keep payouts flowing during big sales. You stop card-testing bots and risky orders before they ship. You answer chargebacks with the right proof on time.

**Related:** [Module 22 Shopify store setup](22-shopify-store-setup.md), [Module 23 Fulfilment, shipping and returns](23-fulfilment-shipping-returns.md), [Module 25 Account safety, ad accounts and agencies](25-account-safety-agencies.md), [Module 27 Accounting, bookkeeping and tax time](27-accounting-bookkeeping-tax-time.md)

> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Grow (keep more of every sale, then scale). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** What each way to pay costs you. And how to keep your money safe from holds, fraud and disputes.
>
> **Why it matters:** A payout hold or a wave of chargebacks can leave you unable to pay suppliers. That can happen even in a record month.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Work out your real payment fee rate and put it in Helix.
> 2. Check the fraud risk on every order before you ship, and refund anything suspicious.
> 3. Answer every chargeback before the deadline with the right evidence.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Chargeback:** When a customer asks their bank to reverse a card payment. The money is taken back, and you can send evidence to dispute it.
> - **Card testing:** When fraudsters use your checkout to try many stolen card numbers with small orders.
> - **Payout:** The money your payment provider sends to your bank. It takes out its fees, refunds and any held amounts first.
> - **Payment reserve:** Part of your sales that a payment provider holds back for a while. It covers refunds or chargebacks.
> - **3D Secure:** An extra check where the shopper's bank asks them to confirm a card payment. It often happens in their banking app.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 24.1: Your payment mix and what it really costs
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Each way to pay has its own fee, payout speed and risk. The fee on the price page is rarely the whole cost.

**Step 1.** Download last month's payout reports from each provider (Shopify Payments, PayPal, buy now pay later).

**Expected result:** You have one report per provider.

**Step 2.** For each provider, divide total fees by the sales it processed.

**Expected result:** You have a real fee rate per provider.

**Step 3.** Add hidden costs: currency conversion on withdrawal, fixed per-order fees on small orders and fees kept on refunds.

**Expected result:** Your fee rates include everything.

**Step 4.** Enter your blended fee rate (all fees divided by all sales) in Helix as payment fees.

**Expected result:** Helix profit uses your real fees.

<!-- do:numbers -->

**Step 5.** Keep the ways to pay that customers use. Drop any under about 2% of orders that costs much more than cards.

**Expected result:** Your payment list is lean.

**Watch out:** Outside the US: buy now pay later often lifts order value. Its fee is often several times a card fee. US: Shop Pay Installments has its own fee, set per store. Compare it with your margin before promoting it.

**Step 6.** Check each provider's fee page once a year.

**Expected result:** Fee rises never surprise you.

## Lesson 24.2: Holds, reserves and frozen payouts
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A payment provider can hold part of your money if it sees risk. For example: a new account, a sudden jump in sales, slow delivery or many disputes. A hold in peak season can leave you unable to pay suppliers.

**Step 1.** Read each provider's rules on holds and reserves once (see [Shopify: reserves](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/payouts/reserves)). A reserve is money held back for a while in case of refunds.

**Expected result:** You know what triggers a hold.

**Step 2.** Before a big sale or launch, tell your providers the dates and expected sales.

**Expected result:** An expected spike is less likely to trigger a hold.

**Step 3.** Add tracking to every order within a day.

**Expected result:** Slow fulfilment is a known risk signal, so fast tracking makes holds less likely.

**Step 4.** Show honest delivery times and state pre-order ship dates clearly.

**Expected result:** Customers know when to expect orders.

**Step 5.** Keep at least one month of costs in cash and a second payment method ready to switch on.

**Expected result:** A hold cannot stop the business.

**Step 6.** If money is held, give the provider everything in one go.

1. Ask exactly what they need, for example tracking, invoices or ID.
2. Send it all in one reply.
3. Add a follow-up date to your Helix calendar.

**Expected result:** The hold is resolved as fast as possible.

**Watch out:** Keep business details, bank account and ID up to date with every provider. Old details often cause freezes.

## Lesson 24.3: Chargebacks: how they work and how to answer
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A chargeback is when a customer asks their bank to reverse a card payment. The money and a chargeback fee are taken straight away. On Shopify Payments, the fee comes back if you win. You have a short time to prove the sale was fine.

**Step 1.** Open the chargeback in Shopify and note the deadline to reply. It is usually 7 to 21 days on Shopify Payments (see [Shopify: responding to chargebacks](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks/chargeback-process)).

**Expected result:** You know your deadline. Customers usually have about 120 days to dispute, often counted from the expected delivery date.

**Step 2.** Read the reason: "not received", "not as described" or "fraudulent".

**Expected result:** You know which evidence you need.

**Step 3.** For "not received", gather tracking showing delivery to the address given, plus any messages confirming details.

**Expected result:** You have proof of delivery.

**Step 4.** For "not as described", gather your product page at the time and photos. Add your returns policy and messages offering a fix.

**Expected result:** You can show the product matched the listing.

**Step 5.** For "fraudulent", prove the real cardholder ordered. Show matching billing and shipping address, past orders and opened tracking emails.

**Expected result:** You have the strongest proof available. These are often the hardest to win.

**Step 6.** Write a short, factual response with the strongest proof first and submit it.

**Expected result:** The response is submitted before the deadline.

**Step 7.** Contact the customer politely, because some disputes are confusion they can withdraw.

**Expected result:** Some disputes are withdrawn.

**Step 8.** Track your chargeback rate (disputes divided by orders) monthly (see [Shopify: resolving a chargeback](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks/resolve-chargeback)). Disputes count even when you win.

**Expected result:** You catch a rising rate before card networks penalise you. Mastercard flags merchants from 1.5% and 100 disputes a month. Visa's line is 1.5% and 1,500 disputes plus fraud reports in most regions (see [Shopify: monitoring programs](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks/monitoring-programs)). Your provider may act sooner, so a common rule of thumb is to stay well under 1%.

**Watch out:** For high-value orders, use signature on delivery. It is the strongest proof you can buy.

## Lesson 24.4: Stopping fraud and card testing
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Card testing is when crooks use your checkout to try stolen card numbers. They usually place many tiny orders on your cheapest product. Each one can turn into a chargeback and fees.

**Step 1.** Learn the signs. Look for bursts of small orders, the same cheap item, or odd names or emails. Also many failed payments, or different addresses on one card.

**Expected result:** You can spot card testing.

**Step 2.** Use Shopify's fraud analysis and the card testing protection built into Shopify Payments (see [Shopify: preventing fraud](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/fraud-prevention/preventing-fraud)). Checkout [bot protection](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/bot-protection) is for Shopify Plus flash sales only.

**Expected result:** You know what Shopify already blocks.

**Step 3.** Read the fraud risk level on every order before shipping. Hold high-risk orders while you contact the customer.

**Expected result:** No high-risk order ships unchecked.

**Step 4.** Cancel and refund any order that looks fraudulent before shipping.

**Expected result:** You pay a refund instead of a chargeback.

**Step 5.** Use 3D Secure where your provider allows it. Shopify Payments applies it only when the card's bank asks.

**Expected result:** Risky payments get an extra check where your provider supports it. On Stripe, Radar rules can request it for risky payments.

**Step 6.** If testing keeps happening, raise the minimum order or hide your cheapest product for a while.

**Expected result:** The attacks stop.

**Watch out:** Be careful with gift cards and very large first orders going to parcel forwarding addresses.

## Lesson 24.5: Refunds, partial refunds and goodwill
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A fast, fair refund is cheaper than a dispute. A clear rule saves you deciding each case from scratch.

**Step 1.** Write a one-page refund rule. Say when you refund in full, when you offer part refunds or credit, and who says yes.

**Expected result:** Your team can handle any refund the way you would.

**Step 2.** For small problems, offer a fix first. Give a part refund or store credit if the customer keeps the item.

**Expected result:** Fewer full refunds.

**Watch out:** Outside the US: in Australia, faulty items carry a legal right to repair, replacement or refund. Goodwill is extra (see [ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees)). US: state law and your published policy set the rules (see the [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance)).

**Step 3.** Always refund to the original payment method.

**Expected result:** No dispute from money sent another way.

**Step 4.** Note every refund reason.

**Expected result:** You can fix the cause.

**Step 5.** Enter refunds in Helix.

**Expected result:** Profit stays true.
