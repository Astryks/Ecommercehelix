# Module 25: Account safety, ad accounts and agencies

**Outcome:** every account that runs your business is locked down and owned by you. You know what to do in the first hour of a hack, how to get a disabled ad account reviewed, and how to work with or leave an agency without losing your history.

**Related:** [Module 7 Meta ads in practice](07-meta-ads.md), [Module 20 Ad analysis, audiences and campaign setup](20-ad-analysis-audiences-and-setup.md), [Module 16 Founder rhythm, team and resilience](16-founder-team-productivity.md), [Module 10 Email, SMS and messaging](10-email-sms-whatsapp.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **What it is:** Keep your ad accounts, store and email safe, and owned by you.
>
> **Why it matters:** A hacked or disabled ad account can stop your sales overnight or spend thousands of dollars in hours.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Turn on two-factor sign-in with an app on every account and add a second trusted admin.
> 2. Own your business portfolio and ad account; give agencies partner access only.
> 3. Set an account spending limit and know the first-hour steps if you are hacked.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Two-factor authentication:** A second check, like a code from an app, needed to log in. It stops most account takeovers.
> - **Business portfolio:** Meta's home for your business assets: ad accounts, Pages, pixels and the people who can use them.
> - **Partner access:** Letting an agency or freelancer use your ad account through their own business, while you keep ownership.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 25.1: Lock down every account

One stolen password can cost more than a bad month of sales. Thieves target ad accounts because they can spend your money quickly.

**Do this:**

1. List every account the business depends on: email and domain, Shopify, Meta, Google, TikTok, your email tool, payment providers, bank and domain registrar.
2. Turn on two-factor authentication (a second check after your password) for each one. Use an authenticator app or a security key rather than text messages, which can be hijacked.
3. Use a password manager and a different long password for every account.
4. Save the backup codes for each account somewhere offline that a trusted person can reach.
5. Make sure at least two trusted people are full admins on Meta, Google and Shopify, each with their own login. Never share one login.
6. Every quarter, remove people who no longer work with you and check the list of admins and partners. Helix puts this check on your calendar.
7. Turn on login and spending alerts by email and phone.

**Australia:** report cybercrime and get help at [ReportCyber](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report-and-recover/report). **US:** report online fraud to the [FBI's IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/).

**Self-check:** does every account have two-factor turned on with an app or key? Are there two admins you trust on each ad account?

## Lesson 25.2: Set up your Meta business the safe way

Meta keeps your ad accounts, Pages, pixel and people in a business portfolio (formerly Business Manager). Whoever owns the portfolio controls everything in it.

**Do this:**

1. Create the business portfolio yourself, with your business email, and make yourself and one trusted person admins. See [Meta Business Help Centre](https://www.facebook.com/business/help).
2. Complete business verification when Meta asks. Use your legal business name, address and documents exactly as registered. Missing a verification deadline can pause advertising.
3. Create the ad account inside your own portfolio. Agencies and freelancers get partner access, never ownership.
4. Set an account spending limit a little above your planned monthly spend. If someone breaks in, it caps the damage.
5. Add a backup payment method. Failed payments are a common reason accounts get restricted.
6. Check your billing currency and time zone before your first ad. They cannot be changed later without a new ad account.

**Australia:** if you are registered for GST, add your ABN and confirm GST registration in Meta's billing settings so you are not charged GST. If Meta charges it by mistake, ask Meta for a refund. See [ATO: imported services](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/rules-for-specific-transactions/international-transactions/australian-business-importing-goods-and-services). Your card may also charge an international transaction fee on ad spend; check your card terms.

**US:** use a business card in the business name. Some card issuers decline large overseas or ad charges; call yours before you raise budgets.

**Self-check:** is your ad account owned by your own portfolio? Is there a spending limit and a backup payment method?

## Lesson 25.3: When your ad account is disabled or restricted

Accounts get restricted for failed payments, policy flags, identity checks or unusual activity. Most cases can be fixed if you stay calm and follow Meta's process.

**Do this:**

1. Open Account Quality in Meta and read the exact reason. Do not guess.
2. If a payment failed, fix the payment method first. Pay any balance owed.
3. If Meta asks for identity checks (photo ID or a video selfie), complete them in the official app or Help Centre only.
4. Request a review once, with a short, factual explanation. Then wait. Repeated requests rarely speed things up.
5. Do not open new ad accounts or portfolios to get around a restriction. It can be treated as avoiding enforcement and make things worse.
6. While you wait, keep sales coming from email, Google and organic posts.
7. Afterwards, write down what caused it and fix that: backup payment method, ad copy that fits policy, landing pages that match the ad.

**Australia and US:** watch out for fake "Meta support" messages and pages. Meta does not ask for passwords or payment by direct message.

**Self-check:** do you know where Account Quality is? Do you have other channels ready if ads pause for a week?

## Lesson 25.4: Hacked: the first hour

Speed matters. A hacked ad account can spend thousands in a few hours.

**Do this:**

1. Turn off every active campaign you did not create, then pause all campaigns.
2. Remove unknown people and partners from your business portfolio and ad account.
3. Change your passwords (email first, then Meta) and sign out of all sessions.
4. Call your bank or card provider to block the card linked to the ad account. Ask about disputing fraudulent charges.
5. Report the hack through Meta's official hacked account page: [facebook.com/hacked](https://www.facebook.com/hacked).
6. Write down a timeline with screenshots: when you noticed, what was spent, what you changed.
7. Report the crime (see Lesson 25.1 for where) and keep the reference number for your bank and Meta.
8. Once safe, add the protections in Lessons 25.1 and 25.2, especially a spending limit.

**Australia and US:** tell your customers if a fake store or ad was set up in your name, and report the fake pages to Meta.

**Self-check:** do you know who to call at your bank, and where the hacked page is, before anything happens?

## Lesson 25.5: Working with agencies, and leaving one

An agency can save time, but your accounts, data and history must stay yours. See also [Lesson 16.7](16-founder-team-productivity.md#lesson-167-agencies-vs-in-house).

**Do this:**

1. Before you sign, list what you must own: ad accounts, pixel and datasets, Shopify, domain, email tool and Google Ads account. Give the agency partner or user access only.
2. Put it in the contract: you own all accounts, content and data; notice period; how handover works; what reports you receive.
3. Ask for a monthly report you understand: spend, sales, profit and what they changed.
4. To leave, give notice and ask for a handover pack: campaign notes, audiences, creative files and any login they set up for you.
5. On the last day, remove their access, change shared passwords and check nothing is still billing to their card.
6. If the agency owns your ad account, ask them in writing to transfer it. If they will not, create a new ad account in your own portfolio and share your pixel to it. Past campaign history stays behind, but your pixel data is yours.
7. For the first month on your own, change one thing at a time and watch profit daily in Helix.

**Australia and US:** keep copies of all creative and reports. Contracts that say the agency owns the ad account are worth refusing at the start.

**Self-check:** do you own every account in your list? Does your contract say so?

## Lesson 25.6: Trusted sender names for texts and emails

Phone networks and inbox providers now check that messages really come from you. Unregistered senders end up labelled or in spam.

**Do this:**

1. Email: make sure your sending domain is authenticated (SPF, DKIM and DMARC). Your email tool's settings walk you through it.
2. Send from your own domain, never a free email address.
3. Text messages: register your sender details through your SMS provider before you send campaigns.
4. Keep your sender name the same everywhere so customers recognise you.

**Australia:** since 1 July 2026, texts sent with a business name as the sender need that name registered on the national SMS Sender ID Register through your provider. Unregistered names show as "Unverified". The name must match your business name, trademark or domain. See [ACMA: sending texts with your business name](https://www.acma.gov.au/sending-text-messages-your-business-or-organisation-name).

**US:** business texting from standard numbers needs carrier registration (often called 10DLC), and toll-free numbers need verification. Your SMS provider handles the forms.

**Self-check:** is your sending domain authenticated? Is your SMS sender registered?
