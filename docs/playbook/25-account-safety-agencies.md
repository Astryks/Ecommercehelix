# Module 25: Account safety, ad accounts and agencies

**Outcome:** every account that runs your business is locked down and owned by you. You will know what to do in the first hour of a hack. You will know how to get a blocked ad account reviewed. You will also know how to work with or leave an agency without losing your history.

**Related:** [Module 7 Meta ads in practice](07-meta-ads.md), [Module 20 Ad analysis, audiences and campaign setup](20-ad-analysis-audiences-and-setup.md), [Module 16 Founder rhythm, team and resilience](16-founder-team-productivity.md), [Module 10 Email, SMS and messaging](10-email-sms-whatsapp.md)

> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Grow (keep more of every sale, then scale). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** Keep your ad accounts, store and email safe, and owned by you.
>
> **Why it matters:** A hacked or blocked ad account can stop your sales overnight. It can also spend thousands of dollars in hours.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Turn on two-factor sign-in with an app on every account and add a second trusted admin.
> 2. Own your business portfolio and ad account; give agencies partner access only.
> 3. Set an account spending limit and know the first-hour steps if you are hacked.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Two-factor authentication:** A second check, like a code from an app, needed to log in. It stops most account takeovers.
> - **Business portfolio:** Meta's home for your business: ad accounts, Pages, pixels and the people who can use them.
> - **Partner access:** An agency or freelancer uses your ad account through their own business. You still own it.
>

## Lesson 25.1: Lock down every account
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** One stolen password can cost more than a bad month of sales. Thieves target ad accounts because they can spend your money fast. An hour of lock-down work closes most doors.

**Step 1.** List every account the business needs. That is email and domain, Shopify, Meta, Google and TikTok. Add your email tool, payment providers, bank and the place you bought your domain.

**Expected result:** You have one list of every account.

**Step 2.** Turn on two-factor authentication (a second check after your password) on each one. Use an authenticator app or security key, not text messages.

**Expected result:** Every account asks for a second check at login.

**Watch out:** Text message codes can be hijacked by moving your number to another SIM.

**Step 3.** Store a different long password for every account in a password manager.

**Expected result:** No password is reused.

**Step 4.** Save each account's backup codes offline where a trusted person can reach them.

**Expected result:** You can get back in if you lose your phone.

**Step 5.** Make at least 2 trusted people full admins on Meta, Google and Shopify, each with their own login.

**Expected result:** No one shares a login and no single person can lock you out.

**Step 6.** Every quarter, tidy who has access.

1. Remove people who no longer work with you.
2. Look over the admin and partner lists for anyone you do not know.

**Expected result:** The access check is on your Helix calendar.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 7.** Turn on login and spending alerts by email and phone.

**Expected result:** You hear about strange activity within minutes.

**Watch out:** Report cybercrime in some countries at [ReportCyber](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report-and-recover/report) and in the US at the [FBI's IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/).

## Lesson 25.2: Set up your Meta business the safe way
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Meta keeps your ad accounts, Pages, pixel and people in a business portfolio (formerly Business Manager). Whoever owns the portfolio controls everything in it, so it must be you.

**Step 1.** Make the business portfolio yourself with your business email. Make yourself and one trusted person admins (see [Meta Business Help Centre](https://www.facebook.com/business/help)).

**Expected result:** You own the portfolio and have a backup admin.

**Step 2.** Complete business verification when Meta asks, using your legal name, address and documents exactly as registered.

**Expected result:** The portfolio shows as verified. Missing a deadline can pause ads.

**Step 3.** Create the ad account inside your own portfolio, giving agencies and freelancers partner access only.

**Expected result:** The ad account lists your portfolio as owner.

**Step 4.** Set an account spending limit a little above your planned monthly spend.

**Expected result:** A break-in cannot spend more than the cap.

**Step 5.** Add a backup payment method.

**Expected result:** A failed card does not restrict the account.

**Step 6.** Check billing currency and time zone before your first ad.

**Expected result:** Both are correct; they cannot be changed later without a new ad account.

**Watch out:** Outside the US: if you are registered for GST, add your ABN in Meta billing. Then you are not charged GST. Check your card's overseas fee too (see [ATO: imported services](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/rules-for-specific-transactions/international-transactions/australian-business-importing-goods-and-services)). US: use a business card and call the issuer before raising budgets.

**Step 7.** Connect the ad account to Helix.

**Expected result:** Helix pulls your Meta spend and results each day.

## Lesson 25.3: When your ad account is disabled or restricted
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Ad accounts get restricted for failed payments, policy flags, identity checks or unusual activity. Most cases can be fixed if you stay calm and follow Meta's process.

**Step 1.** Open **Account Quality** in Meta and read the exact reason.

**Expected result:** You know why the account is restricted.

**Step 2.** If a payment failed, fix the payment method and pay any balance owed.

**Expected result:** Billing shows no outstanding balance.

**Step 3.** Meta may ask you to prove who you are (photo ID or video selfie). Only do it in the official app or Help Centre.

**Expected result:** The identity check is submitted through an official channel.

**Watch out:** Meta never asks for passwords or payment by direct message. Ignore fake "Meta support" messages.

**Step 4.** Request a review once with a short, factual explanation, then wait.

**Expected result:** One review request is in progress.

**Step 5.** Do not open new ad accounts or portfolios to get around the restriction.

**Expected result:** You avoid being treated as dodging enforcement.

**Step 6.** While you wait, keep sales coming from email, Google and organic posts.

**Expected result:** Sales continue while ads are paused.

**Step 7.** Afterwards, write down the cause and fix it. Add a backup payment, safe ad text and landing pages that match the ad.

**Expected result:** The same restriction is unlikely to happen again.

## Lesson 25.4: Hacked: the first hour
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Speed matters. A hacked ad account can spend thousands in a few hours, so work through these steps in order.

**Step 1.** Turn off every active campaign you did not create, then pause all campaigns.

**Expected result:** Nothing is spending.

**Step 2.** Remove unknown people and partners from your business portfolio and ad account.

**Expected result:** Only people you know have access.

**Step 3.** Change your passwords (email first, then Meta) and sign out of all sessions.

**Expected result:** The intruder is logged out.

**Step 4.** Call your bank to block the card linked to the ad account and ask about disputing fraudulent charges.

**Expected result:** No more money can leave and a dispute is started.

**Step 5.** Report the hack at [facebook.com/hacked](https://www.facebook.com/hacked).

**Expected result:** Meta has a case open.

**Step 6.** Write a timeline with screenshots: when you noticed, what was spent, what you changed.

**Expected result:** You have evidence for Meta, the bank and police.

**Step 7.** Report the crime (see Lesson 25.1) and keep the reference number.

**Expected result:** You have a reference for your bank and Meta.

**Step 8.** Once safe, add the protections in Lessons 25.1 and 25.2, especially a spending limit.

**Expected result:** The account is locked down.

**Watch out:** If someone set up a fake store or ad in your name, tell your customers. Report the fake pages to Meta.

## Lesson 25.5: Working with agencies, and leaving one
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** An agency can save time, but your accounts, data and history must stay yours.

**Step 1.** Before you sign, list what you must own. That is ad accounts, pixel and datasets, Shopify, domain, email tool and Google Ads. Give the agency partner or user access only.

**Expected result:** Every account is owned by you.

**Step 2.** Put this in the contract: you own all accounts, content and data. Add the notice period, handover steps and the reports you get.

**Expected result:** The contract states you own everything.

**Watch out:** Refuse contracts that say the agency owns the ad account.

**Step 3.** Ask for a monthly report showing spend, sales, profit and what they changed.

**Expected result:** You understand every report.

**Step 4.** To leave, give notice and ask for a handover pack. It should have campaign notes, audiences, ad files and any logins they set up.

**Expected result:** You have everything needed to run ads yourself.

**Step 5.** On the last day, close everything off.

1. Remove their access from every account.
2. Change any shared passwords.
3. Look at billing to make sure nothing still charges their card.

**Expected result:** The agency has no access.

**Step 6.** If the agency owns your ad account, ask in writing for it to be moved to you. Or make a new account in your portfolio and share your pixel to it.

**Expected result:** You run ads from an account you own. Past campaign history may stay behind, but pixel data is yours.

**Step 7.** For your first month alone, change one thing at a time and watch profit daily in Helix.

**Expected result:** You see the effect of each change.

<!-- do:growth -->

**Step 8.** Check that ad accounts, ads, customer lists and reports are in your name. Look in Meta **Business Settings > Ad accounts** and Google **Admin > Access**.

**Expected result:** You own every account and asset.

**Step 9.** Ask your agency to report on MER and contribution profit. MER is total ad spend as a share of total sales. ROAS (sales for each $1 of ads) alone is not enough.

**Expected result:** Agency reports show profit numbers.

**Step 10.** If you bring work in-house, start with the daily ad check (Lesson 7.6). Keep experts for the gaps.

**Expected result:** You run the daily check yourself.

## Lesson 25.6: Trusted sender names for texts and emails
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Phone networks and inbox providers now check that messages really come from you. Unregistered senders get labelled or sent to spam.

**Step 1.** In your email tool's settings, set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC for your sending domain. These three records prove email is really from you.

**Expected result:** The tool shows the domain as authenticated.

**Step 2.** Send from your own domain, never a free email address.

**Expected result:** Emails come from you@yourbrand.

**Step 3.** Register your SMS sender details through your SMS provider before sending campaigns.

**Expected result:** Texts show your business name, not "Unverified".

**Watch out:** Outside the US: since 1 July 2026, business names on texts must be on the national SMS Sender ID Register. The name must match your business name, trademark or domain (see [ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/sending-text-messages-your-business-or-organisation-name)). US: standard numbers need carrier registration (10DLC) and toll-free numbers need verification.

**Step 4.** Use the same sender name everywhere.

**Expected result:** Customers recognise you in every inbox.
