# Module 3: Offer and product

**Outcome:** a reason to buy now that beats every reason to wait, and a simple way to judge which products deserve your ad money.

**Related SOPs:** [04 Offer design](../sops/04-offer-design.md), [16 Customer research](../sops/16-customer-research-and-reviews.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **What it is:** Your offer is what people get and why they should buy now.
>
> **Why it matters:** A great offer makes every ad and email work better. A weak one makes everything cost more.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Write down why a customer should buy from you instead of waiting.
> 2. Add one reason to act now, like a bundle, a gift or free shipping over an amount.
> 3. Check which products make the most profit and push those first.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Margin:** The share of each sale you keep after product cost. A $100 sale with $40 product cost is a 60% margin.
> - **Bundle:** Two or more products sold together, often with a small saving.
> - **Guarantee:** A clear promise that removes the fear of a bad purchase, like a 30-day money-back promise.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 3.1: The five offer levers

Your offer is everything a customer gets and every reason they have to buy now. You can make it stronger by pulling five levers.

| Lever | The question in the shopper's head | What you can use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Promise** | What do I get, and how will my life be better? | A headline about the result. The good things they gain and the bad things they avoid. How others will see them. |
| **Belief** | Why should I believe you? | Demos, reviews, side-by-side comparisons, proof from experts or creators, your story as the founder. |
| **Urgency** | Why buy now and not later? | Limited runs, preorders with a clear ship date, seasonal ranges, honest low-stock alerts. |
| **Safety** | What if it goes wrong? | Easy returns, trials, a guarantee, sizing help, support that is easy to reach, fast dispatch. |
| **Easy yes** | Is it simple and fair? | How you present the price, bundles, a higher-priced option shown next to it, payment options, free shipping. |

## Lesson 3.2: Sharpen the promise

The promise is the main result your product gives. Make it clear in one line.

1. Write the main result in your customer's own words. Reviews are the best source.
2. Write the problem or pain it takes away.
3. Write how other people will see your customer differently.
4. Combine these into one line. Use it under your product title. Use it again as the opening line of an ad.

## Lesson 3.3: Stack belief

People do not buy until they believe the product will work for them. Build that belief on purpose.

1. **List every reason someone might not buy.** These are objections. Aim for 20. Ask your support team, read reviews and returns notes.
2. **Group them.** Price, quality, fit or size, trust, timing, and "do I need it?".
3. **Answer each one with proof.** Put the answers on the product page, in the FAQ, in ads and in your welcome emails.

**Example:** a store selling a high-priced item added a short home trial. Sales from the same visitors went up about three times, and returns stayed low.

## Lesson 3.4: Real scarcity

Scarcity means there is a real reason the product may not be available later. It gives people a reason to act now.

**Honest ways to do it:**

1. Limited production runs and numbered batches.
2. Preorders with a clear date, shipping within weeks.
3. Seasonal ranges or new colours released for a short time.
4. Low-stock emails to people who looked at the product, and to your best customers.

**Never** use fake countdown timers or made-up stock counts. They destroy trust and may break consumer law.

## Lesson 3.5: Remove risk

Shoppers worry about making a bad purchase. Remove as much of that worry as you can.

1. Reviews with photos.
2. A sizing tool or size chart.
3. Live chat.
4. A real person and face behind the brand.
5. A dispatch promise ("ships within 24 hours") that you keep.
6. A clear, easy returns policy.

**Decision rule:** generous returns usually add more sales than they cost. After you change your returns policy, measure your return rate for 60 days to check.

## Lesson 3.6: Make yes easy (pricing)

How you show the price changes how people feel about it. Test these ideas one at a time.

1. **Cost per use.** "Under $1 a day" feels smaller than "$29".
2. **Rounding tests.** Moving from just under one price point to just under the next one (for example from $39 to $49) often changes the conversion rate very little, while you keep more money.
3. **Anchors.** Show a premium option or the full bundle value next to the single price. The single price then looks better value.
4. **Decoys.** Add a middle option that makes the option you want them to pick look like the obvious choice.
5. **Free shipping or a lower price?** Test which one your customers prefer.
6. **Buy now pay later** (Afterpay, Klarna, Shop Pay Installments). It helps with higher prices because people can pay in parts.

## Lesson 3.7: Product-market fit snapshot

This shows which products deserve more ad money. Do it once a month.

1. For each product, write down: ad spend, sales, return (sales ÷ ad spend), units sold, and margin (the share you keep after product cost).
2. Put each product into one of four boxes and act:

| Box | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| High spend, good return | This is your engine. Make more ads for it, give it more budget, and protect the stock. |
| Low spend, good return | It has not been tested properly. Make a batch of new ads for it. |
| High spend, poor return | Fix the product page and offer, or cut the spend. |
| Low spend, poor return | Let it sell without ads, or stop selling it. |

## Lesson 3.8: New product pipeline

New products keep the store growing. Use a simple process.

1. **Collect ideas.** Look at customer requests, reviews, what people search for, and products that go with your best sellers.
2. **Test cheaply first.** A waitlist page, a preorder, a small batch, or feedback from creators.
3. **Launch with a plan** (see Module 13). Build excitement, give early access, prepare a launch offer and have ads ready.
4. **Review after 30 days.** Use the snapshot in Lesson 3.7.

## Lesson 3.9: Offers expire

An offer that works today will not work forever. Competitors copy it, the market changes and customers get used to it.

**Decision rule:** review your main offer every 3 months. Also review it whenever your conversion rate drops and you cannot find another cause.

## Self-check

1. Which lever does a free returns policy belong to?
2. Name two honest ways to create urgency.
3. A product has low ad spend and a great return. What do you do?
4. Why list 20 objections?
5. When should you review your main offer?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Safety.
2. Any two of: limited runs, preorders with a date, seasonal releases, honest low-stock alerts.
3. Make a batch of new ads for it and give it more budget to test.
4. Each objection you answer removes a reason not to buy. The list feeds your pages, ads and emails.
5. Every 3 months, and whenever conversion drops without another cause.
