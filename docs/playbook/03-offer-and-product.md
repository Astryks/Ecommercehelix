# Module 3: Offer and product

**Outcome:** a reason to buy now that beats every reason to wait, and a simple way to judge which products deserve your ad money.

**Related SOPs:** [04 Offer design](../sops/04-offer-design.md), [16 Customer research](../sops/16-customer-research-and-reviews.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Convert (turn visits into sales). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** Your offer is what people get and why they should buy now.
>
> **Why it matters:** A great offer makes every ad and email work better. A weak one makes everything cost more.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Write down why a customer should buy from you instead of waiting.
> 2. Add one reason to act now, like a bundle, a gift or free shipping over an amount.
> 3. Check which products make the most profit and push those first.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Margin:** The share of each sale you keep after product cost. A $100 sale with $40 product cost is a 60% margin.
> - **Bundle:** Two or more products sold together, often with a small saving.
> - **Guarantee:** A clear promise that removes the fear of a bad purchase, like a 30-day money-back promise.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 3.1: The five offer levers
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Your offer is everything a customer gets and every reason they have to buy now rather than later. When ads and site are fine but sales are slow, the offer is usually the gap. There are five levers you can pull, and you only need to strengthen the weakest one first.

**Step 1.** Read the five levers in the table below (promise, belief, urgency, safety, easy yes) and score your main product from 1 to 5 on each.

**Expected result:** You have five scores on one line, for example "Promise 4, Belief 2, Urgency 3, Safety 4, Easy yes 3".

**Step 2.** Circle the lowest score and open the matching lesson: promise 3.2, belief 3.3, urgency 3.4, safety 3.5, easy yes 3.6.

**Expected result:** You know which single lever to work on this week.

### Good to know

| Lever | The question in the shopper's head | What you can use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Promise** | What do I get, and how will my life be better? | A headline about the result. The good things they gain and the bad things they avoid. How others will see them. |
| **Belief** | Why should I believe you? | Demos, reviews, side-by-side comparisons, proof from experts or creators, your story as the founder. |
| **Urgency** | Why buy now and not later? | Limited runs, preorders with a clear ship date, seasonal ranges, honest low-stock alerts. |
| **Safety** | What if it goes wrong? | Easy returns, trials, a guarantee, sizing help, support that is easy to reach, fast dispatch. |
| **Easy yes** | Is it simple and fair? | How you present the price, bundles, a higher-priced option shown next to it, payment options, free shipping. |

## Lesson 3.2: Sharpen the promise
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** The promise is the main result your product gives. If shoppers cannot repeat it back in one line, they will not buy. Reviews hold the exact words that sell.

**Step 1.** Read your 20 most recent 4 and 5 star reviews and copy the phrases customers use for the result they got.

**Expected result:** You have a list of 5 to 10 phrases in customers' own words.

**Step 2.** Write one sentence for the problem or pain the product takes away, using the same words.

**Expected result:** You have a pain sentence, for example "No more tangled cables in your bag".

**Step 3.** Write one sentence for how other people will see your customer differently after buying.

**Expected result:** You have a "how others see me" sentence.

**Step 4.** Combine the three into one line of 15 words or fewer, then add it under your product title (Shopify: **Products > [product] > Description**, first line) and use it as the first line of your next ad.

**Expected result:** The same promise line appears on the product page and in an ad.

**Watch out:** Lead with the result, not the feature. "Sleep through the night" beats "Memory foam core".

## Lesson 3.3: Stack belief
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** People do not buy until they believe the product will work for them. Each objection (a reason not to buy) that you answer with proof removes a barrier. Stores that answer objections on purpose often see large jumps in conversion from the same visitors.

**Step 1.** List 20 reasons someone might not buy, using support emails, reviews, return notes and questions from your team.

**Expected result:** You have a numbered list of 20 objections.

**Step 2.** Group them under six headings: price, quality, fit or size, trust, timing, and "do I need it?".

**Expected result:** Every objection sits under one heading, and you can see which group is biggest.

**Step 3.** Write one proof for each objection (a review quote, a photo, a guarantee, a stat or a demo video).

**Expected result:** Each objection has an answer you can show, not just claim.

**Step 4.** Add the answers to the product page FAQ, your ads and your welcome emails, starting with the biggest group.

**Expected result:** The top objections are answered in at least three places shoppers see.

<!-- do:flow-welcome -->

### Good to know

**Example:** a store selling a high-priced item added a short home trial. Sales from the same visitors went up about three times, and returns stayed low.

## Lesson 3.4: Real scarcity
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Scarcity means there is a real reason the product may not be available later, which gives people a reason to act now. Honest scarcity builds trust; fake timers destroy it and can break consumer law.

**Step 1.** Pick one honest scarcity method that fits your product: a limited production run or numbered batch, a preorder with a clear ship date, a seasonal range or colour for a short time, or low-stock alerts.

**Expected result:** You have chosen one method you can stand behind.

**Step 2.** Write the reason in one sentence on the product page, for example "Batch 4 of 6: 300 made, ships 14 November".

**Expected result:** Shoppers can see why it might not be available later.

**Step 3.** Set up a low-stock email to people who viewed the product and to your best customers when stock falls below about 2 weeks of sales.

**Expected result:** An email goes out automatically when stock gets low.

**Watch out:** Never use fake countdown timers or made-up stock counts.

## Lesson 3.5: Remove risk
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Shoppers worry about making a bad purchase. Every worry you remove turns more visits into orders. Generous, clear policies usually add more sales than they cost.

**Step 1.** Turn on photo reviews in your review app and email recent buyers asking for a photo with their review.

**Expected result:** Your best sellers show reviews with customer photos.

<!-- do:flow-review-request -->

**Step 2.** Add a size chart or sizing tool to every product where fit matters.

**Expected result:** Each relevant product page has a size guide link above the Add to cart button.

**Step 3.** Turn on live chat (Shopify Inbox is free) for your opening hours.

**Expected result:** A chat bubble appears on your store and messages reach your phone.

**Step 4.** Add a real face to the brand: a founder photo and two sentences on the About page.

**Expected result:** Shoppers can see a real person behind the store.

**Step 5.** Write a dispatch promise you can keep (for example "Ships within 24 hours, Monday to Friday") and a clear returns policy, and show both near the price.

**Expected result:** Dispatch time and returns are visible without scrolling on mobile.

**Watch out:** After changing your returns policy, measure your return rate for 60 days to check it pays off.

## Lesson 3.6: Make yes easy (pricing)
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** How you show the price changes how people feel about it. Small framing changes can lift conversion or let you keep more per order. Test one idea at a time so you know what worked.

**Step 1.** Add a cost-per-use line under the price, for example "Under $1 a day" for a $29 monthly product.

**Expected result:** The price feels smaller without changing it.

**Step 2.** Test rounding up one price point (for example $39 to $49) on one product for two weeks.

**Expected result:** You can compare conversion and profit per order. Often conversion barely moves while you keep more money.

**Step 3.** Show an anchor: a premium option or the full bundle value next to the single price.

**Expected result:** The single price looks like better value beside it.

**Step 4.** Add a decoy middle option that makes the option you want people to pick look like the obvious choice.

**Expected result:** More shoppers pick your target option.

**Step 5.** Test free shipping against a lower price, then turn on buy now pay later (Afterpay, Klarna or Shop Pay Installments) in **Settings > Payments** for higher-priced items.

**Expected result:** You know which one your customers prefer, and shoppers can pay in parts.

## Lesson 3.7: Product-market fit snapshot
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Not every product deserves your ad money. A monthly snapshot shows which ones to push, which to test more and which to stop. It takes about 20 minutes.

**Step 1.** For each product, write down last month's ad spend, sales, return (sales divided by ad spend), units sold and margin (share of price you keep after product cost).

**Expected result:** You have one row per product with five numbers.

**Step 2.** Put each product into one of the four boxes in the table below and do the action listed for that box.

**Expected result:** Every product has a box and a next step, for example "low spend, good return: make new ads and give it more budget".

**Step 3.** Move ad budget towards the "good return" boxes in your next weekly review.

**Expected result:** More of your spend goes to products that pay it back.

### Good to know

**2. Put each product into one of four boxes and act:**

| Box | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| High spend, good return | This is your engine. Make more ads for it, give it more budget, and protect the stock. |
| Low spend, good return | It has not been tested properly. Make a batch of new ads for it. |
| High spend, poor return | Fix the product page and offer, or cut the spend. |
| Low spend, poor return | Let it sell without ads, or stop selling it. |

## Lesson 3.8: New product pipeline
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** New products keep a store growing, but launching untested ideas ties up cash. A simple pipeline lets you test cheaply first and launch with a plan.

**Step 1.** Start an ideas list from customer requests, reviews, search terms and products that go with your best sellers.

**Expected result:** You have at least 5 product ideas with a reason next to each.

**Step 2.** Test the best idea cheaply: a waitlist page, a preorder, a small batch or feedback from 5 creators.

**Expected result:** You have real demand evidence, such as waitlist sign-ups or preorders.

**Step 3.** Launch with a plan from Module 13: build excitement, give early access, prepare a launch offer and have ads ready.

**Expected result:** The launch has dates, an offer and ads ready before day one.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 4.** Review the product after 30 days using the snapshot in Lesson 3.7.

**Expected result:** The new product sits in one of the four boxes with a next step.

## Lesson 3.9: Offers expire
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** An offer that works today will not work forever. Competitors copy it, the market changes and customers get used to it. A regular review keeps it fresh.

**Step 1.** Add a recurring reminder every 3 months to review your main offer.

**Expected result:** A reminder sits in your calendar for the next review date.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 2.** When conversion rate drops for 2 weeks and you cannot find another cause, review the offer straight away using the five levers in Lesson 3.1.

**Expected result:** You have a fresh lever score and one change to test.

## Self-check

1. Which lever does a free returns policy belong to?
2. Name two honest ways to create urgency.
3. A product has low ad spend and a great return. What do you do?
4. Why list 20 objections?
5. When should you review your main offer?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Safety.
2. Any two of: limited runs, preorders with a date, seasonal releases, honest low-stock alerts.
3. Make a batch of new ads for it and give it more budget to test.
4. Each objection you answer removes a reason not to buy. The list feeds your pages, ads and emails.
5. Every 3 months, and whenever conversion drops without another cause.
