# Module 3: Offer and product

**Outcome:** a reason to buy now that beats every reason to wait, and a way to judge which products deserve your budget.

**Related SOPs:** [04 Offer design](../sops/04-offer-design.md), [16 Customer research](../sops/16-customer-research-and-reviews.md)

---

## Lesson 3.1: The five offer levers

| Lever | Question | Tools |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | What do I get and who do I become? | Outcome headline, "towards" and "away from" benefits, status shift |
| Belief | Why should I believe it? | Demo, reviews, comparisons, expert or creator proof, founder story |
| Urgency | Why now? | Limited runs, preorders with dates, seasonal ranges, honest low-stock alerts |
| Safety | What if it goes wrong? | Generous returns, trials, guarantees, sizing help, visible support, fast dispatch |
| Easy yes | Is it simple and fair? | Price framing, bundles, anchors, payment options, free shipping |

## Lesson 3.2: Sharpen the promise

1. Write the main outcome in the customer's words.
2. Write the pain it removes.
3. Write how others will see them differently.
4. Combine into one line under your product title and one ad hook.

## Lesson 3.3: Stack belief

1. List every reason someone might not buy (aim for 20).
2. Group them: price, quality, fit, trust, timing, need.
3. Answer each with proof on the product page, in FAQs, in ads and in the welcome flow.
Example: a high-priced item with a short home trial converted about three times better and returns stayed low.

## Lesson 3.4: Real scarcity

- Limited production runs and numbered batches.
- Preorders that ship within weeks, clearly dated.
- Seasonal or colour drops.
- Low-stock emails to people who viewed the product and to VIPs.
Never use fake timers or invented stock counts. They erode trust and may breach consumer law.

## Lesson 3.5: Remove risk

Reviews with photos, sizing tools, live chat, a real person and face behind the brand, fast dispatch promises, clear returns. Lenient returns usually lift sales by more than they cost; measure return rate after changes.

## Lesson 3.6: Make yes easy (pricing)

- Cost per use ("under $1 a day").
- Price rounding tests (moving from just under one price point to just under the next often has little effect on conversion).
- Anchors: show the premium option or the bundle value next to the single price.
- Decoys: a middle option that makes your preferred option the obvious pick.
- Free shipping vs a lower price: test which your customers prefer.
- Buy now, pay later for higher prices.

## Lesson 3.7: Product-market fit snapshot

Monthly, per product: ad spend, revenue, return (revenue ÷ spend), units, margin.
| Quadrant | Action |
| --- | --- |
| High spend, good return | Your engine. More creative, more budget, protect stock. |
| Low spend, good return | Under-tested. Give it a creative batch. |
| High spend, poor return | Fix page and offer or cut spend. |
| Low spend, poor return | Leave organic or retire. |

## Lesson 3.8: New product pipeline

1. Ideas from customer requests, reviews, search data and best-seller adjacencies.
2. Validate cheaply: waitlist page, preorder, small batch, creator feedback.
3. Launch with a plan (Module 13): hype, early access, launch offer, creative ready.
4. Review after 30 days with the snapshot.

## Lesson 3.9: Offers expire

Competitors copy, markets shift and customers get used to an offer. Review your main offer every quarter and whenever conversion drops without another cause.

## Self-check

1. Which lever does a free returns policy belong to?
2. Name two honest ways to create urgency.
3. A product has low spend and great return. What do you do?
4. Why list 20 objections?
5. When should you review your main offer?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Safety.
2. Limited runs, dated preorders, seasonal drops, honest low-stock alerts (any two).
3. Give it a creative batch and more budget to test.
4. Each objection answered removes a reason not to buy; the list feeds pages, ads and emails.
5. Quarterly, and when conversion drops without another cause.
</details>
