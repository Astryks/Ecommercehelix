# Module 2: Diagnose before you fix

**Outcome:** you can name the one constraint holding your profit back and pick the right type of fix.

**Related SOPs:** [03 Constraint diagnosis](../sops/03-constraint-diagnosis.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **What it is:** Before you change anything, find the one thing holding profit back.
>
> **Why it matters:** Fixing the wrong thing wastes weeks. Usually only one problem is the real blocker at a time.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Look at your numbers: visits, conversion rate, order value and ad costs.
> 2. Find the weakest one compared with a healthy range.
> 3. Fix only that one this week, then check again.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Conversion rate:** Out of every 100 visits, how many end in an order. 2% means 2 orders per 100 visits.
> - **AOV:** Average order value: total sales divided by number of orders.
> - **CPA:** Cost per acquisition: how much ad money it took to get one sale.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 2.1: Three kinds of constraint

| Constraint | Looks like | Fix family |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throughput | Good ratios, too few orders | Traffic, creative, conversion, new channels |
| Efficiency | One or more cost ratios too high | Pricing, product cost, shipping, AOV, ad efficiency |
| Capacity | Stockouts, cash squeeze, slow dispatch | Stock planning, cash forecasting, supplier terms, fulfilment |

Most stores are throughput-constrained even when they believe they have an "ads problem".

## Lesson 2.2: The five situations

1. **Healthy profit (10%+):** scale. Buy every customer you can profitably serve.
2. **Fixed costs heavy:** scale with care. Doubling revenue halves FCR. Trim waste but keep the team and tools you need to grow.
3. **Variable costs heavy:** stop scaling and fix unit economics first.
4. **Marketing heavy (high MER):** diagnose the funnel before changing ads.
5. **Everything borderline:** fix MER first, then VCR, then scale.

## Lesson 2.3: Fixing variable costs

- [ ] Product cost: renegotiate on volume, change packaging spec, consolidate suppliers, review MOQ trade-offs.
- [ ] Shipping above 15% of revenue: renegotiate carrier rates, change box sizes, raise the free-shipping threshold, charge for express.
- [ ] Packaging that is luxurious but does not lift repeat rate: simplify.
- [ ] Product mix: push higher-margin products in ads and on the homepage.
- [ ] Pricing: test a small price rise (often barely noticed) and rounding up to the next price point.
- [ ] AOV: bundles and tiers spread per-order costs over more revenue (Module 9).
- [ ] Fees: check payment and app fees as % of revenue.

## Lesson 2.4: Fixing fixed costs

- [ ] List every subscription. Cancel unused apps.
- [ ] Ask what each role produces. Is it tied to an outcome?
- [ ] Compare agency retainers with what they deliver.
- [ ] Remember the alternative fix: grow revenue so fixed costs become a smaller share.

## Lesson 2.5: Fixing marketing costs (high MER)

Follow the tree:
1. Temporary shock? (stockout, sale hangover, competitor sale, season, tracking break, site outage)
2. RPV below benchmark? Site and offer problem. Split into AOV vs conversion rate.
3. RPV fine? Ad problem. Check outbound CTR (below 0.5% means message or creative), cost per click versus RPV, frequency, and whether engaging ads fail to convert (page mismatch).

**RPV guide:** $2.50 to $4 is a healthy band for many categories; below $2 is challenging; below $1 is very hard to advertise profitably. Fashion often runs lower RPV with higher volume.

## Lesson 2.6: When several KPIs are high at once

Rank by dollars: which ratio, if moved back to its healthy band, adds the most profit per month? Fix that one first. Usually it is MER (fast to move) or VCR (biggest lasting impact).

## Lesson 2.7: The scale test

1. Pick steps (for example $300, then $600, then $900 a day).
2. Expect efficiency to drop as spend rises. Model a worse MER at each step.
3. Find break-even: the revenue at which extra spend still adds profit.
4. If break-even needs you to more than double, fix drivers first.
5. Your worst case is the extra spend. Decide it before starting.
6. Hold each step 10 to 14 days. MER usually worsens first and settles. If it does not settle, step back and diagnose.
7. Have fresh creative ready before you scale.

## Example

A $50k/month store: MER 20%, VCR 40%, FCR 30%, profit 10%. Doubling revenue to $100k with the same fixed dollars gives FCR 15%. Even if MER rises to 30% while scaling, profit becomes 100 - 30 - 40 - 15 = 15%. Growth was the fix for fixed-cost weight.

## Self-check

1. Which constraint is most common?
2. Name three fixes for high shipping cost.
3. RPV is $3.20 and MER is high. Site or ads?
4. Outbound CTR is 0.4%. What do you change?
5. What is the worst-case loss of a scale test?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Throughput.
2. Renegotiate rates, right-size boxes, raise the free-shipping threshold (also: charge for express, regional 3PL).
3. Ads (RPV is healthy).
4. The creative, message or offer in the ad.
5. The extra ad spend.
</details>
