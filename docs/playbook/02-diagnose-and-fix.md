# Module 2: Diagnose before you fix

**Outcome:** you can name the one thing holding your profit back and pick the right kind of fix.

**Related SOPs:** [03 Constraint diagnosis](../sops/03-constraint-diagnosis.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Grow (keep more of every sale, then scale). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** Before you change anything, find the one thing holding profit back.
>
> **Why it matters:** Fixing the wrong thing wastes weeks. Usually only one problem is the real blocker at a time.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Look at your numbers: visits, conversion rate, order value and ad costs.
> 2. Find the weakest one compared with a healthy range.
> 3. Fix only that one this week, then check again.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Conversion rate:** Out of every 100 visits, how many end in an order. 2% means 2 orders per 100 visits.
> - **AOV:** Average order value: total sales divided by number of orders.
> - **CPA:** Cost per acquisition: how much ad money it took to get one sale.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 2.1: Three kinds of constraint
<!-- stage:grow -->

A constraint is the one thing that limits your profit right now. There are three kinds.

| Kind | What it looks like | What fixes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Throughput** (not enough sales) | Your cost ratios are healthy, but you get too few orders. | More visitors, better ads, a better-converting site, new channels. |
| **Efficiency** (costs too high) | One or more cost ratios are too high. | Better prices, cheaper product cost, cheaper shipping, bigger orders, better ads. |
| **Capacity** (cannot keep up) | Products sell out, cash runs short, orders ship slowly. | Stock planning, a cash forecast, better supplier terms, faster fulfilment. |

Most stores are short of sales (throughput), even when the owner thinks they have an "ads problem".

## Lesson 2.2: The five situations
<!-- stage:grow -->

Look at your scorecard. Which of these five sounds like you? Each one has a different next move.

1. **Healthy profit (10% or more):** grow. Win every customer you can serve at a profit.
2. **Fixed costs are heavy:** grow, carefully. If sales double, fixed costs become half as big a share of sales. Cut waste, but keep the people and tools you need to grow.
3. **Variable costs are heavy:** stop growing for now. Fix the cost of each order first (product cost, shipping, order size).
4. **Marketing is heavy (MER is high, so ads eat too much of sales):** find where buyers are dropping out before you change your ads. See Lesson 2.5.
5. **Everything is borderline:** fix MER first, then VCR (order costs), then grow.

## Lesson 2.3: Fixing variable costs
<!-- stage:grow -->

Variable costs are the costs that come with each order. Work through this list.

1. **Product cost.** Ask suppliers for a lower price on bigger orders. Change the packaging spec. Use fewer suppliers. Weigh up a bigger minimum order (MOQ) against the cash it ties up.
2. **Shipping above 15% of sales.** Ask carriers for better rates. Use boxes that fit the product better. Raise the free shipping amount. Charge for express delivery.
3. **Packaging.** If fancy packaging does not make people buy again, make it simpler.
4. **Product mix.** Show your higher-margin products in ads and on the homepage. Margin is the share of the price you keep after product cost.
5. **Pricing.** Test a small price rise. Customers often barely notice. Also test rounding up to the next price point.
6. **Order size.** Bundles and "buy more, save more" tiers spread the cost of each order over more sales (see Module 9).
7. **Fees.** Check payment and app fees as a share of sales.

## Lesson 2.4: Fixing fixed costs
<!-- stage:grow -->

Fixed costs stay the same no matter how many orders you get.

1. List every subscription. Cancel the apps you do not use.
2. For each role, ask: what does it produce? Is it tied to a result?
3. Compare what you pay agencies with what they deliver.
4. Remember the other fix: grow sales. Then fixed costs become a smaller share.

## Lesson 2.5: Fixing marketing costs (high MER)
<!-- stage:grow -->

High MER means ads eat too much of your sales. Do not rush to change the ads. Follow these steps in order.

1. **Is it a short-term shock?** Check for: a best seller out of stock, the slow week after a sale, a competitor's sale, the season, tracking that stopped working, or the site going down. If yes, fix that or wait it out.
2. **Is RPV (revenue per visit) below the healthy range?** Then the problem is the site or the offer, not the ads. Split it further: is the order size (AOV) low, or is the conversion rate (visits that turn into orders) low?
3. **Is RPV fine?** Then the problem is the ads. Check:
   - **Outbound CTR** (out of 100 people who see the ad, how many click through to your site). Below 0.5% means the message or the ad itself is weak.
   - **Cost per click compared with RPV.** If a click costs more than a visit earns, you lose money.
   - **Frequency** (how often the same person sees the ad). Too high means people are tired of it.
   - **Ads that get lots of likes and clicks but no sales.** This usually means the page does not match what the ad promised.

**What is a healthy RPV?**

- $2.50 to $4 per visit is healthy for many types of store.
- Below $2 is hard work.
- Below $1 makes it very hard to advertise at a profit.
- Fashion stores often have a lower RPV but more visitors.

## Lesson 2.6: When several KPIs are high at once
<!-- stage:grow -->

A KPI is a key number you track. If several are bad at once, do not try to fix everything.

1. For each bad number, ask: if this went back to its healthy range, how many dollars of profit would it add each month?
2. Fix the one that adds the most dollars first.
3. Usually that is MER (it is fast to change) or VCR (it has the biggest lasting effect).

## Lesson 2.7: The scale test
<!-- stage:grow -->

"Scaling" means spending more on ads to get more sales. Do it as a planned test, not a leap.

1. **Pick spending steps.** For example $300 a day, then $600, then $900.
2. **Expect results to get a little worse as you spend more.** Plan for a higher MER at each step. More spend usually means each sale costs a bit more.
3. **Find your break-even point.** That is the level of sales where the extra spend still adds profit.
4. **If break-even needs more than double your current sales, stop.** Fix your costs first (Lessons 2.3 and 2.4).
5. **Decide your worst case before you start.** The most you can lose is the extra ad spend. Pick a number you are OK losing.
6. **Hold each step for 10 to 14 days.** MER usually gets worse at first, then settles. If it does not settle, go back one step and find out why.
7. **Have new ads ready before you scale.** More spend tires out your ads faster.

## Example

A store makes $50,000 a month. MER is 20%, VCR 40%, FCR 30%, and profit 10%.

1. The store doubles sales to $100,000. Fixed costs stay the same in dollars, so FCR drops to 15%.
2. While growing, MER rises to 30%. That is normal.
3. New profit = 100 - 30 (MER) - 40 (VCR) - 15 (FCR) = 15%.

**What it shows:** the store's problem was heavy fixed costs. Growth was the fix, even though ads got less efficient.

## Self-check

1. Which kind of constraint is most common?
2. Name three fixes for high shipping cost.
3. RPV is $3.20 and MER is high. Is it the site or the ads?
4. Outbound CTR is 0.4%. What do you change?
5. What is the most you can lose in a scale test?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Not enough sales (throughput).
2. Ask for better carrier rates, use boxes that fit, raise the free shipping amount. Also: charge for express, or use a warehouse closer to your customers.
3. The ads. An RPV of $3.20 is healthy.
4. The ad itself: the picture or video, the message, or the offer.
5. The extra ad spend.
</details>
