# Module 13: Promotions, launches and big sales

**Outcome:** a year of sales peaks that are planned, profitable and easy to repeat, and a step-by-step plan for every big sale, from setting the target to the weeks after.

**Related SOP:** [15 Promo calendar and sales](../sops/15-promo-calendar-and-sales.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Convert (turn visits into sales). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** How to plan sales and launches so they make money, not just noise.
>
> **Why it matters:** Busy periods can make your year. Planned sales are far more profitable than last-minute ones.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Mark your big dates for the year.
> 2. For each sale, set a profit target and the discount you can afford.
> 3. Warm up your list and audiences before the sale starts.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Warm audience:** People who know you: they visited, followed or watched your videos.
> - **Margin:** The share of each sale you keep after product cost. A $100 sale with $40 product cost is a 60% margin.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 13.1: The promotional rhythm
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** A year of promotions works best as a rhythm, so customers learn when to look and you are never scrambling. Four kinds of promotion cover the year, and only one of them needs deep discounts, as a last resort.

**Step 1.** Pick a regular day and time for new product releases (for example the first Thursday of each month, 7pm) and put the next 6 on your calendar.

**Expected result:** Your release dates are set and repeat monthly.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 2.** Add gifting events and cultural moments (Mother's Day, Valentine's Day) with a curated selection rather than a store-wide discount.

**Expected result:** Each event has a product selection planned.

**Step 3.** Choose 3 to 4 big sales peaks for the year, with Black Friday usually the biggest.

**Expected result:** Your peaks are on the calendar with dates.

<!-- do:bfcm -->

**Step 4.** For slow stock, plan gentle tools first: a free gift with purchase, bundles, VIP codes or gift cards.

**Expected result:** Slow stock has a clearing plan that avoids deep discounts.

<!-- do:stock -->

## Lesson 13.2: Launch phases
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** A launch has four phases, and each needs its own message. Skipping the build-up or the follow-up leaves most of the sales on the table.

**Step 1.** Before launch, tease the product and open a waitlist and early-access sign-up.

**Expected result:** People are signing up before the product is out.

**Step 2.** On launch day, give VIPs (best customers) access first, then everyone.

**Expected result:** VIPs get a head start and feel looked after.

<!-- do:flow-vip -->

**Step 3.** Mid-launch, release something new for 24 hours, like a new colour or a bundle.

**Expected result:** The launch gets a second spike.

**Step 4.** After launch, share reviews, results and restock news.

**Expected result:** Proof keeps sales going after the launch week.

## Lesson 13.3: New product drop campaign structure
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** How you structure a product drop depends on your spend. Either way, your warm audiences and email list should get first access while normal campaigns keep running.

**Step 1.** If you spend a lot (roughly $1,000 a day or more), make a separate launch campaign; if less, add launch ads to your normal cold and warm campaigns.

**Expected result:** Launch ads have a home that fits your budget.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 2.** Give warm audiences and your email list first access and keep evergreen (always-on) campaigns running.

**Expected result:** Your best audiences hear first and your steady flow of new customers continues.

## Lesson 13.4: The big sale, step by step
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** A big sale is a project with eight parts, from the target to the shipping cut-offs. Planning each part in order is what makes a sale profitable and repeatable.

**Step 1.** Set the target: last year's sales for this event times your growth rate, checked against list size, budget and stock.

**Expected result:** You have a sales target you can resource.

<!-- do:goals -->

**Step 2.** Build the offer: a main offer, free gift levels (spend $100, get a gift), something new mid-sale and a VIP perk, checking margin at every level.

**Expected result:** Every offer level still makes money.

**Step 3.** Pick a theme: a name, a look and a message that stand out from competitors.

**Expected result:** The sale has a name and a visual style.

**Step 4.** Plan the ad budget per day for each phase (hype, launch, middle, finish) and the split between normal and sale campaigns.

**Expected result:** Every sale day has a planned budget.

**Step 5.** List the ads needed for each phase and audience (cold, warm, hot), and plan emails and texts (Lesson 10.13).

**Expected result:** Creative and messages are planned for every phase.

**Step 6.** Prepare the site: sale landing pages and banners, a code freeze 2 weeks before, and every discount code tested.

**Expected result:** The site is ready and stable before launch.

**Step 7.** Check stock, packing staff, gift stock and shipping cut-off dates.

**Expected result:** You can fulfil the sales you plan to make.

<!-- do:bfcm -->

## Lesson 13.5: Hype phase
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** The hype phase builds excitement before the sale starts, so launch day begins with a crowd. Moving budget across slowly protects the campaigns that bring new customers every day.

**Step 1.** Run sign-up ads and teasers for 3 to 5 days before the sale.

**Expected result:** Your early-access list grows each day.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 2.** Track cost per sign-up and the share of landing page visitors who sign up.

**Expected result:** You know what the hype is costing.

**Step 3.** Move budget from normal campaigns to the hype campaign slowly, never cutting normal campaigns to zero.

**Expected result:** Normal campaigns keep learning and bringing new customers.

## Lesson 13.6: Running the sale
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Running the sale is a daily routine: the right campaigns, spending limits, a daily check and a strong finish. Judge the whole event, not single days.

**Step 1.** Set up sale campaigns: a copy of your best cold campaign, a cold campaign with combined audiences and exclusions, a mixed campaign with no exclusions, warm campaigns, and hot campaigns (add to cart and checkout).

**Expected result:** All sale campaigns are built and paused before launch.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 2.** Set spending limits on every sale campaign.

**Expected result:** Nothing can overspend.

**Step 3.** Each day, check MER against plan, stock, site speed and ad fatigue.

**Expected result:** You catch problems the same day.

<!-- do:growth -->

**Step 4.** Mid-sale, add a new offer, fresh ads and an email with a new reason to buy.

**Expected result:** The middle of the sale gets a boost.

**Step 5.** Finish strong with last-chance ads, final emails and texts.

**Expected result:** The last day has its own push.

**Watch out:** Quiet days in the middle are normal. Do not panic and cut spend.

## Lesson 13.7: After the sale
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** After a sale there is always a slow period, and how you handle it decides how many new buyers you keep. A short review makes the next event better.

**Step 1.** Turn normal campaigns back up in steps over a week, not all at once.

**Expected result:** Campaigns recover without a spending shock.

**Step 2.** Check that new buyers are getting your post-purchase emails.

**Expected result:** New customers get thanks and how-to tips.

<!-- do:flow-post-purchase -->

**Step 3.** Review sales against target, MER, new customers and stock left, and write down one thing to improve.

**Expected result:** You have a short review with one improvement for next time.

<!-- do:growth -->

## Lesson 13.8: If the sale is failing
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** When a sale is behind plan, the cause is usually one of five things. Find it fast, then use a rescue move that does not damage trust.

**Step 1.** Check the five causes: an offer weaker than competitors', an offer that is not clear in the first seconds of ads, a site problem, a list that is too small, or bad timing.

**Expected result:** You have named the likely cause.

**Step 2.** Pick one rescue move: add a free gift level, release something new mid-sale, send a stronger email or move budget to warm audiences.

**Expected result:** A rescue move is live within hours.

**Watch out:** Extend the sale only if it will not damage trust. Customers notice fake "last chances".

## Lesson 13.9: Experience levels
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Your sale plan should grow with your experience. Starting simple and adding layers each time keeps sales profitable while you learn.

**Step 1.** Find your level: starter (one offer, simple schedule, sale versions of flows), seasoned (gift levels, hype phase, mid-sale release, separate sale campaigns) or advanced (daily budget plan, every channel planned, product sales targets).

**Expected result:** You know your level.

**Step 2.** Add one element from the next level to your next sale.

**Expected result:** Each sale is a little more sophisticated than the last.

## Lesson 13.10: Key dates and beyond Black Friday
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Plan the whole busy season, not one weekend. For many stores Black Friday and Cyber Monday is the biggest window of the year, and it is won months before November. Helix adds the staged plan to your calendar from 1 August.

**Step 1.** Year-round, test ad angles and offers and grow your list, so you know what works before the busy season.

**Expected result:** You have proven ads and a bigger list by August.

**Step 2.** Follow the Black Friday stages: August review last year, set targets and test; September order stock 10 to 12 weeks out, lock the offer and scale winners; October finalise creative, warm audiences, build flows and load stock; November launch, check daily and scale winners; December and January review and plan next year.

**Expected result:** Every stage is on your calendar with dates.

<!-- do:bfcm -->

**Step 3.** Add gifting campaigns with delivery cut-off dates ("order by the 18th for Christmas").

**Expected result:** Shoppers know the last safe order date.

**Step 4.** Plan for Boxing Day and the quiet weeks after (sometimes called the fifth quarter), when ad costs drop and new customers can be cheaper.

**Expected result:** You have a plan for late December and January.

**Step 5.** Add end of financial year sales where they matter (June in Australia) and Valentine's, Mother's and Father's Day gift selections.

**Expected result:** Your year has every key date planned.

<!-- do:calendar -->

## Lesson 13.11: Cross-channel checklist
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Big sales use every channel at once, and one missed setting can cost a day of sales. Tick this list a week before launch.

**Step 1.** Confirm Meta and Google sale campaigns are built and switched off.

**Expected result:** Campaigns are ready to switch on.

**Step 2.** Confirm sale prices and promotion assets are in your product feed.

**Expected result:** Shopping ads will show sale prices.

**Step 3.** Confirm emails and texts are scheduled and flows switch to sale versions at the right time.

**Expected result:** Messages go out on their own.

**Step 4.** Confirm free posts and creator posts are scheduled.

**Expected result:** Social is ready.

**Step 5.** Test site banners, landing pages and discount rules with a real test order.

**Expected result:** Discounts apply correctly at checkout.

**Step 6.** Give customer service saved replies for common sale questions.

**Expected result:** The team can answer fast during the rush.

## Self-check

1. How many big sales peaks should you plan each year?
2. What do you try before deep discounts to clear stock?
3. Why not cut normal ad spend to zero during the hype phase?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Three to four.
2. A free gift with purchase, bundles, VIP codes or gift cards.
3. Campaigns lose what they have learned and you lose the flow of new customers. Move budget across slowly instead.
