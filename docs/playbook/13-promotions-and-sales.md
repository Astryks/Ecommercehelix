# Module 13: Promotions, launches and big sales

**Outcome:** a year of sales peaks that are planned, make a profit and are easy to repeat. Plus a step-by-step plan for every big sale, from setting the target to the weeks after.

**Related SOP:** [15 Promo calendar and sales](../sops/15-promo-calendar-and-sales.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Convert (turn visits into sales). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** How to plan sales and launches so they make money, not just noise.
>
> **Why it matters:** Busy periods can make your year. Planned sales are far more profitable than last-minute ones.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Mark your big dates for the year.
> 2. For each sale, set a profit target and the discount you can afford.
> 3. Warm up your list and audiences before the sale starts.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Warm audience:** People who know you: they visited, followed or watched your videos.
> - **Margin:** The share of each sale you keep after product cost. A $100 sale with $40 product cost is a 60% margin.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 13.1: Your yearly sales calendar
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** A year of sales works best as a rhythm. Customers learn when to look, and you are never rushing. Four kinds of promotion cover the year. Deep discounts belong only to clearing slow stock, and only as a last resort.

**Step 1.** Pick a regular day and time for new products, like the first Thursday of each month at 7pm. Put the next 6 on your calendar.

**Expected result:** Your release dates are set and repeat monthly.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 2.** Add gift days and cultural moments (Mother's Day, Valentine's Day). Offer a picked selection, not a store-wide discount.

**Expected result:** Each event has a product selection planned.

**Step 3.** Choose 3 to 4 big sales peaks for the year, with Black Friday usually the biggest.

**Expected result:** Your peaks are on the calendar with dates.

<!-- do:bfcm -->

**Step 4.** For slow stock, plan gentle tools first: a free gift with purchase, bundles, VIP codes or gift cards.

**Expected result:** Slow stock has a clearing plan that avoids deep discounts.

<!-- do:stock -->

## Lesson 13.2: Launch phases
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** A launch has four phases, and each needs its own message. Skipping the build-up or the follow-up leaves most of the sales on the table.

**Step 1.** Before launch, tease the product and open a waitlist and early-access sign-up.

**Expected result:** People are signing up before the product is out.

**Step 2.** On launch day, give VIPs (best customers) access first, then everyone.

**Expected result:** VIPs get a head start and feel looked after.

<!-- do:flow-vip -->

**Step 3.** Mid-launch, release something new for 24 hours, like a new colour or a bundle.

**Expected result:** The launch gets a second spike.

**Step 4.** After launch, share reviews, results and restock news.

**Expected result:** Proof keeps sales going after the launch week.

## Lesson 13.3: Launching a new product: how to build the campaigns
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** How you structure a product drop depends on your spend. Either way, your warm audiences and email list should get first access while normal campaigns keep running.

**Step 1.** Choose where launch ads go.

- Spend about $1,000 a day or more (a rough rule of thumb): make a separate launch campaign.
- Spend less: add launch ads to your normal cold and warm campaigns.

**Expected result:** Launch ads have a home that fits your budget.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 2.** Give warm audiences and your email list first access and keep evergreen (always-on) campaigns running.

**Expected result:** Your best audiences hear first and your steady flow of new customers continues.

## Lesson 13.4: The big sale, step by step
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** A big sale is a project with seven parts, from the target to the shipping cut-offs. Planning each part in order is what makes a sale profitable and repeatable.

**Step 1.** Set the target: last year's sales for this event times your growth rate. Check it against list size, budget and stock.

**Expected result:** You have a sales target you can resource.

<!-- do:goals -->

**Step 2.** Build the offer: a main offer and free gift levels (spend $100, get a gift). Add something new mid-sale and a VIP extra. Check your margin at every level.

**Expected result:** Every offer level still makes money.

**Watch out:** A "was" or strike-through price must be one you really charged for a reasonable time just before the sale. Fake "was" prices break the law in Australia and the US (see [ACCC: two-price advertising](https://www.accc.gov.au/publications/advertising-selling/advertising-and-selling-guide/pricing/two-price-comparison-advertising) and [FTC: deceptive pricing guides](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-233)). In the EU, the "was" price must be your lowest price in the 30 days before the sale.

**Step 3.** Pick a theme: a name, a look and a message that stand out from competitors.

**Expected result:** The sale has a name and a visual style.

**Step 4.** Plan the ad budget per day for each part of the sale (hype, launch, middle, finish). Also plan the split between normal and sale campaigns.

**Expected result:** Every sale day has a planned budget.

**Step 5.** List the ads needed for each phase and audience (cold, warm, hot). Plan emails and texts too (Lesson 10.13).

**Expected result:** Creative and messages are planned for every phase.

**Step 6.** Get the site ready: sale pages and banners, and no site changes for 2 weeks before. Test every discount code.

**Expected result:** The site is ready and stable before launch.

**Step 7.** Check stock, packing staff, gift stock and shipping cut-off dates.

**Expected result:** You can fulfil the sales you plan to make.

## Lesson 13.5: The build-up before a launch
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** The build-up builds excitement before the sale starts, so launch day begins with a crowd. Moving budget across slowly protects the campaigns that bring new customers every day.

**Step 1.** Run sign-up ads and teasers for 3 to 5 days before the sale.

**Expected result:** Your early-access list grows each day.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 2.** Track cost per sign-up and the share of landing page visitors who sign up.

**Expected result:** You know what the hype is costing.

**Step 3.** Move budget from normal campaigns to the hype campaign slowly, never cutting normal campaigns to zero.

**Expected result:** Normal campaigns keep learning and bringing new customers.

## Lesson 13.6: Running the sale
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Running the sale is a daily routine. You need the right campaigns, spending limits, a daily check and a strong finish. Judge the whole event, not single days.

**Step 1.** Set up sale campaigns. Copy your best cold campaign. Add a cold campaign with combined audiences and people left out, and a mixed campaign that leaves nobody out. Then add warm campaigns, and hot campaigns for add to cart and checkout.

**Expected result:** All sale campaigns are built and paused before launch.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 2.** Set spending limits on every sale campaign.

**Expected result:** Nothing can overspend.

**Step 3.** Each day, check MER (total ad spend as a share of total sales) against your plan. Also check stock, site speed and tired ads.

**Expected result:** You catch problems the same day.

<!-- do:growth -->

**Step 4.** Mid-sale, add a new offer, fresh ads and an email with a new reason to buy.

**Expected result:** The middle of the sale gets a boost.

**Step 5.** Finish strong with last-chance ads, final emails and texts.

**Expected result:** The last day has its own push.

**Watch out:** Quiet days in the middle are normal. Do not panic and cut spend.

## Lesson 13.7: After the sale
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** After a sale there is always a slow time. How you handle it decides how many new buyers you keep. A short review makes the next event better.

**Step 1.** Turn normal campaigns back up in steps over a week, not all at once.

**Expected result:** Campaigns recover without a spending shock.

**Step 2.** Check that new buyers are getting your post-purchase emails.

**Expected result:** New customers get thanks and how-to tips.

<!-- do:flow-post-purchase -->

**Step 3.** Review sales against target, MER, new customers and stock left, and write down one thing to improve.

**Expected result:** You have a short review with one improvement for next time.

<!-- do:growth -->

## Lesson 13.8: If the sale is failing
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** When a sale is behind plan, the cause is usually one of five things. Find it fast, then use a rescue move that does not damage trust.

**Step 1.** Check the five causes. The offer is weaker than rivals', or not clear in the first seconds of ads. There is a site problem, the list is too small, or the timing is bad.

**Expected result:** You have named the likely cause.

**Step 2.** Pick one rescue move. Add a free gift level, release something new mid-sale, send a stronger email or move budget to warm audiences.

**Expected result:** A rescue move is live within hours.

**Watch out:** Extend the sale only if it will not damage trust. Customers notice fake "last chances". Stores have paid ACCC penalties after their countdown timers reset past the "end" (see [ACCC: Black Friday penalties](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/james-lane-and-vuly-pay-penalties-for-allegedly-making-misleading-representations-during-last-year%E2%80%99s-black-friday-sales)).

## Lesson 13.9: Experience levels
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Your sale plan should grow with your experience. Starting simple and adding layers each time keeps sales profitable while you learn.

**Step 1.** Find your level. **Starter:** one offer, a simple plan, sale versions of flows. **Seasoned:** gift levels, a hype phase, a mid-sale release, separate sale campaigns. **Advanced:** a daily budget plan, every channel planned, sales targets per product.

**Expected result:** You know your level.

**Step 2.** Add one element from the next level to your next sale.

**Expected result:** Each sale is a little more sophisticated than the last.

## Lesson 13.10: Key dates through the year
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Plan the whole busy season, not one weekend. For many stores, Black Friday and Cyber Monday is the biggest time of the year. In 2026, Black Friday is 27 November and Cyber Monday is 30 November. It is won months before November. Helix adds the staged plan to your calendar from 1 August.

**Step 1.** All year, test ad angles and offers and grow your list. Then you know what works before the busy season.

**Expected result:** You have proven ads and a bigger list by August.

**Step 2.** Put the Black Friday stages in your Helix calendar.

- August: review last year. Set targets. Test new ads.
- September: order stock 10 to 12 weeks out. Lock the offer.
- October: finish ads. Warm up audiences. Build email flows.
- November: launch. Check numbers daily.
- December and January: review and plan next year.

**Expected result:** Every stage is on your calendar with dates.

<!-- do:bfcm -->

**Step 3.** Add gifting campaigns with delivery cut-off dates ("order by the 18th for Christmas").

**Expected result:** Shoppers know the last safe order date.

**Step 4.** Plan for Boxing Day and the quiet weeks after. Ad costs often drop then, so new customers can be cheaper.

**Expected result:** You have a plan for late December and January.

**Step 5.** Add end of financial year sales where they matter (30 June in Australia). Add gift picks for Valentine's, Mother's and Father's Day.

**Expected result:** Your year has every key date planned.

<!-- do:calendar -->

## Lesson 13.11: Cross-channel checklist
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Big sales use every channel at once, and one missed setting can cost a day of sales. Tick this list a week before launch.

**Step 1.** Confirm Meta and Google sale campaigns are built and switched off.

**Expected result:** Campaigns are ready to switch on.

**Step 2.** Confirm sale prices and promotion assets are in your product feed.

**Expected result:** Shopping ads will show sale prices.

**Step 3.** Confirm emails and texts are scheduled and flows switch to sale versions at the right time.

**Expected result:** Messages go out on their own.

**Step 4.** Confirm free posts and creator posts are scheduled.

**Expected result:** Social is ready.

**Step 5.** Test site banners, landing pages and discount rules with a real test order.

**Expected result:** Discounts apply correctly at checkout.

**Step 6.** Give customer service saved replies for common sale questions.

**Expected result:** The team can answer fast during the rush.

## Lesson 13.12: A smaller sale between the big ones
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Relying on one big sale a year makes cash flow lumpy and leaves stock sitting for months. One or two smaller planned sales clear old stock and bring back past customers. Good times are the end of the financial year or the change of season. This way buyers do not learn to wait for discounts.

**Step 1.** Pick one window at least 8 weeks away from your biggest sale. Choose the reason: clearing a season's stock, end of financial year, or your brand's birthday.

**Expected result:** You have one date range of 3 to 5 days and a reason customers will believe.

**Step 2.** Choose what goes on sale. Put slow stock and last season's colours first. Give best sellers a smaller discount or none. Check the margin on each with your profit setup.

**Expected result:** A list of products and discounts where every line still makes money after marketing.

**Step 3.** Plan it in Helix Calendar. Send one teaser email 3 days before, then a launch email and SMS. Send a reminder on the middle day and a last-chance email. Add ads to past buyers and site visitors.

**Expected result:** The sale and its messages are on your calendar with dates.

**Step 4.** After it ends, write down how it went.

1. Record sales, profit, units cleared, and new versus returning customers.
2. Write one thing to change next time.

**Expected result:** You know whether the sale made money and whether to repeat it.

**Watch out:** Count big and small sales together. The more often you run them, the more customers wait for the next one. Keep sales planned, rare and with a clear reason.

## Self-check

1. How many big sales peaks should you plan each year?
2. What do you try before deep discounts to clear stock?
3. Why not cut normal ad spend to zero during the build-up?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Three to four.
2. A free gift with purchase, bundles, VIP codes or gift cards.
3. Campaigns lose what they have learned and you lose the flow of new customers. Move budget across slowly instead.
