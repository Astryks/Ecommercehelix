# Module 26: Tax, GST, sales tax and duties

**Outcome:** you know when you must register for GST or sales tax. You know how to keep tax money apart from your money. You know which dates matter. You also know how VAT, duties and tariffs change your prices when you sell or buy overseas.

> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Grow (keep more of every sale, then scale). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** When you must register for GST or sales tax. And how tax and duties change prices across borders.
>
> **Why it matters:** Tax mistakes grow quietly and come back with penalties. Knowing the thresholds early keeps you in control.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Check your GST or state sales against the registration thresholds every quarter.
> 2. Move tax money into its own account every week and lodge on time.
> 3. Add duty, freight and fees to your landed cost and decide who pays import tax for each country.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **GST:** Goods and services tax. It is 10% in Australia (15% in New Zealand), added to most sales once you register.
> - **BAS:** Business activity statement: the form Australian businesses lodge with the ATO to report and pay GST.
> - **Nexus:** In the US, a link with a state, like a warehouse there or enough sales into it. It means you must collect that state's sales tax.
> - **VAT:** Value added tax: the UK and EU version of GST, usually included in the shown price.
> - **IOSS:** An EU scheme for parcels worth up to 150 euros. The seller charges EU VAT at checkout and pays it in one country.
> - **De minimis:** A value below which imported parcels skip duty or tax. The US ended its $800 de minimis for all countries in August 2025.
> - **DDP:** Delivered duty paid: you pay the import taxes so the customer pays nothing extra on delivery.
> - **Duties:** Taxes charged by a country on goods coming in from overseas.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 26.1: GST in Australia: register, charge and lodge
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** GST (goods and services tax) is 10% on most Australian sales once you are registered. The money you collect is held for the ATO (Australian Taxation Office), not earned by you, so set it aside.

**Step 1.** Check your GST turnover (this month plus the last 11). Register within 21 days of reaching $75,000. Or register as soon as you expect to reach it within 12 months (see [ATO: GST](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst)).

**Expected result:** You know whether you must register.

**Step 2.** Once registered, show prices to Australians with GST included and issue tax invoices.

**Expected result:** Checkout prices include GST.

**Step 3.** Open a separate bank account and each week move one eleventh of GST-inclusive sales into it.

**Expected result:** The GST bill is already saved when it is due.

**Step 4.** Send in your BAS (business activity statement) on time. Quarterly due dates are 28 October, 28 February, 28 April and 28 July (see [ATO: BAS due dates](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/due-dates-for-lodging-and-paying-your-bas)).

**Expected result:** Dates are on your calendar. Lodging online may add two weeks, except for the December quarter.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 5.** Claim GST credits on business costs that include GST, like local stock and services. Keep the tax invoices.

**Expected result:** Your BAS includes your credits.

**Step 6.** In Helix **Settings**, keep **My prices include GST/VAT** on.

**Expected result:** Helix profit leaves GST out.

**Watch out:** New Zealand: exports are usually GST-free. But small sales (NZ$1,000 or less) to NZ shoppers may pass NZ$60,000 in 12 months. Then you must register for NZ GST at 15% (see [IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/gst-for-overseas-businesses/gst-on-low-value-imported-goods/registering-for-gst-on-low-value-imported-goods)). US sellers into Australia must register once sales to Australian consumers pass A$75,000 (see [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/gst-for-non-resident-businesses)).

## Lesson 26.2: GST on what you buy: ads, apps and imports
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Many costs come from overseas: ads, apps, software and stock. GST works differently on each, and getting it right stops you paying tax you could avoid or claim back.

**Step 1.** If you are registered, give every overseas service and app supplier your ABN (ads, Shopify apps, software). Tell them you are GST registered (see [ATO: imported services](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/rules-for-specific-transactions/international-transactions/australian-business-importing-goods-and-services)).

**Expected result:** Their invoices no longer include GST.

**Step 2.** If a supplier charged GST wrongly, ask them for a refund.

**Expected result:** The wrong GST comes back. You generally do not owe reverse-charge GST on fully business purchases.

**Step 3.** If you are not registered, do not claim to be; pay GST like any consumer.

**Expected result:** Your supplier records are correct.

**Step 4.** For stock imports over A$1,000, pay GST and any duty at the border. Do it through your broker or shipping agent. Keep the paperwork, and claim that GST back on your BAS (the Australian tax form for GST).

**Expected result:** Import GST appears as a credit on your BAS.

**Step 5.** Record stock cost without GST in your books and in Helix.

**Expected result:** Your cost of goods is ex GST.

**Watch out:** US: there is no GST. Give suppliers a resale certificate, so you do not pay sales tax on stock you resell. Check your state's use tax rules too.

## Lesson 26.3: US sales tax: nexus, registering and filing
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** The US has no national sales tax. Each state sets its own rules. You only collect a state's tax once you have nexus there (a strong enough link to that state).

**Step 1.** List states where you have physical nexus. That is where you live, have staff or keep stock, including in a hired warehouse.

**Expected result:** You have a list of physical nexus states.

**Step 2.** Check each state's sales limit for economic nexus, often $100,000 a year. California and Texas use $500,000. New York uses over $500,000 plus over 100 sales (see [California CDTFA](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair/) and [Streamlined Sales Tax](https://www.streamlinedsalestax.org/)).

**Expected result:** You know each state's threshold.

**Step 3.** Open Shopify **Settings > Taxes and duties**. Watch how close you are to each limit (see [Shopify: US taxes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/taxes/us)).

**Expected result:** You see thresholds before you cross them.

**Step 4.** Register with a state before collecting its tax, then turn that state on in Shopify.

**Expected result:** Shopify collects tax only where you are registered.

**Step 5.** File and pay on the state's schedule (monthly, quarterly or yearly), including nil returns if asked.

**Expected result:** Every filing is on time.

**Watch out:** Marketplaces like Amazon usually collect for you on their sales. Your own store is yours to handle. Non-US sellers face the same thresholds even without a US company or warehouse.

## Lesson 26.4: Selling to the UK, EU and Canada
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Selling abroad means charging the customer's tax or leaving them to pay it on delivery. Surprise fees at the door cause refused parcels and angry reviews.

**Step 1.** Decide who pays import tax and duty. Either you pay at checkout (DDP, delivered duty paid), or the customer pays on delivery (DAP, delivered at place).

**Expected result:** You have one rule per country. DDP means more paperwork but smoother delivery.

**Step 2.** UK: for parcels of £135 or less, register for UK VAT. Charge it at checkout and pay HMRC (see [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-and-overseas-goods-sold-directly-to-customers-in-the-uk)).

**Expected result:** UK VAT is collected at checkout.

**Step 3.** EU: for parcels up to 150 euros, use IOSS (import one stop shop). You charge EU VAT at checkout and file in one country. Usually you use an EU agent (see [EU VAT One Stop Shop](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en)).

**Expected result:** EU VAT is collected at checkout.

**Step 4.** Canada: if taxable sales of goods to Canada pass C$30,000 in 12 months, register for GST/HST (see [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/find-out-need-register.html)).

**Expected result:** You know whether you must register in Canada.

**Step 5.** Use Shopify Markets to show local prices and collect duties at checkout where available.

**Expected result:** Shoppers see a full local price.

**Step 6.** Recalculate profit per country with tax, duty and shipping (see [Lesson 15.2](15-international-expansion.md#lesson-152-recalculate-unit-economics-per-country)).

**Expected result:** You know which countries make money.

**Watch out:** Goods made in Australia may pay less UK duty under the Australia-UK trade deal. Goods made in the US may pay less Canada duty under the US-Mexico-Canada deal, if the origin rules are met.

## Lesson 26.5: Duties, tariffs and the end of US de minimis
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Duties are taxes on imported goods. They depend on the tariff code (what the product is), where it was made and its value. They change your landed cost: product plus freight, duty and fees.

**Step 1.** Find the HS code (harmonised system tariff code) for each product with your forwarder or broker.

**Expected result:** Every product has a code. A wrong code means a wrong duty.

**Step 2.** Record the country of origin (where it was made, not where it ships from) for each product.

**Expected result:** Every product has an origin.

**Step 3.** Add duty, freight and broker fees to landed cost in Helix [Suppliers and stock](/dashboard/stock).

**Expected result:** Product margins include duty.

<!-- do:stock -->

**Step 4.** When rates change, decide per product.

1. Work out the new margin for each product.
2. For each one, pick one fix from this list.

- A higher price.
- Cheaper shipping.
- A new place to make it.
- A lower margin for now.

**Expected result:** You have a decision for each affected product.

**Step 5.** Keep import paperwork as long as your tax records.

**Expected result:** Paperwork is filed.

**Watch out:** US: since 29 August 2025, the $800 de minimis rule is gone, so small parcels pay duty. Compare duty per parcel with shipping in bulk to a US warehouse (see [CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/e-commerce/faqs)). Australia: imports over A$1,000 need a customs form. Trade deals can cut duty with a certificate of origin (see [ABF](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/importing)).

### Good to know

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent, customs broker or accountant.
