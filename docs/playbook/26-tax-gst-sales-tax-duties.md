# Module 26: Tax, GST, sales tax and duties

**Outcome:** you know when you must register for GST or sales tax, how to keep tax money separate from your money, which dates matter, and how VAT, duties and tariffs change your prices when you sell or buy across borders.

**Related:** [Module 22 Shopify store setup](22-shopify-store-setup.md), [Module 15 International expansion](15-international-expansion.md), [Module 27 Accounting, bookkeeping and tax time](27-accounting-bookkeeping-tax-time.md), [Module 14 Inventory, cash and the year plan](14-inventory-cash-planning.md)

> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Grow (keep more of every sale, then scale). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** When you must register for GST or sales tax, and how tax and duties change prices across borders.
>
> **Why it matters:** Tax mistakes grow quietly and come back with penalties. Knowing the thresholds early keeps you in control.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Check your GST or state sales against the registration thresholds every quarter.
> 2. Move tax money into its own account every week and lodge on time.
> 3. Add duty, freight and fees to your landed cost and decide who pays import tax for each country.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **GST:** Goods and services tax: 10% in Australia (15% in New Zealand), added to most sales once you are registered.
> - **BAS:** Business activity statement: the form Australian businesses lodge with the ATO to report and pay GST.
> - **Nexus:** In the US, a link with a state (like a warehouse there, or enough sales into it) that means you must collect its sales tax.
> - **VAT:** Value added tax: the UK and EU version of GST, usually included in the shown price.
> - **IOSS:** The EU scheme that lets a seller charge EU VAT at checkout on parcels worth up to 150 euros and pay it in one country.
> - **De minimis:** A value below which imported parcels skip duty or tax. The US ended its $800 de minimis for all countries in August 2025.
> - **DDP:** Delivered duty paid: you pay the import taxes so the customer pays nothing extra on delivery.
> - **Duties:** Taxes charged by a country on goods coming in from overseas.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 26.1: GST in Australia: register, charge and lodge
<!-- stage:grow -->

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

GST is 10% on most sales in Australia once you are registered. The money you collect is held for the ATO, not earned by you.

**Do this:**

1. Check the threshold. You must register within 21 days of your GST turnover reaching $75,000 (this month plus the previous 11 months), or as soon as you expect to reach it in the next 12 months. See [ATO: registering for GST](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst).
2. Once registered, show prices to Australian shoppers with GST included and issue tax invoices.
3. Open a separate bank account and move the GST part of each payout there every week (one eleventh of GST-inclusive sales). Then the bill is never a surprise.
4. Lodge your BAS on time. For quarterly reporters the due dates are 28 October, 28 February, 28 April and 28 July. Lodging online may give you an extra two weeks, except for the December quarter, which already has extra time. See [ATO: BAS due dates](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/due-dates-for-lodging-and-paying-your-bas).
5. Claim GST credits on business costs that include GST, such as stock bought in Australia and local services. Keep the tax invoices.
6. In Helix, keep "My prices include GST/VAT" on (Settings) so your profit leaves GST out.

**New Zealand customers:** goods you send overseas are usually GST-free for you when exported within the ATO's time limit. If your sales of goods worth NZ$1,000 or less to New Zealand consumers pass NZ$60,000 in 12 months, you must register for New Zealand GST (15%) and charge it at checkout. See [IRD: GST on low-value imported goods](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/gst-for-overseas-businesses/gst-on-low-value-imported-goods/registering-for-gst-on-low-value-imported-goods).

**US sellers into Australia:** overseas sellers whose sales to Australian consumers pass A$75,000 a year must register and charge GST on low-value goods (A$1,000 or less). See [ATO: GST for non-resident businesses](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/gst-for-non-resident-businesses).

**Self-check:** do you know your GST turnover for the last 12 months? Is GST money moved to its own account each week?

## Lesson 26.2: GST on what you buy: ads, apps and imports
<!-- stage:grow -->

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

Many of your costs come from overseas: ads, apps, software and stock. GST works differently on each.

**Do this:**

1. Overseas services and apps (ads, Shopify apps, software): if you are registered, give the supplier your ABN and say you are registered for GST. They should then not charge GST. See [ATO: imported services and digital products](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/rules-for-specific-transactions/international-transactions/australian-business-importing-goods-and-services).
2. If a supplier charged GST wrongly, ask them for a refund. You generally do not have to pay reverse-charge GST on these purchases if they are fully for your business.
3. If you are not registered, do not give your ABN as GST-registered. You pay the GST like any consumer.
4. Stock imports over A$1,000: you usually pay GST (and any duty) at the border through your customs broker or freight forwarder, and claim that GST back on your BAS. Keep the import paperwork.
5. Record stock cost without GST in your books and in Helix, because you claim the GST back.

**US:** there is no GST. You generally pay sales or use tax on things you buy for your own use, but not on stock you buy to resell when you give the supplier a resale certificate. Check your state's rules.

**Self-check:** has every overseas supplier got your ABN? Are your import GST amounts on your BAS?

## Lesson 26.3: US sales tax: nexus, registering and filing
<!-- stage:grow -->

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

There is no national sales tax in the US. Each state sets its own rules, and you collect a state's tax only once you have nexus there (a strong enough link).

**Do this:**

1. Physical nexus: you usually have it where you live, have staff, or store stock. Stock in a third-party warehouse counts.
2. Economic nexus: most states set a sales threshold, often $100,000 a year into that state. Some are higher: California and Texas use $500,000, and New York uses more than $500,000 plus more than 100 sales. Some states also count the number of orders. See [California CDTFA](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair/) and the [Streamlined Sales Tax project](https://www.streamlinedsalestax.org/).
3. Watch your numbers. Shopify shows how close you are to each state's threshold. See [Shopify: US taxes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/taxes/us).
4. Register with a state before you start collecting its tax. Then turn that state on in Shopify.
5. File and pay on the schedule the state gives you (monthly, quarterly or yearly). File even when you owe nothing, if the state asks.
6. Marketplaces such as Amazon usually collect sales tax for you on their sales. Your own store's sales are still yours to handle.

**Australia and other non-US sellers:** the same thresholds apply to you. Once you pass a state's threshold, you have to register there even with no US company or warehouse.

**Self-check:** do you know which states you have nexus in? Have you registered before collecting?

## Lesson 26.4: Selling to the UK, EU and Canada
<!-- stage:grow -->

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

Selling abroad means charging the customer's tax, or leaving them to pay it on delivery. Surprise fees at the door cause refused parcels and angry reviews.

**Do this:**

1. Decide who pays import tax and duty: you at checkout (DDP, delivered duty paid) or the customer on delivery (DAP). DDP costs you more paperwork but gives a smoother delivery.
2. **UK:** for parcels worth £135 or less sent from overseas, the seller must register for UK VAT, charge it at checkout and pay it to HMRC. Above £135, import VAT and duty are charged at the border. See [GOV.UK: VAT on goods sold to UK customers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-and-overseas-goods-sold-directly-to-customers-in-the-uk).
3. **EU:** for parcels worth up to 150 euros, the IOSS scheme lets you charge EU VAT at checkout and file in one country. Most sellers outside the EU need an EU-based intermediary. See [EU: VAT One Stop Shop](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en).
4. **Canada:** a seller outside Canada whose taxable sales of goods to Canada pass C$30,000 in 12 months generally must register for GST/HST. See [Canada Revenue Agency: digital economy GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/find-out-need-register.html).
5. Use Shopify Markets to show local prices and, where available, collect duties at checkout.
6. Recalculate profit per country with tax, duty and shipping included. See [Lesson 15.2](15-international-expansion.md#lesson-152-recalculate-unit-economics-per-country).

**Australia:** UK sales may qualify for lower or no duty under the Australia-UK free trade agreement if your goods meet the origin rules. Ask your freight forwarder.

**US:** the same UK, EU and Canada rules apply to US sellers. Duty on US-made goods into Canada can be lower under the US-Mexico-Canada agreement if the goods qualify.

**Self-check:** for each country you sell to, do you know who pays import tax and whether you must register?

## Lesson 26.5: Duties, tariffs and the end of US de minimis
<!-- stage:grow -->

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent, customs broker or accountant.

Duties are taxes on imported goods. They depend on what the product is (its tariff code), where it was made and its value.

**Do this:**

1. Find the tariff code (HS code) for each product. Your freight forwarder or customs broker can help. A wrong code means a wrong duty.
2. Record country of origin for each product. It is where the goods were made, not where they ship from.
3. Add duty, freight and broker fees to your landed cost in Helix ([Suppliers and stock](/dashboard/stock)).
4. When rates change, recheck your margin per product. Then choose: raise prices, change how you ship, change where you make, or accept a lower margin for a time.
5. Keep import paperwork for at least as long as your tax records.

**US:** since 29 August 2025, the $800 de minimis rule no longer applies to any country, so low-value parcels into the US pay duty. Tariff rates have changed many times since 2025, so always check the current rate. If your customers are in the US and your stock ships from overseas, compare sending each parcel (and paying duty on each) with shipping in bulk to a US warehouse. See [US Customs: e-commerce FAQs](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/e-commerce/faqs).

**Australia:** imports over A$1,000 need a customs declaration and may pay duty plus GST. Free trade agreements (for example with China) can reduce duty when your supplier provides a certificate of origin. See [Australian Border Force: importing](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/importing).

**Self-check:** do you know the tariff code and country of origin of your top five products? Does your landed cost include duty?
