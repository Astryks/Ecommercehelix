# Module 19: Creators, UGC and video ads

**Outcome:** you can find and sign creators, run partnership (whitelisted) ads, build an affiliate programme that does not clash with your own offers, and make video ads that stop the scroll: hooks, scripts, formats, shooting and editing, briefed so creators deliver what you need first time.

**Related:** [Module 5 Creative strategy](05-creative-strategy.md), [Module 6 Defining campaigns](06-defining-campaigns.md), [SOP 08 Creative briefs, UGC and creators](../sops/08-creative-briefs-ugc-creators.md), [SOP 07 Creative testing](../sops/07-creative-testing.md)

## Lesson 19.1: Why other people's faces sell your product

People trust people more than brands. When a stranger who looks like your customer explains why they switched, it lands differently to the brand saying "we love our product". Creator content also brings variety: different ages, styles, settings and reasons to buy, which is exactly the creative diversity ad platforms need to find new pockets of buyers.

Three ways to work with creators:

| Model | What you give | What you get | Best when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gifting | Product | Organic post (not guaranteed), sometimes raw footage | Early stage, low budget, testing fit |
| Paid UGC | Fee per video (plus product) | Raw videos you own for ads; no posting required | You need ad creative at volume |
| Influencer or partnership | Fee and/or commission | Posts to their audience, and permission to run ads from their handle | They have an engaged audience that matches yours |
| Affiliate | Commission per sale, discount code | Ongoing posts, often lots of small creators | Repeatable products, good margins |

Most stores end up mixing all four. Start with gifting and paid UGC, keep the people whose content performs, then promote them to paid partners.

## Lesson 19.2: Finding the right creators

Look for fit before follower count. A creator with 4,000 engaged followers who already talks about your category beats a 400,000-follower lifestyle account.

Where to look:

1. **Your own customers and tagged posts.** Search tags and mentions. People who already bought are the easiest yes.
2. **Manual search on the platform.** Search the problems your product solves, not your product name. Save creators whose videos you would actually stop for.
3. **Platform creator marketplaces.** Meta and TikTok have creator search tools that filter by audience location, size and category.
4. **Competitor tags and comments.** Who already makes content for similar brands?
5. **UGC marketplaces and agencies** for paid-only content when you need speed.

Score each creator out of 10 on: on-camera comfort, speaks to your customer, production quality fits the platform (native, not glossy), comments show a real community, posts consistently, values fit. Shortlist 7+.

## Lesson 19.3: Outreach and onboarding

**First message (adapt it, keep it personal):**

> Hi [name], I loved your video about [specific thing]. We make [product], built for people who [problem]. I think your audience would get a lot from it. Could I send you one to try, no strings attached? If you like it, we would love to talk about a paid collaboration and our affiliate programme.

Rules that save time:

- Mention a specific video. Generic outreach gets ignored.
- Offer product first, money after the content resonates. Then increase: more product plus a paid fee for the next video.
- Put the affiliate offer in the first or second message so they know there is ongoing upside.
- Send a one-page brand overview: what you stand for, who you serve, what makes you different, three proof points.
- Track everyone in a simple sheet: name, handle, platform, status (contacted, sent, posted, paid, partner), content links, results.
- Allow lead time. Creator content often takes 4 to 8 weeks to arrive. Brief for peak season in September, not November.
- Want them to keep featuring you? Make sure you have stock for months, not weeks, and keep sending new drops.

## Lesson 19.4: Deals, rates and usage rights

| Term | What to agree | Typical starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliverables | Number of videos, length, raw files, hooks | 1 video plus 3 alternative hooks |
| Usage rights | Where and how long you can run the content as ads | 3 to 12 months paid usage |
| Exclusivity | No competitor posts for a period | Only if you pay for it |
| Partnership ad access | Permission to run ads from their handle | Agreed in writing, time-boxed |
| Payment | Fee, product, commission, or a mix | Small creators often start from product plus a modest fee |
| Revisions | How many rounds | 1 round |
| Disclosure | Paid partnership label or #ad | Always required |

Pay more for: raw footage, multiple hooks, longer usage, exclusivity and whitelisting access. Get everything in writing, even a short email confirmation.

## Lesson 19.5: Affiliate programmes that do not cannibalise you

A common example structure: **10% off for the creator's audience and 15% commission on new-customer orders over $100.** Adjust to your margins using your break-even CPA (Module 1): total discount plus commission must stay well under it.

Avoid the offer clash. If your site pop-up gives 15% off and the creator code gives 10%, shoppers use your code and the creator earns nothing. Creators notice and stop posting. Either match the creator code to your best public offer or give creators something exclusive (a bundle, a gift, free express shipping).

Give affiliates a content guide:

- product facts and the three main problems it solves
- angles and hooks that already work in your ads
- do and don't list (claims you cannot make)
- examples of great affiliate videos
- how and when they get paid

Recruit in batches. Ten small affiliates posting twice a month beats one big name posting once.

## Lesson 19.6: Partnership ads (whitelisting)

Partnership ads run paid ads from a creator's handle (or a founder or niche page) instead of your brand page. The audience sees a familiar person, not an advert, so they stop scrolling and trust more. Meta has reported lower acquisition cost and higher click-through for partnership ads that sit inside normal sales campaigns. Treat platform figures as a reason to test, not a promise.

How to run them:

1. Ask permission in writing and agree the time period.
2. The creator connects your ad account through the platform's partnership tools.
3. Use their existing posts that already performed organically, or new content made for ads.
4. Add them to your normal cold campaign as a new batch. Do not create a separate campaign just for creators.
5. Make sure the brand is clear in the first five seconds, so the story does not drift and lose the product.
6. Judge them on the same CPA and hook rate rules as every other ad (Module 6, Lesson 6.12).

Ideas that work: a creator explaining why your product costs what it does; a niche expert's "the one item I would keep"; a founder page telling the story behind a feature.

## Lesson 19.7: Video ad formats

Pick formats that fit the job and the awareness stage (Module 5, Lesson 5.5).

| Format | What it looks like | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talking head testimonial | Creator to camera: problem, switch, result | Cold, problem-aware |
| Problem then solution | Show the pain, then the product fixing it | Cold |
| Demo or how-to | Hands and product, step by step | Solution-aware, complex products |
| Unboxing | First impressions, what's in the box | Gifting, premium feel |
| Before and after | Visual change (follow platform policy) | Visible results |
| Comparison | Us vs the usual option, side by side | Competitive categories |
| Listicle | "4 reasons I switched" | Explaining several benefits |
| Founder story | Why we made it | Trust, launches, premium price |
| Myth busting | "You have been told X. Here's the truth" | Educated buyers |
| Street or customer interview | Real reactions | Social proof |
| Day in the life | Product inside a routine | Lifestyle products |
| Reaction or stitch | Responding to a comment or one-star review | Objection handling |
| Static and carousel | Image with bold claim, review or offer | Warm audiences, sales, catalogue |

Keep a spread. A healthy account has several formats live at once, not ten versions of one talking head.

## Lesson 19.8: Hooks that win the first three seconds

Every video has two hooks working together:

- **Visual hook:** what you see in frame one (movement, an odd object, a result, a face mid-reaction, bold on-screen text).
- **Verbal hook:** the first sentence spoken.

They do not need to say the same thing. A strong verbal hook can pack in a lot without feeling heavy, for example a dad saying he packs a full week of lunches every Sunday in 40 minutes.

Hook types to rotate:

- **Bold statement:** a confident claim you can back up.
- **Call out:** "If you have [problem], stop scrolling."
- **Curiosity gap:** "Nobody tells you this about [category]."
- **Gossip:** "I wasn't supposed to share this..."
- **Myth or friction:** "Most [products] are mostly filler. Here is what is actually inside."
- **One-star rebuttal:** read a bad review (yours or the category's) and answer it.
- **Price question:** "Why is this $120 when the supermarket one is $20?"
- **Result first:** show the outcome, then explain.
- **Opinion:** "Hot take: [common habit] is a waste of money."
- **Statistic:** a surprising, true number about the problem.

Mine your hooks from customer reviews, support emails, and competitors' one- and two-star reviews. They tell you what people hate, and what you do differently.

Measure hooks with **hook rate** (3-second views ÷ impressions). Under 20% means the opening is not working. Then look at **hold rate** (views to 50% or ThruPlays ÷ 3-second views) to judge the body.

## Lesson 19.9: Scripting

A simple structure for 20 to 45 seconds:

| Part | Seconds | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0 to 3 | Stop the scroll |
| Problem or context | 3 to 8 | "This is me" moment |
| Product and proof | 8 to 25 | How it works, why it's different, evidence |
| Objection handling | 25 to 35 | Price, quality, fit, delivery |
| Call to action | last 3 to 5 | What to do and why now |

Script frames to rotate (examples are made up):

- **Bold statement and proof:** one bold claim about one product, then a case study, stat or review that backs it.
- **Four reasons:** "Four reasons I swapped my old water bottle."
- **Myth and friction:** name a common belief, explain why it's wrong, show your product as the fix.
- **Gossip and problem-solution:** a casual, insider tone that reveals a problem and the fix.
- **Same product, different problem:** one product, three personas, three reasons to buy. A planner for students, parents and freelancers.
- **Why now:** connect the product to a moment: summer, back to school, a launch, a gift date.
- **Personify the product:** let the product "speak" or be the character.
- **Own the competition:** "We are not the only ones making this, but here is why ours is better."

**Calls to action that convert:** be specific ("Tap shop now and grab the starter kit"), give the reason ("so it arrives before Friday"), and match the offer on the landing page. Lots of views but few sales usually means a weak or missing CTA, or a hook that attracts the wrong people.

Length: short and sharp still works, but 30 to 60 second videos with real explanation or storytelling often convert well when the product needs understanding. Test both.

## Lesson 19.10: Shooting on a phone

- Natural light facing a window; avoid overhead light only.
- Film vertical 9:16 at 1080p or higher, back camera when possible.
- Clean audio matters more than video. Use a small clip-on mic or film in a quiet, soft room.
- Shoot several takes of the hook. The hook is where testing happens.
- Capture B-roll: product close-ups, in-use shots, packaging, hands, results, settings.
- Keep it native: real homes and real people beat studio polish on social feeds.

## Lesson 19.11: Editing for performance

- Cut every one to two seconds in the opening; remove pauses and filler.
- Burned-in captions (most people watch muted). Keep text within platform safe zones.
- Bold on-screen hook text in the first frame.
- Layer B-roll over talking head to show what is being said.
- Add music under voice at low volume; check licence rules for ads.
- Export versions: 9:16 for Reels, Stories and TikTok; 4:5 for feed; 1:1 when needed.
- Make 3 to 5 hook variants of each winning body. Change only the first three seconds.
- AI editing tools can resize, caption, translate and create variants fast. Label synthetic people or voices where platforms require it, and never fake reviews or results.
- Before launching, review any platform auto-enhancements so they do not change your message.

## Lesson 19.12: The UGC brief

Send this to every creator. A clear brief is the difference between usable content and wasted product.

```
Brand: ______  Product: ______  Link: ______
Who we are (2 lines): ______

Format: [talking head | demo | unboxing | listicle | other]
Hooks to film (film each separately):
  1) ______
  2) ______
  3) ______
Script outline: hook / problem / product + proof / objection / CTA
Call to action: ______  Offer or code: ______

```

## Lesson 19.13: Building a creative engine

- Target 4 to 8 new ads a week at small to mid spend, more as you scale.
- Keep a hook bank and a winners library tagged by angle, format, persona and result.
- Every Monday: review last week's results, pick the winning concept, brief iterations (new hooks, new creators, new formats on the same message).
- Quality over quantity: one well-briefed concept with five hooks beats twenty random videos.
- Skin popular formats: take a structure that is working on social right now and apply it to your product with your own story. Copy the format, never the content.
- Speak to subcultures: niche groups (runners, new parents, gamers, nurses) respond to content made for them.

## Self-check

1. A creator's code gives 10% off but your pop-up gives 15%. What happens and how do you fix it?
2. What are the two hooks in every video?
3. Your video gets lots of views but few sales. Name two likely causes.
4. When should you pay a creator a fee instead of only gifting?
5. Where do partnership ads belong in your account structure?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Shoppers use the bigger public code, the creator earns nothing and stops posting. Match the offers or give creators an exclusive perk.
2. The visual hook and the verbal hook.
3. A weak or missing call to action, or a hook that attracts people who are not buyers. Also check the landing page matches the ad.
4. Once their content has tested well, or when you need raw footage, hooks and usage rights on a deadline.
5. Inside your normal sales campaigns as a creative batch, judged on the same rules as other ads.
