# Module 19: Creators, UGC and video ads

**Outcome:** you can find and sign creators and run partnership ads (ads from a creator's own account). You can build an affiliate program that does not clash with your own offers. You can also make video ads that stop the scroll. You will learn to write hooks and scripts, pick formats, film and edit. You will also brief creators so they deliver what you need the first time.

**Related:** [Module 5 Creative strategy](05-creative-strategy.md), [Module 6 Defining campaigns](06-defining-campaigns.md), [SOP 08 Creative briefs, UGC and creators](../sops/08-creative-briefs-ugc-creators.md), [SOP 07 Creative testing](../sops/07-creative-testing.md)

> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Attract (get seen by people who want what you sell). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** How to work with creators and make video ads people watch.
>
> **Why it matters:** Real people showing your product often sell better than polished brand ads.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Find 3 small creators who already like your kind of product.
> 2. Send a short brief: the problem, the product, the opening line ideas.
> 3. Run their best video as a partnership ad and compare cost per sale.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Creator:** A person who makes social content and can feature your product.
> - **UGC:** Photos and videos made by real customers or creators instead of the brand.
> - **Partnership ad:** An ad that runs from a creator's own account. People see their name instead of yours.
> - **Hook:** The first line or first 3 seconds of an ad. Its only job is to make people stop scrolling.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 19.1: Why other people's faces sell your product
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** People trust people more than brands. When someone like your customer explains why they switched, it feels more real than a brand praising itself. It also gives ad platforms the mix of ages, styles and reasons they need to find new buyers. UGC (user-generated content) means photos and videos made by customers or creators instead of the brand.

**Step 1.** Read the four ways to work with creators in the table below. They are gifting, paid UGC, influencer or partnership, and affiliate.

**Expected result:** You know what each costs and what you get.

**Step 2.** Start with gifting and paid UGC. Send product to 10 creators and pay 2 or 3 for videos ready for ads.

**Expected result:** You have your first creator videos to test.

**Step 3.** Keep working with the creators whose videos perform, and move the best ones up to paid partners.

**Expected result:** Your creator roster grows from proven performers.

### Good to know

**Four ways to work with creators:**

| Way | What you give | What you get | Best when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gifting | Free product | Maybe a post (not promised), sometimes raw video | You are early, the budget is low, or you are testing if they fit |
| Paid UGC | A fee per video (plus product) | Raw videos you own and can use in ads. They do not have to post. | You need lots of ad videos |
| Influencer or partnership | A fee, commission, or both | Posts to their audience, plus permission to run ads from their account | Their audience is engaged and matches your customers |
| Affiliate | Commission on each sale, and a discount code | Regular posts, often from many small creators | Products people buy again, with good margins |

## Lesson 19.2: Finding the right creators
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Fit matters more than follower count. A creator with 4,000 engaged followers who already talks about your kind of product beats a 400,000-follower lifestyle account.

**Step 1.** Search your own tags and mentions on Instagram and TikTok for customers who already post about you.

**Expected result:** You have a list of warm creator leads.

**Step 2.** Search the platforms for the problems your product solves, not your product name. Save creators you would stop to watch.

**Expected result:** Your list grows with problem-focused creators.

**Step 3.** Use Meta and TikTok creator marketplaces. Filter by audience location, size and category. Check who makes content for rivals.

**Expected result:** You have 20 or more candidates.

**Step 4.** Score each one out of 10. Look at comfort on camera and speaking to your customer. Check natural style, a real community in comments, regular posting and shared values.

**Expected result:** Each creator has a score.

**Step 5.** Shortlist creators who score 7 or more.

**Expected result:** You have a focused shortlist to contact.

**Watch out:** For paid-only videos at speed, UGC marketplaces and agencies are a good backup.

## Lesson 19.3: Outreach and onboarding
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Good outreach is personal, offers product first and makes the ongoing money clear early. Creator videos often take 4 to 8 weeks to arrive, so start early.

**Step 1.** Send a personal first message that mentions a specific video of theirs (template below).

**Expected result:** Each message names something they made.

**Step 2.** Offer product first, and money once their content works. Mention the affiliate offer in the first or second message.

**Expected result:** Creators know there is ongoing money in it.

**Step 3.** Send a one-page brand overview. Say what you stand for, who you serve, what makes you different, and give three proofs.

**Expected result:** Every creator has the same clear brand summary.

**Step 4.** Track everyone in a sheet: name, account, platform, status (contacted, sent, posted, paid, partner), content links and results.

**Expected result:** You can see the whole pipeline at a glance.

**Step 5.** Brief creators for the busy season in September, not November. Keep stock and new products flowing to your best partners for months.

**Expected result:** Creator content arrives in time for peak.

<!-- do:bfcm -->

### Good to know

> Hi [name], I loved your video about [specific thing]. We make [product], built for people who [problem]. I think your audience would get a lot from it. Could I send you one to try, no strings attached? If you like it, we would love to talk about a paid collaboration and our affiliate program.

## Lesson 19.4: Deals, rates and usage rights
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Clear terms prevent disputes and let you use content the way you planned. Seven terms cover every creator deal.

**Step 1.** For each creator, agree the seven terms in the table below. They are deliverables, usage rights, exclusivity, partnership ad access, payment, changes and disclosure.

**Expected result:** Every term has an agreed answer.

**Step 2.** Pay more for raw footage, several hooks, longer usage, exclusivity and partnership ad access.

**Expected result:** Higher fees buy the extras you need.

**Step 3.** Confirm the deal in writing, even if only a short email.

**Expected result:** You have written terms for every creator.

### Good to know

| Term | What to agree | A typical starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What they deliver | Number of videos, length, raw files, hooks | 1 video plus 3 different hooks |
| Usage rights | Where and for how long you can run their video as an ad | 3 to 12 months of paid use |
| Exclusivity | No posts for competitors for a set time | Only if you pay extra for it |
| Partnership ad access | Permission to run ads from their account | Agreed in writing, for a set time |
| Payment | A fee, product, commission, or a mix | Small creators often start with product plus a modest fee |
| Changes | How many rounds of edits | 1 round |
| Disclosure | A "Paid partnership" label or #ad | Always required by law |

## Lesson 19.5: Affiliate programmes that do not cannibalise you
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** An affiliate earns a commission on each sale they bring. Cannibalising means your own offers steal the sale from them, and when that happens creators stop posting.

**Step 1.** Set up a sample offer. For example: 10% off for the creator's audience, and 15% commission on orders over $100 from new customers.

**Expected result:** The offer is live in your affiliate app.

**Step 2.** Check the discount plus commission stays well under your break-even CPA (Module 1).

**Expected result:** Every affiliate sale still makes money.

<!-- do:metrics -->

**Step 3.** Fix offer clashes. If your pop-up gives 15% and their code gives 10%, match the creator code to your best public offer. Or give creators something only they have, like a bundle, a gift or free express shipping.

**Expected result:** Creator codes are always the best deal for their audience.

**Step 4.** Give affiliates a content guide. Include product facts and the top three problems it solves, proven angles and hooks, and a do and don't list. Add sample videos, and how and when they get paid.

**Expected result:** Affiliates can post without asking you questions.

**Step 5.** Recruit in batches of 10 or more.

**Expected result:** Ten small affiliates posting twice a month beat one big name posting once.

## Lesson 19.6: Partnership ads (whitelisting)
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Partnership ads (also called whitelisting) are paid ads that run from a creator's account. They do not run from your brand page. People see a familiar person, not an ad. Meta reports a lower cost per customer when these ads sit inside normal sales campaigns. Treat that as a reason to test, not a promise.

**Step 1.** Ask the creator's permission in writing and agree how long it lasts.

**Expected result:** You have written permission with an end date.

**Step 2.** Ask the creator to connect your ad account in Meta's partnership ad settings. On Instagram that is **Settings > Business tools > Branded content**.

**Expected result:** Their account shows in Ads Manager as an identity you can use.

**Step 3.** Choose content: posts that already did well for free, or new videos made for ads.

**Expected result:** You have picked 2 to 5 creator posts.

**Step 4.** Add them to your normal cold campaign as a new batch, not a separate campaign. Make the brand clear in the first five seconds.

**Expected result:** Partnership ads run alongside your other ads.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 5.** Judge them by the same rules as every other ad: cost per sale and hook rate (Lesson 6.12).

**Expected result:** Creator ads earn their budget on the same terms.

**Step 6.** Brief every creator on one page. Include the goal, audience, key message, must say, must not say, 3 hook ideas, deliverables, usage rights and deadline.

**Expected result:** Each creator has a written brief before filming.

**Step 7.** Offer simple terms, for example a 10% off code for their audience and 15% commission on new-customer sales.

**Expected result:** Every creator has written terms.

**Step 8.** Get written permission. Then run the creator's video as a partnership ad from their account, inside your normal campaigns.

**Expected result:** Partnership ads are live, often at a lower cost per new customer.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 9.** Give affiliates (people paid per sale) a simple guide. Include product facts, angles that work and examples.

**Expected result:** Affiliates have what they need to post without asking you.

## Lesson 19.7: Video ad formats
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** The format is how a video tells its story: testimonial, demo, comparison and more. A healthy account has several formats live at once, matched to how well people know you.

**Step 1.** Label each live video ad with its format from the table below.

**Expected result:** You can see your current mix.

**Step 2.** Add one format you are missing to your next batch. Pick it to fit how much the audience knows (Lesson 5.5).

**Expected result:** Your account has at least four formats live.

### Good to know

| Format | What it looks like | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talking to camera (testimonial) | Creator talks to camera: the problem, the switch, the result | Cold audiences who know the problem |
| Problem then solution | Show the pain, then the product fixing it | Cold audiences |
| Demo or how-to | Hands and product, step by step | People comparing options; products that need explaining |
| Unboxing | First impressions, what is in the box | Gifts, premium feel |
| Before and after | A visible change (follow platform rules) | Products with visible results |
| Comparison | You versus the usual option, side by side | Crowded categories |
| Listicle | "4 reasons I switched" | Explaining several benefits |
| Founder story | Why we made it | Trust, launches, higher prices |
| Myth busting | "You have been told X. Here's the truth" | Buyers who research a lot |
| Street or customer interview | Real reactions | Social proof |
| Day in the life | The product inside a daily routine | Lifestyle products |
| Reaction or stitch | Replying to a comment or a one-star review | Answering objections |
| Still image and carousel | An image with a bold claim, review or offer | Warm audiences, sales, catalog |

## Lesson 19.8: Hooks that win the first three seconds
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Every video has two hooks working together in the first 3 seconds. One is what you see (the visual hook). The other is the first sentence (the spoken hook). They do not have to say the same thing. Hooks are what you test most.

**Step 1.** Collect hook ideas from customer reviews, support emails and competitors' one and two star reviews.

**Expected result:** You have a hook bank of 10 or more ideas.

**Step 2.** Write a visual hook for your next video. Try movement, an odd object, a result, a face mid-reaction or bold text on screen.

**Expected result:** The first frame grabs attention with sound off.

**Step 3.** Write 3 spoken hooks of different types. Try a bold claim, a call out, a curious gap or gossip. Or a myth, a reply to a one-star review or a price question. Or the result first, an opinion or a surprising fact.

**Expected result:** You have 3 openings to film.

**Step 4.** After 7 days, check two numbers in Ads Manager. Hook rate is 3-second views divided by impressions. Hold rate is views to 50% (or ThruPlays) divided by 3-second views.

**Expected result:** You know which opening works. Under 20% hook rate means the opening is not working.

## Lesson 19.9: Scripting
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** A script turns a hook into a sale. A simple five-part structure for 20 to 45 seconds works for most products, and rotating script ideas keeps ads fresh.

**Step 1.** Draft your script in the five parts in the table below. They are the hook, the problem or setting, product and proof, answering a worry, and the call to action.

**Expected result:** You have a 20 to 45 second script.

**Step 2.** Pick a script idea to rotate in. Try a bold claim and proof, four reasons, a myth, gossip, or the same product for a different problem. Or try why now, let the product talk, or face the rivals.

**Expected result:** Your next batch uses a script idea you have not tried recently.

**Step 3.** Write a clear call to action with a reason. For example: "Tap Shop now and grab the starter kit, so it arrives before Friday". It should match the offer on the landing page.

**Expected result:** The call to action is specific and matches the page.

**Watch out:** Lots of views but few sales usually means a weak call to action. Or the hook draws the wrong people. Test 30 to 60 second versions when the product needs explaining.

### Good to know

**A simple structure for a 20 to 45 second video:**

| Part | Seconds | Its job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0 to 3 | Stop the scroll |
| Problem or setting | 3 to 8 | Make viewers think "that's me" |
| Product and proof | 8 to 25 | How it works, why it is different, the evidence |
| Answer a worry | 25 to 35 | Price, quality, fit or delivery |
| Call to action | Last 3 to 5 | What to do, and why now |

## Lesson 19.10: Shooting on a phone
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** A phone is enough to shoot ads that work. Light, sound and extra shots matter more than gear.

**Step 1.** Face a window for natural light, and avoid overhead light only.

**Expected result:** The face is evenly lit with no harsh shadows.

**Step 2.** Film tall (9:16) at 1080p or higher, using the back camera when you can.

**Expected result:** Footage is sharp and the right shape.

**Step 3.** Use a clip-on microphone, or film in a quiet room with soft furnishings.

**Expected result:** Audio is clean with no echo.

**Step 4.** Film several takes of the hook. Then film extra clips: close-ups, the product in use, packaging, hands, results and settings.

**Expected result:** You have spare hooks and cutaway shots for editing.

## Lesson 19.11: Editing for performance
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Editing decides whether people watch past 3 seconds. Fast cuts, captions and several hook versions are what lift results.

**Step 1.** Cut every 1 to 2 seconds at the start and remove pauses and filler words.

**Expected result:** The opening moves fast.

**Step 2.** Burn in captions inside the safe zones and add bold hook text on the first frame.

**Expected result:** The video works with sound off.

**Step 3.** Lay B-roll over talking and add quiet music with a licence that allows ads.

**Expected result:** The video shows what is said and the music is cleared.

**Step 4.** Export 9:16 (Reels, Stories, TikTok), 4:5 (feed) and 1:1 when needed.

**Expected result:** You have every size.

**Step 5.** Make 3 to 5 hook versions of each winning video, changing only the first three seconds.

**Expected result:** Winners get fresh openings to test.

**Watch out:** Label AI-made people or voices where required, never fake reviews, and check auto-enhance settings before launch.

## Lesson 19.12: The UGC brief
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** A clear brief is the difference between videos you can use and wasted product. Send this one-page brief to every creator before they film.

**Step 1.** Copy the brief below and fill in brand, product, link, who you are and who it is for.

**Expected result:** The top of the brief is complete.

**Step 2.** Fill in the goal, key message, proof points, must say and must not say.

**Expected result:** The creator knows what to say and avoid.

**Step 3.** Fill in format, 3 hooks, script outline, call to action and offer.

**Expected result:** The creative direction is clear.

**Step 4.** Fill in deliverables, B-roll list, do and don't, usage rights, deadline, payment, disclosure and example links, then send.

**Expected result:** The creator has everything needed to deliver the first time.

## Lesson 19.13: Make new ads every week, on a schedule
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** A weekly ad-making habit is a steady weekly supply of new ads. Accounts that keep winning are the ones that never run out of fresh, well-briefed creative.

**Step 1.** Aim for 4 to 8 new ads a week at small to medium spend. Make more as spend grows.

**Expected result:** You have a weekly creative target.

**Step 2.** Keep a hook bank and a library of winners, tagged by angle, format, type of person and result.

**Expected result:** You can find any past winner in seconds.

**Step 3.** Every Monday, look at last week's results and pick the winning idea. Brief new versions with the same message: new hooks, creators and formats.

**Expected result:** A new batch is briefed every Monday.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 4.** Borrow popular formats and speak to specific groups (runners, new parents, gamers, nurses).

**Expected result:** Each batch includes a format that is working on social right now and a niche-focused ad.

**Watch out:** Copy the format, never the content. One well-briefed idea with five hooks beats twenty random videos.

## Self-check

1. A creator's code gives 10% off but your pop-up gives 15%. What happens, and how do you fix it?
2. What are the two hooks in every video?
3. Your video gets lots of views but few sales. Name two likely causes.
4. When should you pay a creator a fee instead of only sending free product?
5. Where do partnership ads belong in your ad account?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Shoppers use the bigger public code, so the creator earns nothing and stops posting. Match the offers, or give creators something only they can offer.
2. The visual hook and the spoken hook.
3. A weak or missing call to action, or a hook that attracts people who will not buy. Also check the landing page matches the ad.
4. Once their content has tested well, or when you need raw footage, hooks and usage rights by a deadline.
5. Inside your normal sales campaigns as a batch of ads, judged by the same rules as other ads.
