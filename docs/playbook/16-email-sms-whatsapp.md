# Module 16: Email, SMS and messaging

**Outcome:** automatic emails (flows) that earn money every day, and a steady email plan that does not tire your list. Plus text and chat messages where they help.

**Related SOPs:** [13 Email and SMS flows](../sops/13-email-sms-flows.md), [14 List growth and campaigns](../sops/14-list-growth-and-campaigns.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Convert (turn visits into sales). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** Emails and texts that send themselves when someone does something, plus regular newsletters.
>
> **Why it matters:** Your list is free to reach. Good automatic emails earn money every day while you sleep.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Turn on the welcome, abandoned checkout and thank-you emails first.
> 2. Send a helpful email to your list once or twice a week.
> 3. Check sales from email each week and fix the weakest flow.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Flow:** An email or text series that sends by itself when something happens, like a new sign-up.
> - **Abandoned checkout:** When someone starts checkout but does not finish. A reminder email can bring them back.
> - **Segment:** A smaller group of your list, like people who bought in the last 90 days.
> - **Campaign email:** A one-off email you send to your list, like a new arrival announcement.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 16.1: Sign-up forms
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** A sign-up form (often a pop-up) asks visitors for their email or phone number. It feeds every flow and campaign you send. As a rule of thumb, a good form signs up 3 to 10 of every 100 people who see it. A weak one wastes your traffic.

**Step 1.** Pick one reward. Dollars off ($10 off) works if your orders are big, and percent off (10% off) if they are small. Or try a free gift, early access or a prize draw.

**Expected result:** Your form has one clear reason to sign up.

**Step 2.** In your email or form app, build a form with steps. First a one-click question ("What are you shopping for?"), then email, then phone number.

**Expected result:** The form asks an easy question first, which raises completion (micro-commitment).

**Step 3.** Show it after about 5 to 8 seconds or 30% scroll. On computers, also show it when the mouse moves to leave. Hide it from people who already signed up.

**Expected result:** The form shows at the right moment and never to people already on the list.

**Watch out:** Game-style forms (spin to win) can lift sign-ups. But watch the cost of discounts and the quality of the list.

**Step 4.** If you sell to several regions, add location rules. Then each region sees offers and text sign-ups that suit it.

**Expected result:** Overseas visitors see an offer that fits them.

**Step 5.** After 2 weeks, measure and improve.

1. Open the form's report and find the sign-up rate.
2. Change one thing (the offer, the headline or the timing) and wait another 2 weeks.

**Expected result:** Over 3 in 100 people who see the form sign up, or a test is running to get there. As a rule of thumb, 6 to 10 is possible with a strong offer.

**Step 6.** Put a sign-up form inside a page too, not only as a pop-up.

1. In your email app, make a new **Embedded** form.
2. Copy its form ID or code.
3. In Shopify, click **Online Store**, then **Themes**, then **Customize**.
4. Open the page you want (your home page or an "About" page).
5. Click **Add section** or **Add block**.
6. Pick your email app's form block (or **Custom Liquid**).
7. Paste the form ID or code.
8. Click **Save**.

**Expected result:** The form shows inside the page, and a test sign-up appears in your email list within a minute.

**Watch out:** Only send marketing to people who said yes. In Australia, the Spam Act needs consent, your business name and a working unsubscribe (see [ACMA: avoid sending spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam)). In the UK and EU, a pre-ticked box is not consent (see [ICO: electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/)).

### Good to know

Sign-up reward and form rules:

- **Count the cost.** A 15% code on a $60 order costs you $9, but only when someone buys. Work out what the reward costs for each new buyer, not each sign-up.
- **Prize draws grow lists fast, but fewer of those people buy.** Check sales from those sign-ups before you run another.
- **Match the form to the page.** Show a different form (or offer) on different pages, or to people from different ads. Most email apps can target by page address or ad link.
- **Before a sale, swap the reward.** Two to three weeks before, change the form to "Get early access" so people join for the sale.
- **Test one offer against another for 2 weeks.** Give each offer its own welcome email so everyone gets the code they were promised. Pick the winner by sales and profit, not just sign-ups.

## Lesson 16.2: The five core flows and extras
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** A flow is a series of emails or texts that sends itself when someone does something. Five core flows earn money every day without you lifting a finger. Timings are in [SOP 13](../sops/13-email-sms-flows.md).

**Step 1.** Set up the **Welcome** flow, triggered when someone joins your list.

**Expected result:** New subscribers get your first email within minutes.

<!-- do:flow-welcome -->

**Step 2.** Set up **Browse abandonment**, triggered when someone views a product but does not add to cart.

**Expected result:** Browsers get a reminder of what they looked at.

<!-- do:flow-browse-abandonment -->

**Step 3.** Set up **Cart abandonment**, triggered when someone adds to cart but does not start checkout.

**Expected result:** Cart abandoners get a reminder within a few hours.

<!-- do:flow-abandoned-cart -->

**Step 4.** Set up **Checkout abandonment**, triggered when someone starts checkout but does not pay.

**Expected result:** The highest-intent shoppers get a reminder within about an hour.

<!-- do:flow-abandoned-checkout -->

**Step 5.** Set up **Post-purchase**, triggered when someone buys.

**Expected result:** New buyers get thanks, how-to tips and a cross-sell.

<!-- do:flow-post-purchase -->

**Step 6.** Then add the extras one at a time. Win-back, back in stock, price drop, time to reorder, VIP and birthday. Also a clean-up flow (removing people who never open) and a review request. And a "you may also like" email after the first order.

**Expected result:** Your flow list grows month by month.

## Lesson 16.3: Writing emails that get clicks
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Emails that get clicks feel personal and have one clear job. Mixing plain founder emails with designed ones keeps your list interested.

**Step 1.** Alternate plain-text style emails from the founder (images are fine) with designed emails.

**Expected result:** Your next four campaigns include at least one founder-style email.

**Step 2.** Use what people told you in forms to make emails fit them. That could be what they like, skin type or size.

**Expected result:** Different subscribers see different product picks.

**Step 3.** Give each email one main button (call to action).

**Expected result:** Every email has one obvious next step.

**Watch out:** Use countdown timers only for real deadlines, and show a message (not zeros) when time is up.

## Lesson 16.4: Segments
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A segment is a group of people on your list who share something. Sending to the right segment keeps emails out of spam folders and makes each message more useful.

**Step 1.** In your email app, make groups. Use people who engaged in the last 30, 60 and 90 days, buyers, repeat buyers and VIPs.

**Expected result:** The segments show live counts.

**Step 2.** Add window shoppers (looked recently, never bought), lapsed buyers (used to buy, stopped) and product-interest segments.

**Expected result:** You can target each stage of the customer journey.

**Step 3.** Send most campaign emails to the engaged 60 or 90 day segment.

**Expected result:** Open rates rise and spam complaints fall.

**Watch out:** Some email apps open emails automatically to protect privacy, so opens alone overstate engagement. Build "engaged" segments on clicks, site visits and orders too.

## Lesson 16.5: Campaign cadence and ideas
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A campaign email is a one-off email to your list, like a newsletter. Cadence means how often you send. Building up slowly keeps your list healthy while you learn what they like.

**Step 1.** Start with 2 campaign emails a month. Move to weekly after a month, then 2 to 4 a week if people keep opening.

**Expected result:** You have a written send schedule.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 2.** Fill the plan from this list: new arrivals, how-to tips, customer stories, behind the scenes and founder notes. Also gift guides, myth busting, customer photos, seasonal moments, limited bundles and rare flash sales.

**Expected result:** You have a month of email ideas.

## Lesson 16.6: Reviewing performance
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Monthly reviews show where emails leak money. Fixing the weakest step first gives the biggest gain for the least work.

**Step 1.** Open your email app's reports. Write down last month's flow and campaign numbers shown in the table below.

**Expected result:** You have one line of numbers for flows and one for campaigns.

**Step 2.** Find the weakest step. Low opens: work on the subject line. Low clicks: work on the content and button. Clicks but few orders: work on the landing page.

**Expected result:** You have one fix to make this month.

### Good to know

A rule-of-thumb guide, not a promise. Some email apps open emails automatically for privacy, which inflates open rates, so trust clicks and sales more. The spam complaint line is Google's (Lesson 16.8).

| | Open rate | Click rate | Other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flows | Above 30% | About 5 to 6% | Revenue per recipient going up |
| Campaigns | Above 30% | About 1.5 to 2% | Unsubscribes under 0.3%, spam complaints under 0.1% |

## Lesson 16.7: How to run a fair email split test
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Email A/B tests show what your list responds to. One change, two big enough groups and a written guess make results trustworthy.

**Step 1.** Write a hypothesis (your guess), for example "a question in the subject line gets more opens".

**Expected result:** The test has a written guess.

**Step 2.** Change one thing only: subject line, send time, offer or layout. If you can, split the list into two groups of at least a few thousand each.

**Expected result:** The test is fair and big enough to read.

**Step 3.** Record the result and use the winner from then on.

**Expected result:** Your test log grows and your emails improve each month.

## Lesson 16.8: Deliverability
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Deliverability means your emails reach the inbox, not spam. Without it, the best email in the world earns nothing. Most of it is one-time setup.

**Step 1.** Open your email app's domain settings. Follow the guide to add SPF, DKIM and DMARC records at your domain host. These settings prove your emails really come from you.

**Expected result:** The email app shows your sending domain as verified or authenticated. Gmail and Yahoo require SPF, DKIM and DMARC from bulk senders. Gmail counts 5,000 or more emails a day as bulk (see [Google: email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)).

**Step 2.** On a new account, start slowly. Send each email to no more than about 1.5 times as many people as the last one, engaged people first.

**Expected result:** Sends grow steadily without bounce or spam spikes.

**Step 3.** Remove bad addresses, like typos (gmial.com). Set up a clean-up flow for people who never open.

**Expected result:** Your list holds only reachable, interested people.

**Step 4.** Watch bounce rates by email provider each week. Avoid all caps, lots of exclamation marks and emails that are one big image.

**Expected result:** You catch problems with one provider early (for example, bounces from only one provider's addresses).

**Watch out:** Gmail and Yahoo also need one-click unsubscribe in every marketing email. Keep spam complaints under 0.3%, and aim for under 0.1% (see [Yahoo: sender best practices](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)). Most email apps add the one-click header for you.

**Watch out:** Every marketing email needs a working unsubscribe and your business details. Australia's Spam Act says act on unsubscribes within 5 working days. The US CAN-SPAM Act says 10 business days and needs a postal address (see [FTC: CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).

## Lesson 16.9: Platform costs
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Email platforms often charge by the number of people (profiles) on your list. Many tools bill on active profiles, and suppressed profiles often do not count. Check your tool's pricing page. Cleaning the list saves money and improves deliverability at the same time.

**Step 1.** Suppress or remove people who have not opened or clicked in 180 days.

**Expected result:** Your billable profile count drops.

**Step 2.** Archive old lists you no longer send to.

**Expected result:** Only active lists remain.

**Step 3.** If cost per profile is more than a profile is worth to you, compare two other platforms.

**Expected result:** You know whether to stay or switch.

## Lesson 16.10: SMS
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Text messages get read fast but cost more than email. Used for big moments only, they earn well; used every day, they drive unsubscribes.

**Step 1.** Add a text step in the welcome flow and checkout reminder. It is for people who did not act on the email.

**Expected result:** SMS only goes out where email did not work.

<!-- do:flow-abandoned-checkout -->

**Step 2.** Use texts for sale launches and last chance messages.

**Expected result:** Your sale plan has two SMS sends.

**Step 3.** Finish your SMS app's sender registration before sending. Turn on its quiet hours setting, and keep each text short with one link.

**Expected result:** Texts arrive from your registered name or number, at sensible times, with one clear link.

**Watch out:** In Australia, a text from a business name (sender ID) must be registered. Since 1 July 2026, unregistered names show as "Unverified" (see [ACMA: SMS Sender ID Register](https://www.acma.gov.au/sms-sender-id-register)). The Spam Act's consent and unsubscribe rules apply to texts too.

**Watch out:** In the US, carriers block texts from numbers that are not registered (toll-free verification or 10DLC). Get clear written consent first, honour STOP replies, and send only 8am to 9pm local time. Some states, like Florida, are stricter (see [47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)).

## Lesson 16.11: WhatsApp and chat commerce
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** WhatsApp and similar chat apps work well where your customers already use them. They are great for VIPs and for pricey products people want to ask about first. The free WhatsApp Business app suits one-to-one chats. Promotions at scale need the WhatsApp Business Platform, usually through your email or SMS app.

**Step 1.** Set up WhatsApp Business and only message people who opted in.

**Expected result:** You have an opted-in WhatsApp list.

**Step 2.** Get templates (pre-approved message formats) approved for marketing messages and set up quick replies for common questions.

**Expected result:** You can send approved promotions and answer common questions in one tap.

**Step 3.** Use chat for VIPs, launches and selling through conversation.

**Expected result:** High-value customers can buy through a conversation.

**Watch out:** Since 1 July 2025, Meta charges for each message delivered. Marketing messages cost money, unless someone just messaged you from a click-to-WhatsApp ad. Since 1 October 2026, replies within 24 hours of a customer's message are free only for the first 1,000 a month per number, and order updates (utility messages) are charged too (see [Meta: WhatsApp pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/)).

**Watch out:** Since April 2025, Meta has paused marketing templates to US (+1) phone numbers. Check with your provider before planning US WhatsApp promotions.

## Lesson 16.12: Loyalty
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A loyalty program rewards people who buy again. It only pays off when enough customers already come back, and it must be measured by repeat purchases, not points.

**Step 1.** Check your repeat purchase rate (the share of customers who bought more than once) in Shopify **Analytics > Reports > Customer cohort analysis**. As a rule of thumb, start loyalty only if over about 15 to 20% of customers already buy again.

**Expected result:** You know whether loyalty is worth it yet.

**Step 2.** Choose rewards your margin can afford (points or cashback) and add VIP levels with early access.

**Expected result:** The program is set up with costed rewards.

<!-- do:flow-vip -->

**Step 3.** Add referral rewards for customers who bring a friend.

**Expected result:** Customers can share a referral link.

**Step 4.** After 90 days, measure the change in repeat purchase rate.

**Expected result:** You know whether the program changed behaviour.

## Lesson 16.13: Sale email and SMS schedule
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Big sales live or die on email and SMS. A fixed schedule from tease to thank-you makes sure every phase gets a message, and nobody gets spammed.

**Step 1.** Before the sale, build your early-access list.

1. Two weeks before, turn on an "early access" sign-up form.
2. Each week, send one teaser email to that list.

**Expected result:** Your early-access list is growing.

<!-- do:bfcm -->

**Step 2.** On launch, send one email and one text.

**Expected result:** Your list hears about the sale first.

**Step 3.** Mid-sale, send a new reason to buy (a new product, a bundle or a gift).

**Expected result:** The middle of the sale has fresh news.

**Step 4.** On the last day, send a last chance email and text.

**Expected result:** Final-day urgency reaches everyone.

**Step 5.** After the sale, send new buyers thanks, how-to tips, results and a small deal that ends soon. Switch your cart flows back from their sale versions.

**Expected result:** New buyers are looked after and flows are back to normal.

## Lesson 16.14: Plain-text emails that feel personal
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Designed emails look like ads. A short plain email looks like a note from a person. Plain emails often get more replies and can land in the main inbox more often.

**Step 1.** Pick one email in a flow to test. A good first choice is the second welcome email or the abandoned checkout email.

**Expected result:** You know which email you will change.

**Step 2.** Make a copy and remove the header, big images and buttons, keeping your name at the bottom.

**Expected result:** An email that is mostly text, like one from a friend.

**Step 3.** Write it as the founder in 60 to 120 words: one idea, one question and one plain product link.

**Expected result:** A short, warm email with one link.

**Step 4.** Send from a real name, like "Sam from your store", and let replies reach a real inbox.

**Expected result:** People can reply, and someone reads the replies.

**Step 5.** Split test it against the designed version for 2 weeks (Lesson 16.7). Compare clicks and orders, not just opens.

**Expected result:** You know which style wins for that email.

**Watch out:** Plain does not mean messy. Short lines, no spelling mistakes, and one clear ask.

## Lesson 16.15: Ask shoppers what they want, then use the answers
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** When a shopper tells you what they need, you can send them the right products instead of guessing. Answers given on purpose are more useful than clicks. They also make every email feel like it was written for that person.

**Step 1.** Pick one question that splits your customers into groups. For example: "What is your skin type?", "Who are you shopping for?" or "Which size is your dog?"

**Expected result:** One question with 3 to 5 simple answers.

**Step 2.** Add the question to your sign-up form, as a second step after the email box (Lesson 16.1).

**Expected result:** New subscribers can tap one answer in a second.

**Step 3.** In your email app, check the answer saves to each person's profile. Most apps call this a custom field or profile property.

**Expected result:** You can see the answer on a test profile.

**Step 4.** In your welcome flow (Lesson 16.2), add a split based on the answer. Send each group an email with the 3 products that suit them best.

**Expected result:** Each group gets product picks that fit them.

**Step 5.** For people who did not answer, add a one-click question in the second welcome email. Make each answer a button that saves the answer.

**Expected result:** More of your list has an answer.

**Step 6.** After 30 days, compare clicks and sales from the matched emails with your old welcome emails (Lesson 16.6).

**Expected result:** You know if the answers lift sales. Then use the same groups in sale emails (Lesson 16.4).

**Watch out:** Ask only what you will use. Every extra question means fewer people finish the form.

## Lesson 16.16: Wake up your quieter subscribers before a big sale
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** In a big sale you want to reach more of your list. But if you suddenly email people who have ignored you for months, inbox providers like Gmail can start sending all your emails to spam. Waking those people up slowly, a few weeks early, lets you reach more of them safely.

**Step 1.** In your email app, make four groups based on the last time someone opened or clicked one of your emails: the last 30 days, 60 days, 90 days and 180 days. Add people who joined in the last 15 days to the 30-day group, because they have not had time to open yet.

**Expected result:** Four groups, each showing how many people are in it.

**Step 2.** Make one more group called "sleeping": people who got at least 5 emails but have not opened or clicked anything in 180 days.

**Expected result:** You know how many people on your list are asleep.

**Step 3.** In normal weeks, send your emails to the 90-day group only (it includes the 30- and 60-day people).

**Expected result:** Your usual emails go to people who actually read them.

**Step 4.** Two to three weeks before the sale, send one or two friendly wake-up emails to people in the 180-day group who are not in the 90-day group. Keep them short, with one clear reason to click, like "Get early access to our sale".

**Expected result:** Some quieter subscribers click and move back into your 90-day group.

<!-- do:bfcm -->

**Step 5.** Grow each send slowly. Make each send no more than about 1.5 times bigger than the one before. For example: 4,000 people, then 6,000, then 9,000.

**Expected result:** Your sends get bigger step by step, not all at once.

**Step 6.** Only on the biggest sale days, send to the whole 180-day group. After every send, check unsubscribes and spam complaints. If either jumps, go back to the smaller group for the next send.

**Expected result:** Your biggest days reach the most people, and you stop growing at the first warning sign.

**Step 7.** Turn on a goodbye flow for the sleeping group: two short emails over two weeks asking "Do you still want to hear from us?" with one button. Stop emailing anyone who does not click.

**Expected result:** Sleeping subscribers either come back or leave your sends, and your list stays healthy.

<!-- do:flow-win-back -->

**Watch out:** Some phones and email apps mark emails as opened even when nobody read them. Clicks are the more honest sign that someone is awake.

### Good to know

| Group | When to email |
| --- | --- |
| Opened or clicked in 90 days | Every normal email |
| Opened or clicked in 91 to 180 days | Wake-up emails before a sale, then the biggest sale days |
| Sleeping (180+ days, 5+ emails, no opens or clicks) | Goodbye flow only |

## Lesson 16.17: Do your customers come back? Plan around it
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Some stores sell things people buy again and again, like coffee or skincare. Others sell things people buy once, like a sofa. How often your customers come back should change how much you pay for a new customer, how often you run sales and which emails you send.

**Step 1.** In Shopify, open **Analytics > Reports** and find the customer cohort report. Write down two numbers: the share of customers who bought again within 12 months, and how many days it usually takes them to buy the second time.

**Expected result:** Two numbers, for example "22% come back, usually after about 45 days".

**Step 2.** Label your store. Under about 20% coming back is low repeat. Over about 30% is high repeat. In between is middle: use a bit of both plans below.

**Expected result:** Your store has one label: low, middle or high repeat.

**Step 3.** Set the most you will pay for a new customer. Low repeat: keep the ad cost of a new customer under the profit from their first order. High repeat: you may pay a bit more, up to first-order profit plus the profit you really get back from repeat orders in the next 12 months. Use your own numbers, not hopes.

**Expected result:** One number for the most you will pay to win a new customer.

<!-- do:numbers -->

**Step 4.** Set your sale rhythm. Low repeat: run fewer, bigger events aimed at new buyers. High repeat: add early access for past buyers and small events just for your list.

**Expected result:** Your sales calendar matches your store type.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 5.** Time a reorder email. Send it a little before the usual time to the second order. For example, if people usually come back after 45 days, send a reminder on about day 38.

**Expected result:** A reorder email is live in your post-purchase flow.

<!-- do:flow-post-purchase -->

**Step 6.** Pick three product pairs that are often bought together. In Shopify, open **Apps > Search & Discovery > Product recommendations** and add them as complementary products. Use the same pairs in a cross-sell email a week or two after delivery.

**Expected result:** Three pairs show on product pages and in one follow-up email.

**Watch out:** Customers won in a big sale often come back less than customers who paid full price. Check them as their own group before you plan around them.

### Good to know

| | Low repeat (under about 20%) | High repeat (over about 30%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Most you pay for a new customer | First-order profit | First-order profit plus proven repeat profit |
| Sales | Fewer, bigger, for new buyers | VIP early access and small list-only events |
| Key emails | Thank-you, how-to, reviews, gifts for friends | Reorder reminder timed to the usual gap |
| Cross-sells | Accessories that go with the first buy | Refills, bundles and subscriptions |
