# Module 27: Accounting, bookkeeping and tax time

**Outcome:** you have the right helpers, and your store and your books agree each month. Your stock and product costs are recorded properly. Tax time, whenever your financial year ends, is a checklist, not a panic.

**Related:** [Module 26 Tax, GST, sales tax and duties](26-tax-gst-sales-tax-duties.md), [Module 14 Inventory, cash and the year plan](14-inventory-cash-planning.md), [Module 1 Daily profit foundations](01-daily-profit-foundations.md), [Module 24 Payments, fraud and chargebacks](24-payments-fraud-chargebacks.md)

> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Pick a registered accountant and bookkeeper who know online stores.
> 2. Record each payout as one summary entry and reconcile every week.
> 3. Count stock at year end and follow the tax dates Helix puts on your calendar.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Reconciliation:** Matching the money in your bank with the sales, fees and refunds in your books.
> - **Clearing account:** A holding account in your books. A payment provider's sales and fees sit there until the payout lands in the bank.
> - **Stocktake:** Counting every unit you hold, usually at the end of the financial year. Then your books match the shelf.
> - **Landed cost:** What one unit really costs once it reaches your warehouse: product, freight, duties and fees.
>

## Lesson 27.1: Choose your structure and your helpers
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Your business structure changes how you pay tax and what you are personally liable for. A good accountant pays for themselves, while a poor bookkeeper can cost you a year of clean-up.

**Step 1.** Learn the two roles. A bookkeeper keeps records up to date each week or month. An accountant or tax agent does tax returns, gives tax advice and helps you plan.

**Expected result:** You know which helper you need for what.

**Step 2.** Ask every candidate the same three questions.

- How many online stores do you look after?
- How do you handle Shopify payouts, stock and product cost?
- What does the monthly fee include?

**Expected result:** You can compare candidates on the same answers.

**Step 3.** Choose a fixed monthly fee with a written list of what is included. Also list what costs extra, like tax returns or catch-up work.

**Expected result:** There are no surprise bills.

**Step 4.** Ask for read-only access to your books from day one and a short monthly summary.

**Expected result:** You can see your books any time.

**Step 5.** Review your structure with your accountant once profit is steady.

**Expected result:** Your structure still suits the business.

**Watch out:** Outside the US: check BAS agents and tax agents on the [Tax Practitioners Board register](https://www.tpb.gov.au/public-register). Common setups are sole trader, company and trust (see [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/planning/business-structures-and-types/business-structures)). US: look for a CPA or enrolled agent (see the [IRS directory](https://irs.treasury.gov/rpo/rpo.jsf)). If you live overseas and own a US company, get a cross-border expert.

## Lesson 27.2: Connect your store to your books cleanly
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Copying every order into your accounting software creates thousands of lines that never match your bank. Recording each payout as one summary instead keeps your books clean and quick to check.

**Step 1.** Use one accounting system, such as Xero or QuickBooks.

**Expected result:** All records live in one place.

**Step 2.** Install a connector app that records each payout as one entry. It splits it into sales, shipping charged, discounts, refunds, GST or sales tax, and fees.

**Expected result:** Each payout is one tidy entry. Compare connectors on price and payment method support.

**Step 3.** Make a clearing account (a holding account) for each payment provider. That means Shopify Payments, PayPal and each buy now pay later provider.

**Expected result:** Sales go in, payouts and fees come out, and the balance falls back towards zero.

**Step 4.** Record payment and buy now pay later fees as expenses, not lower sales.

**Expected result:** Your sales figure is the full amount.

**Step 5.** Reconcile (match to the bank) every bank line weekly.

**Expected result:** Ten minutes a week, nothing left at year end.

**Step 6.** Each month compare your books' profit with Helix and find the reason for any big gap.

**Expected result:** The two agree, or you know why (usually missing costs or stock changes).

**Watch out:** Outside the US: check GST codes, especially on overseas costs with no GST (see [ATO record keeping](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/record-keeping-for-business/overview-of-record-keeping-rules-for-business)). US: keep sales tax in its own liability account, never in sales (see [IRS recordkeeping](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping)).

## Lesson 27.3: Cost of goods and stock in your books
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Stock you hold is an asset (something you own), not an expense, until it sells. Getting this right is the biggest difference between your books' profit and your real profit.

**Step 1.** Record stock purchases at landed cost: product price plus freight, duty and broker fees, without claimable GST.

**Expected result:** Stock values are complete and ex GST.

**Step 2.** Each month or quarter, work out COGS (cost of goods sold). That is opening stock value plus purchases minus closing stock value.

**Expected result:** You have COGS for the period.

**Step 3.** Count all stock at least once a year (a stocktake) and adjust your books to match.

**Expected result:** Books match the shelf.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 4.** Write off damaged, lost or unsellable stock with a note of why. Ask your accountant how to value slow stock.

**Expected result:** Dead stock is no longer counted as an asset.

**Step 5.** Use the same method every year.

**Expected result:** Periods compare fairly.

**Watch out:** Outside the US: count stock on or near 30 June. Small businesses whose stock value changes by $5,000 or less may skip the formal step (see [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/income-and-deductions-for-business/accounting-for-trading-stock/valuing-trading-stock)). US: count on or near 31 December or your year end (see [IRS Publication 538](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p538)).

## Lesson 27.4: Records and the monthly close
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A short monthly routine, called the monthly close, keeps tax time simple. It gives you numbers you can trust. Aim to finish within two weeks of month end.

**Step 1.** Reconcile all bank accounts, cards and payment clearing accounts.

**Expected result:** Every balance matches.

**Step 2.** Attach a receipt or bill to every cost. Use a receipt app, or forward emails to your accounting software.

**Expected result:** No expense is missing a receipt.

**Step 3.** Fix all uncategorised lines.

**Expected result:** Every line has a category.

**Step 4.** Compare profit and loss with last month and with Helix.

**Expected result:** Changes make sense.

<!-- do:growth -->

**Step 5.** Move tax money (GST or sales tax plus an income tax estimate) into a separate savings account.

**Expected result:** Tax is set aside.

**Step 6.** Save key reports into a folder for the year.

**Expected result:** The year's records are in one place.

**Watch out:** Keep most records for 5 years in some countries (see [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/record-keeping-for-business/overview-of-record-keeping-rules-for-business)). Keep them at least 3 years in the US (see [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records)).

## Lesson 27.5: End of financial year
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Some financial years end on 30 June. Helix puts these steps on your calendar from May so nothing is rushed.

**Step 1.** In early May, book a planning chat with your accountant about expected profit and tax.

**Expected result:** The meeting is booked.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 2.** Before 30 June, finish the year-end jobs.

1. Count your stock (stocktake).
2. Write off stock that will never sell.
3. Make sure team super is paid on time.

**Expected result:** Stock and super are done.

**Watch out:** From 1 July 2026, employers pay super with each payday. It must reach the fund within 7 business days (see [ATO: Payday Super](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers/payday-super/about-payday-super)).

**Step 3.** Before 30 June, gather receipts for big purchases and check which costs fall in this year.

**Expected result:** Deductions are documented.

**Step 4.** In July, reconcile every account to 30 June and send your accountant the year's reports.

**Expected result:** Your accountant has everything.

**Step 5.** Lodge the June quarter BAS by 28 July (later if lodged online or through an agent).

**Expected result:** BAS is lodged.

**Step 6.** Send in your income tax return by 31 October yourself. Or join a registered tax agent's client list before then, so you can send it later (see [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/your-tax-return/how-to-lodge-your-tax-return/lodge-your-tax-return-with-a-registered-tax-agent)).

**Expected result:** Your return date is known and met.

## Lesson 27.6: Year end and tax season in the US
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Most US stores run on the calendar year. Helix puts these dates on your calendar for US stores.

**Step 1.** In December, count stock and review sales tax registrations against each state's threshold.

**Expected result:** Stock is counted and registrations are current.

**Step 2.** Collect a Form W-9 from every contractor before paying them.

**Expected result:** You have a W-9 for each contractor.

**Step 3.** By 31 January, send Form 1099-NEC to contractors you paid $2,000 or more in 2026. File it with the IRS too. The 2025 limit was $600 (see [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)).

**Expected result:** All 1099s are sent and filed.

**Step 4.** By 15 March, file partnership and S corporation returns (next business day if a weekend).

**Expected result:** Those returns are filed.

**Step 5.** By 15 April, file personal and C corporation returns and pay the first estimated tax. Later payments usually fall in June, September and January.

**Expected result:** Returns are filed and estimates are on the calendar.

**Watch out:** Foreign-owned US companies have extra forms and different dates, so use a cross-border specialist (see [IRS Form 1120-F](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120f)).
