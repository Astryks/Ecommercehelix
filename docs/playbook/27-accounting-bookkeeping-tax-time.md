# Module 27: Accounting, bookkeeping and tax time

**Outcome:** you have the right helpers, your store and your books agree each month, your stock and product costs are recorded properly, and tax time in June (Australia) or the new year (US) is a checklist, not a panic.

**Related:** [Module 26 Tax, GST, sales tax and duties](26-tax-gst-sales-tax-duties.md), [Module 14 Inventory, cash and the year plan](14-inventory-cash-planning.md), [Module 1 Daily profit foundations](01-daily-profit-foundations.md), [Module 24 Payments, fraud and chargebacks](24-payments-fraud-chargebacks.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **What it is:** Keep clean books, record stock properly and make tax time a checklist.
>
> **Why it matters:** Messy books hide your real profit and make tax time slow and expensive.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Pick a registered accountant and bookkeeper who know online stores.
> 2. Record each payout as one summary entry and reconcile every week.
> 3. Count stock at year end and follow the tax dates Helix puts on your calendar.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Reconciliation:** Matching the money in your bank with the sales, fees and refunds in your books.
> - **Clearing account:** A holding account in your books where a payment provider's sales and fees sit until the payout lands in the bank.
> - **Stocktake:** Counting every unit you hold, usually at the end of the financial year, so your books match the shelf.
> - **Landed cost:** What one unit really costs once it reaches your warehouse: product, freight, duties and fees.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 27.1: Choose your structure and your helpers

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

Your business structure changes how you pay tax and what you are personally liable for. A good accountant pays for themselves; a poor bookkeeper can cost you a year of clean-up.

**Do this:**

1. Know the roles. A bookkeeper keeps your records up to date each week or month. An accountant or tax agent prepares returns, gives tax advice and helps you plan.
2. Ask any candidate three questions: How many online stores do you look after? How do you handle Shopify payouts, stock and product cost? What is included in your monthly fee?
3. Prefer a fixed monthly fee with a clear list of what is included. Ask what costs extra (for example, tax returns or catch-up work).
4. Ask for read-only access to your books from day one, and a short monthly summary.
5. Review your structure with your accountant once profit becomes steady. What suited you at the start may not suit you later.

**Australia:** check that a bookkeeper who prepares your BAS is a registered BAS agent, and that your tax agent is registered, on the [Tax Practitioners Board register](https://www.tpb.gov.au/public-register). Common structures are sole trader, company and trust. See [business.gov.au: business structures](https://business.gov.au/planning/business-structures-and-types/business-structures).

**US:** look for a CPA or an enrolled agent. Common structures are sole proprietor, LLC and corporation (with S corporation tax treatment as an option). If you live outside the US and own a US company, choose an accountant who handles cross-border filings, because the forms and penalties are different. See the [IRS directory of tax return preparers](https://irs.treasury.gov/rpo/rpo.jsf).

**Self-check:** are your helpers registered? Do you know exactly what your monthly fee covers?

## Lesson 27.2: Connect your store to your books cleanly

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

Copying every order into your accounting software creates thousands of lines that never match your bank. Matching each payout instead keeps your books clean.

**Do this:**

1. Use one accounting system: Xero and QuickBooks are both common for online stores.
2. Record each payout as one summary entry, split into sales, shipping charged, discounts, refunds, GST or sales tax, and fees. Several connector apps can create these summaries automatically; compare them on price and on how they handle your payment methods.
3. Create a clearing account (a holding account) for each payment provider: Shopify Payments, PayPal and each buy now pay later provider. Sales go in; payouts and fees come out. The balance should fall back towards zero.
4. Record buy now pay later and payment provider fees as expenses, not as lower sales.
5. Reconcile every bank line each week. Ten minutes a week beats ten hours at year end.
6. Compare your books' profit with Helix each month. If they are far apart, find out why (usually missing costs or stock changes).

**Australia:** make sure GST codes are right on each line, especially for overseas costs where no GST was charged. See [ATO: record keeping for business](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/record-keeping-for-business/overview-of-record-keeping-rules-for-business).

**US:** keep sales tax collected in its own liability account, never in sales. See [IRS: recordkeeping](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping).

**Self-check:** do your clearing accounts fall back towards zero each month? Is every bank line reconciled?

## Lesson 27.3: Cost of goods and stock in your books

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

Stock you hold is an asset, not an expense, until it sells. Getting this right is the biggest difference between your books' profit and your real profit.

**Do this:**

1. Record stock purchases at landed cost: product price, freight, duty and broker fees. Leave out GST you can claim back.
2. Work out cost of goods sold each month or quarter: opening stock value plus purchases, minus closing stock value.
3. Count your stock fully at least once a year (a stocktake) and adjust your books to match.
4. Write off damaged, lost or unsellable stock with a note of why. Ask your accountant how to value slow stock.
5. Keep the same method every year so months and years compare fairly.

**Australia:** do your stocktake on or near 30 June. You can value each item at cost, market selling value or replacement value, and small businesses whose stock value changes by $5,000 or less may be able to skip the formal adjustment. Your accountant will choose. See [ATO: valuing trading stock](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/income-and-deductions-for-business/accounting-for-trading-stock/valuing-trading-stock).

**US:** count on or near 31 December (or your year end). Ask your CPA which inventory method suits you. See [IRS: Publication 538, accounting periods and methods](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p538).

**Self-check:** is stock on your balance sheet, not in expenses? When is your next full count?

## Lesson 27.4: Records and the monthly close

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

A short monthly routine keeps tax time simple and gives you numbers you can trust.

**Do this:**

1. Reconcile all bank accounts, cards and payment provider clearing accounts.
2. Attach a receipt or invoice to every expense. Use a receipt app or forward emails to your accounting software.
3. Check for uncategorised lines and fix them.
4. Review the profit and loss against last month and against Helix.
5. Move tax money (GST or sales tax, and an estimate for income tax) to a separate savings account.
6. Save a copy of key reports in a folder for the year.

**Australia:** keep most business records for 5 years; some must be kept longer. See [ATO: record keeping for business](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/record-keeping-for-business/overview-of-record-keeping-rules-for-business).

**US:** keep records for at least 3 years in most cases, and longer in some situations. See [IRS: how long should I keep records](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records).

**Self-check:** did you close last month within two weeks of it ending? Is tax money in its own account?

## Lesson 27.5: End of financial year in Australia

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

The Australian financial year ends on 30 June. Helix puts these steps on your calendar from May.

**Do this:**

1. Early May: book a planning chat with your accountant to talk about expected profit and tax.
2. Before 30 June: do your stocktake, write off dead stock and check that super for your team is paid on time.
3. Before 30 June: gather receipts for big purchases and check which costs fall in this year.
4. In July: reconcile every account up to 30 June and send your accountant the year's reports.
5. Lodge your June quarter BAS by 28 July (or later if lodged online or through an agent).
6. Income tax return: if you lodge your own as an individual or sole trader, it is due 31 October. A registered tax agent can often lodge later, but you must be on their client list before 31 October. See [ATO: lodge with a registered tax agent](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/your-tax-return/how-to-lodge-your-tax-return/lodge-your-tax-return-with-a-registered-tax-agent).

**Team:** from 1 July 2026, employers pay super with each payday, and it must reach the fund within 7 business days. See [ATO: Payday Super](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers/payday-super/about-payday-super).

**Self-check:** is your stocktake date in your calendar? Do you know your tax return due date?

## Lesson 27.6: Year end and tax season in the US

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a CPA or enrolled agent.

Most US stores run on the calendar year. Helix puts these dates on your calendar for US stores.

**Do this:**

1. December: count stock at year end and review sales tax registrations against each state's threshold.
2. Before paying any contractor, collect a Form W-9.
3. By 31 January: send Form 1099-NEC to contractors you paid $2,000 or more during 2026, and file it with the IRS. For payments made in 2025 the limit was $600. See [IRS: Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec).
4. 15 March: partnership and S corporation returns are due for calendar-year businesses (the next business day if it falls on a weekend).
5. 15 April: individual returns (including sole proprietors) and C corporation returns are due, and so is the first estimated tax payment for the year. Other estimated payments usually fall in June, September and January.
6. Foreign-owned US companies have extra forms and different due dates. Use a cross-border specialist. See [IRS: Form 1120-F](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120f).

**Self-check:** do you have a W-9 for every contractor? Are the March and April dates in your calendar?
