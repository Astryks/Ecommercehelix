# Module 26: Team, creators and money

**Outcome:** you hire the right first helper on the right terms and pay creators with clear agreements. You pay overseas suppliers without losing money to currency or scams. You compare loans by their true cost and keep tool bills in check. You look after yourself, and decide calmly if Amazon is worth adding.

**Related:** [Module 27 Founder rhythm, team and resilience](27-founder-team-productivity.md), [Module 6 Creators, UGC and video ads](06-creators-ugc-and-video-ads.md), [Module 19 Inventory, cash and the year plan](19-inventory-cash-planning.md), [Module 29 Choosing your tool stack](29-tool-stack.md)

> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Grow (keep more of every sale, then scale). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** Hire help, pay creators and suppliers safely, choose funding wisely and look after yourself.
>
> **Why it matters:** The right help buys back your time. The wrong deal or loan can cost you for years.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. List your tasks for two weeks and hire for the ones that need a checklist, not you.
> 2. Put creator deals and supplier payment terms in writing, and confirm bank details by phone.
> 3. Compare funding by its true cost and only borrow for stock that already sells.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Contractor:** Someone who runs their own business and does work for you, rather than being your employee.
> - **Usage rights:** Permission to use a creator's content in one place, like paid ads. It lasts for a set time.
> - **Revenue-based financing:** A loan you repay as a share of your daily sales. Payments rise and fall with sales.
> - **Creator:** A person who makes social content and can feature your product.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 26.1: Your first hire
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Your first hire should give you back hours. Pick work that does not need you.

**Step 1.** For two weeks, log every task and its time. Mark each one: only I can do it, someone could do it with a checklist, or it should stop.

**Expected result:** You have a task log with every task marked.

**Step 2.** Group the checklist tasks into one role, such as customer service, order problems, product uploads and reporting.

**Expected result:** You have a first role worth about 10 hours a week or more.

**Step 3.** Write the role as outcomes, for example "every customer email answered within one business day".

**Expected result:** Success is measurable.

**Step 4.** Give shortlisted people a small paid trial task that matches the real work.

**Expected result:** You see real work before hiring.

**Step 5.** Agree terms in writing: hours or deliverables, rate, notice, confidentiality and that their work belongs to you.

**Expected result:** A signed agreement exists.

**Step 6.** Check whether the person is an employee or contractor based on the real arrangement, not the label.

**Expected result:** You know which rules apply.

**Watch out:** Australia: see the ATO [employee or contractor guide](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/employee-or-independent-contractor/difference-between-employees-and-independent-contractors) and [Fair Work](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/). Super guarantee is 12% of eligible pay (see [ATO: super guarantee](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/key-superannuation-rates-and-thresholds/super-guarantee)). From 1 July 2026, super is paid each payday. Some contractors paid mainly for their labour also get super (see [ATO: super for contractors](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers/work-out-if-you-have-to-pay-super/super-for-independent-contractors)). US: the IRS looks at control and independence (W-2 or 1099) (see [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee)).

**Step 7.** Write the SOPs (how the work is done) before you hand it over.

**Expected result:** A new hire can follow written steps from day one.

**Step 8.** Plan 3 to 6 months ahead, and test candidates with a real paid work sample.

**Expected result:** You hire on evidence, with time to train.

**Step 9.** For senior hires, test before you commit.

1. Write what they must achieve in 12 months.
2. Call at least two references.
3. Start with a paid trial project.

**Expected result:** Senior hires have clear goals and have proven themselves.

### Good to know

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant. Also check the official sources above.

## Lesson 26.2: Working well with a virtual assistant
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** A remote helper can be a great first hire. The work must be written down and access must be safe.

**Step 1.** Record your screen doing each task once while talking through it, then turn it into a short checklist.

**Expected result:** Every task has a checklist.

**Step 2.** Give the assistant their own logins with only the access they need (see [Lesson 24.1](24-account-safety-agencies.md#lesson-241-lock-down-every-account)).

**Expected result:** No password is shared.

**Step 3.** Agree working hours that overlap with yours for at least an hour a day.

**Expected result:** You can talk live every day.

**Step 4.** Set 3 weekly numbers they own, such as reply time, tickets closed and errors.

**Expected result:** Their results are measurable.

**Step 5.** Meet for 15 minutes weekly to review the numbers and update checklists.

**Expected result:** Checklists improve each week.

**Step 6.** Pay on time, in their currency if you can, through a trackable method.

**Expected result:** Payments are on time and recorded.

**Watch out:** Check market rates for the skill and country and pay fairly; underpaid assistants leave fast.

**Step 7.** When they leave, remove access the same day.

**Expected result:** Former staff have no access.

## Lesson 26.3: Paying creators: agreements, disclosure and tax
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Creators are paid with free product, a flat fee, commission or a mix. Clear written terms prevent arguments later. For rates and usage, see [Lesson 6.4](06-creators-ugc-and-video-ads.md#lesson-64-deals-rates-and-usage-rights).

**Step 1.** Split every price into parts: making the content and posting it. Then usage rights: using it in your ads, and for how long. Add exclusivity and raw footage.

**Expected result:** You know what each part costs.

**Step 2.** Put it in writing: what they make, dates, changes and usage rights. Add the payment date and what happens if a post is late.

**Expected result:** Every deal has a written agreement.

**Step 3.** Count gifting costs (product, shipping, your time) when comparing it with paid work.

**Expected result:** You compare deals fairly.

**Step 4.** Make sure every paid or gifted post is clearly labelled as an ad.

**Expected result:** All posts are disclosed.

**Watch out:** Australia: see [ACCC: influencer endorsements](https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/publications/social-media-influencer-testimonials-and-endorsements); GST registered creators add GST. If a creator billing you as a business does not give you an ABN, you may have to withhold 47% of the payment (see [ATO: supplier not quoting an ABN](https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/statement-by-supplier-not-quoting-an-abn)). US: see [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers); get a Form W-9 before you pay; creators paid $2,000 or more in 2026 usually need a 1099-NEC (see [Lesson 23.6](23-accounting-bookkeeping-tax-time.md#lesson-236-year-end-and-tax-season-in-the-us)).

**Step 5.** Pay on time and keep invoices.

**Expected result:** Records are complete.

**Step 6.** Track each creator's sales from their code or link and how their content performs as an ad.

**Expected result:** You know which creators to rebook.

### Good to know

> **General information, not tax or legal advice.** Check with a registered tax agent or accountant.

## Lesson 26.4: Paying overseas suppliers and managing currency
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** When you pay in another currency, the exchange rate margin can cost more than the bank fee. Invoice scams also target importers, so confirm before you pay.

**Step 1.** For one real payment, compare the total cost at your bank and at least one money transfer service. Check your rate against the mid-market rate, plus fees.

**Expected result:** You know the cheapest way to pay.

**Step 2.** Pay in the supplier's currency when that is cheaper than letting them convert.

**Expected result:** You avoid a double conversion.

**Step 3.** Agree payment terms in writing: the deposit and when the rest is paid. Say what happens if goods are late or faulty (see [Lesson 19.8](19-inventory-cash-planning.md#lesson-198-finding-and-vetting-suppliers)).

**Expected result:** Terms are written down.

**Step 4.** Before any first payment or change of bank details, call the supplier to check the account. Use a number you already have.

**Expected result:** Bank details are verified by phone.

**Watch out:** Never trust new bank details sent only by email. Report scams at [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/) (Australia) or the [FTC](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) (US).

**Step 5.** If paying by card, check surcharges and your card's foreign transaction fee.

**Expected result:** You know the card's true cost.

**Step 6.** For large payments due months ahead, ask your provider about fixing a rate in advance.

**Expected result:** Your future cost is locked in.

### Good to know

> **General information, not financial advice.** Check with your accountant or bank.

## Lesson 26.5: Funding growth: comparing financing offers
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Revenue-based loans, platform cash advances and bank loans can pay for stock of a proven product. But they make things worse when they pay for losses. See also [Lesson 19.5](19-inventory-cash-planning.md#lesson-195-should-you-borrow-weighing-up-a-loan).

**Step 1.** Write down exactly what you would buy and how it pays back. For example: more stock of a product that already sells at a profit.

**Expected result:** You have a clear use for the money.

**Step 2.** For each offer, record amount received, total repaid, share of daily sales taken and expected repayment time.

**Expected result:** Offers sit side by side.

**Step 3.** Work out the true cost: total repaid minus amount received, divided by amount received. Then think about how fast you repay.

**Expected result:** You know the real cost. A flat fee repaid in 4 months costs far more per year than over 12 months.

**Step 4.** Check what happens if sales drop: share-of-sales repayments slow down; fixed weekly ones do not.

**Expected result:** You know your downside.

**Step 5.** Model cash flow with repayments included before you sign.

**Expected result:** You can afford it if sales drop by a quarter.

**Step 6.** Read for personal guarantees, early repayment rules and what the lender can take if you stop paying.

**Expected result:** There are no hidden traps.

**Watch out:** Look at [business.gov.au grants](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs) (Australia) and [SBA loans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/) (US).

### Good to know

> **General information, not financial advice.** Check with your accountant before you borrow.

## Lesson 26.6: Keep tool and subscription costs in check
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Apps and tools creep up quietly. Many charge by contacts, orders or users, so the bill grows with you even when the value does not.

**Step 1.** Each quarter, export card and bank statements and list every subscription with its monthly cost.

**Expected result:** You have a full list of tools and costs.

**Step 2.** For each tool, write what it does for sales or time saved and cancel anything you cannot justify.

**Expected result:** Only useful tools remain.

**Watch out:** Uninstall apps properly in Shopify; some leave code or keep billing.

**Step 3.** Remove email and SMS contacts who have not opened or clicked for a long time. Send a last win-back message first (see [Module 16](16-email-sms-whatsapp.md)).

**Expected result:** Your contact-based bill drops.

<!-- do:flow-win-back -->

**Step 4.** Check plan limits before a big sale.

**Expected result:** No surprise tier jump.

**Step 5.** Pay yearly only for tools you are sure to keep.

**Expected result:** You are not locked into tools you drop.

**Step 6.** Enter total tool costs in your Helix monthly fixed costs.

**Expected result:** Net profit includes tools.

## Lesson 26.7: Founder load: stress, burnout and getting help
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Running a store can be lonely, and money worries can keep you awake. Looking after yourself is part of running the business.

**Step 1.** Look at numbers on a schedule: profit each morning in Helix and cash once a week.

**Expected result:** Regular small check-ins replace dread.

<!-- do:growth -->

**Step 2.** Keep a cash buffer you do not touch, even a small one.

**Expected result:** You know there is a safety net.

**Step 3.** Write down your 3 biggest worries and one small next step for each.

**Expected result:** Each worry has an action.

**Step 4.** Protect sleep, movement and at least one day a week away from the business.

**Expected result:** One day off a week is on your calendar.

**Step 5.** Talk to someone: a friend, a peer, a mentor or a professional, without waiting for a crisis.

**Expected result:** You have someone to call this week.

**Step 6.** Hand off one draining task this month (see Lesson 26.1).

**Expected result:** One task is off your plate.

**Step 7.** Judge results on the last 3 days and month to date, not one day.

**Expected result:** One bad day does not change your plans.

<!-- do:growth -->

**Step 8.** Keep a wins file of good results and kind customer messages, and keep your decision log.

**Expected result:** You can see your progress and sensible choices when it gets hard.

**Step 9.** Talk to other founders regularly, and in busy seasons plan sleep and exercise like any other task.

**Expected result:** You have support and protected recovery time.

### Good to know

**Australia:** Beyond Blue (1300 22 4636) and Lifeline (13 11 14) offer free support at any hour. Beyond Blue also runs free coaching for small business owners. The Small Business Debt Helpline (1800 413 828) helps with money worries. See [business.gov.au: mental health and wellbeing support](https://business.gov.au/risk-management/mental-health/mental-health-and-wellbeing-support-for-business).

**US:** call or text 988 for the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline at any hour. See [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/).

**UK:** call Samaritans free on 116 123 at any hour. See [samaritans.org](https://www.samaritans.org/).

## Lesson 26.8: Should you sell on Amazon?
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Amazon can add sales, but it has its own fees, rules and competition. Decide with numbers, not fear of missing out.

**Step 1.** Search Amazon for your product type and note prices, review counts and whether copies already exist.

**Expected result:** You know the competition.

**Watch out:** Smaller Amazon marketplaces outside the US have less demand, so check demand first.

**Step 2.** Use Amazon's fee calculator to work out profit per unit. Take the price and subtract referral fee, fulfilment fee (if Amazon ships), storage, ads and returns.

**Expected result:** You know profit per unit after all fees.

**Step 3.** Choose how to sell. You can be a seller (you set prices and listings) or, if invited, a wholesale supplier to Amazon.

**Expected result:** You have a selling model.

**Step 4.** Apply for your trademark and enrol in Amazon Brand Registry before you list. Some countries accept a pending trademark, so check [Amazon's eligibility rules](https://brandservices.amazon.com/eligibility) for yours.

**Expected result:** Your brand is protected.

**Step 5.** List only your best seller first and keep its stock separate.

**Expected result:** Your own store never runs out.

**Step 6.** After 90 days, review profit, the effect on your own store and time spent.

**Expected result:** You have a keep or stop decision. Marketplaces usually collect US sales tax for you (see [Lesson 22.3](22-tax-gst-sales-tax-duties.md#lesson-223-us-sales-tax-nexus-registering-and-filing)).

## Lesson 26.9: Selling to shops: a simple wholesale start
<!-- stage:grow -->

**Why this matters:** Shops that stock your product put it in front of people who never see your ads. Margins are lower per unit, but orders are bigger and there is no ad cost per sale. Done carefully, wholesale adds steady volume.

**Step 1.** Work out your wholesale price: a common rule of thumb is that shops expect to buy at about half the retail price. Check with your profit setup that landed cost, packaging and freight still leave a profit at that price.

**Expected result:** A wholesale price for each product that still makes money. Or a list of products you cannot sell wholesale.

**Step 2.** Write simple terms on one page: minimum order, payment terms (upfront for first orders) and delivery times. Promise not to undercut your stockists on your own site outside planned sales.

**Expected result:** A one-page wholesale terms sheet.

**Step 3.** List 20 independent shops whose customers match yours. Send each a short, personal email with photos, prices and your terms, and offer a small first order.

**Expected result:** 20 shops contacted, with replies tracked in a simple sheet.

**Step 4.** Set up wholesale ordering in Shopify, with a separate price list or a wholesale app. Keep wholesale stock separate in your stock check.

**Expected result:** Stockists can reorder without emails back and forth, and retail stock is protected.

**Watch out:** Big chains can ask for long payment terms, returns of unsold stock and heavy discounts. Start with independent shops, and only say yes to terms your cash flow can carry.
