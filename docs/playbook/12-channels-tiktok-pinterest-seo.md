## Lesson 12.1: TikTok readiness
<!-- stage:attract -->

Get ready before you spend money on TikTok ads.

1. **Post for free (organically) first.** Learn what kind of videos work for you.
2. **Build a bank of 10 to 15 videos** that are good enough to use as ads.
3. **Set up your accounts:** TikTok Business Center, an ad account, the TikTok pixel and its events (the pixel tells TikTok about views, carts and sales).
4. **Set up your report columns:** spend, cost per sale (CPA), add to cart, cost per add to cart, click rate (CTR), cost per click (CPC), 2-second and 6-second views, and the share who watched 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%.

**Decision rule:** only start TikTok ads once Meta and Google are profitable.

## Lesson 12.2: Structures by spend
<!-- stage:attract -->

| Daily spend | Setup |
| --- | --- |
| About $100 | One purchase campaign, plus a small campaign aimed at an earlier step (view content or add to cart). The earlier step gives TikTok more data to learn from. |
| About $200 | Add a catalog or shopping campaign. |
| About $300 or more | Add a traffic campaign to a landing page, or a video-view campaign, for cheap reach. Test each creative style separately. |

**Budgets:** a campaign budget lets TikTok share money between ad groups. An ad group budget lets you control each test.

## Lesson 12.3: Spark ads and creators
<!-- stage:attract -->

Spark ads run normal TikTok posts (yours or a creator's) as ads. They keep the post's likes and comments.

1. Ask the creator for an authorisation code. They make it in their TikTok settings.
2. Use the code in TikTok Ads Manager to run their post.

Creator videos are the main fuel for TikTok ads. See Module 19.

## Lesson 12.4: Optimising TikTok
<!-- stage:attract -->

1. **Check every day or two.** TikTok ads get tired fast, sometimes in 3 to 4 days.
2. **Watch early warning signs:** what each add to cart costs, cost per click, and how much of the video people watch. These get worse before cost per sale does.
3. **Turn off ads that eat the budget** with a poor cost per sale.
4. **If cost per sale rises after you turn off the earlier-step campaign,** turn it back on. TikTok needed that data.
5. **When things work:** make new hooks, try catalog or video shopping ads, use bundles to raise order size, then add new campaigns.
6. **Judge the channel by your total MER** (all ad spend ÷ all sales). A new channel usually starts at about 10% of your total ad budget.

## Lesson 12.5: TikTok search
<!-- stage:attract -->

Many people use TikTok like a search engine.

1. Say your keywords in the voice-over.
2. Put them in on-screen text and captions.
3. Make videos that answer common questions about your product type.

## Lesson 12.6: TikTok Shop
<!-- stage:attract -->

TikTok Shop lets people buy without leaving TikTok. It includes automated campaigns that aim for total sales (GMV), affiliate creators, and live shopping.

**Decision rule:** check your margin after TikTok's commission and fees before you commit.

## Lesson 12.7: Pinterest
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Free (organic) Pinterest:**

1. Add fresh pins every week.
2. Use search words in pin titles and descriptions.
3. Make boards by theme and season.
4. Pins keep getting found for months, much longer than social posts.

**Pinterest ads:**

1. Use catalog shopping ads.
2. Run consideration campaigns first (to get people interested), then conversion campaigns (to get sales).
3. Plan seasonal ads early. People plan ahead on Pinterest, often months before.

**Measuring Pinterest:** people take longer to decide, so use longer attribution windows. Count sales after views and saves, not only clicks. Compare with your total MER.

## Lesson 12.8: SEO step by step
<!-- stage:attract -->

SEO means search engine optimisation: getting found in Google without paying for ads.

1. **Research keywords.**
   - Start with buying searches ("buy linen shirt", "best linen shirt").
   - Check volume (how often people search), difficulty (how hard to rank) and intent (are they ready to buy?).
   - Pick about 10 keywords for each category to start.
2. **Fix your pages.**
   - One main heading (H1) per page.
   - Clear subheadings (H2, H3) in a logical order.
   - A page title and a meta description for each page. The meta description is the short summary under your link in Google. Keep it to about 150 to 160 characters.
   - Collection pages built around a keyword.
   - Links between your own pages.
   - Alt text on images.
3. **Write helpful content** that matches your keywords: buying guides, comparisons and how-tos.
4. **Check the technical side.**
   - Fast pages that work well on phones.
   - Structured data (code that tells Google the price, rating and stock).
   - A sitemap.
   - No broken links and no duplicate pages.
   - Check Google Search Console for errors.
5. **Get mentioned on other sites:** brand mentions, press and partnerships.

## Lesson 12.9: AI search and agentic commerce
<!-- stage:attract -->

More shoppers now ask AI assistants (like ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity) what to buy. Some assistants can even shop for them. This is called agentic commerce.

1. **Be the clearest, most trusted answer.** Show expertise, have plenty of reviews, and keep facts about your brand the same everywhere.
2. **Let trusted AI crawlers read your site.** Keep your product data clean. Add FAQ sections. Have clear policy pages (shipping, returns).
3. **Your store may show up inside AI shopping assistants.** Ads from product feeds inside chat assistants are starting to appear.
4. **Be talked about elsewhere.** AI answers draw on forums, review sites and videos.
5. **Track visits from AI assistants** in Google Analytics.

## Lesson 12.10: Instagram shopping
<!-- stage:attract -->

1. Set up your shop in Meta Commerce Manager.
2. Connect your product catalog and keep it in sync.
3. Tag products in your posts and Reels so people can tap to shop.
