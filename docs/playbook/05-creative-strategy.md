# Module 5: Creative strategy

**Outcome:** a simple system you can repeat to plan, write, film, test and improve ads that make people stop scrolling and buy.

**Related SOPs:** [07 Creative testing](../sops/07-creative-testing.md), [08 Briefs, UGC and creators](../sops/08-creative-briefs-ugc-creators.md)


<!-- plain:start -->
> **In plain words**
>
> **Stage:** Attract (get seen by people who want what you sell). Every lesson below is tagged with its stage; see [Attract, Convert, Grow](stages.md).
>
> **What it is:** A simple system for making ads: plan, write, film, test, repeat.
>
> **Why it matters:** The ad itself is the biggest lever you control. New, better ads are what keep sales growing.
>
> **Do this:**
>
> 1. Pick one reason to buy (an angle) and write 3 opening lines (hooks).
> 2. Make 3 short ads, phone video is fine.
> 3. Test them side by side and keep the one with the lowest cost per sale.
>
> **Words to know:**
>
> - **Creative:** The ad itself: the picture or video plus the words.
> - **Hook:** The first line or first 3 seconds of an ad. Its only job is to make people stop scrolling.
> - **Angle:** The reason to buy that an ad focuses on, like saving time or looking good.
> - **Hook rate:** Out of everyone who saw your video ad, the share who watched at least 3 seconds. It tells you if the opening grabs attention.
>
> All words are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Lesson 5.1: Why creative is the targeting now
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Creative means the ad itself: the picture or video plus the words. Ad platforms like Meta now find the buyers for you, so your creative decides who stops to look. A wide audience with many different ads usually beats a narrow audience with one style of ad.

**Step 1.** Open Meta Ads Manager and go to the **Ads** tab. Count how many different ads are live right now (different ideas, not just new sizes).

**Expected result:** You have a count. Fewer than 6 live ideas usually means the account is short of creative.

**Step 2.** Check that your main ad set for new people uses a broad audience. That means Advantage+ audience, or just country and age, not a stack of interests.

**Expected result:** Your targeting is wide and your ads do the targeting.

**Step 3.** Set a goal for a steady supply of new ads, for example 4 to 8 new ads every two weeks.

**Expected result:** You have a written creative volume target. Lesson 5.2 shows how to hit it.

## Lesson 5.2: The creative management cycle
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Making ads works best as a loop you repeat every week or two. Each turn of the loop makes the next batch smarter, because winners teach you why they won.

**Step 1.** **Research:** spend 20 minutes reading reviews and comments, browsing competitors' ads in the Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library) and noting trends.

**Expected result:** You have 3 to 5 notes on what buyers say and what others run.

**Step 2.** **Plan:** pick the pillar (topic), the angle (reason to buy) and the one thing you will test.

**Expected result:** You have a one-line test, for example "Prove pillar, durability angle, testing review-led hook vs demo hook".

**Step 3.** **Brief:** write one page per concept (ad idea) using the template in Lesson 6.9.

**Expected result:** Each concept has a brief anyone can produce from.

**Step 4.** **Produce:** make the ads yourself, with creators or with AI editing tools.

**Expected result:** You have finished files in 4:5 and 9:16 sizes.

**Step 5.** **Launch:** put the new ads live as a batch, not one at a time.

**Expected result:** All new ads start on the same day so they are judged fairly.

<!-- do:meta-draft -->

**Step 6.** **Learn:** after 7 days, score each ad and write one line on why each winner won.

**Expected result:** Your next brief starts from written learnings, not guesses.

## Lesson 5.3: Pillars and framing
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Every ad does one of four jobs, called creative pillars: teach, prove, feel or stand for something. Covering all four gives the platform more ways to reach different people.

**Step 1.** Label each live ad with one pillar. **Teach:** reasons and demos. **Prove:** reviews, results, tests. **Feel:** fears and wishes. **Stand for:** opinions, myth busting, us versus them.

**Expected result:** Every ad has one pillar label.

**Step 2.** Choose a focus for your next ad: the product, the brand, the result or the customer.

**Expected result:** The next brief names one pillar and one focus.

**Step 3.** Make at least one customer-focused ad showing the person and their life after using the product.

**Expected result:** You have a customer-focused ad, which often reaches the most people.

## Lesson 5.4: Stand-out stack
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** What makes you different comes in layers: feature, benefit, proof, personality and offer. Each ad should lead with one layer and back it with one or two others, so the message stays clear.

**Step 1.** Write one line for each layer. Feature: what it has. Benefit: what that does for the customer. Proof: why believe it. Personality: how it feels to buy from you. Offer: the deal.

**Expected result:** You have five lines, your stand-out stack.

**Step 2.** For your next ad, pick one layer to lead with and one or two to support it.

**Expected result:** The brief names a lead layer.

**Watch out:** During a sale, lead with the offer. Outside sales, lead with product and proof.

## Lesson 5.5: How much your buyer already knows
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** People are at different stages of knowing about you. Some have never heard of the problem, and some are ready to buy. Each stage needs a different ad. Matching the ad to the stage stops you shouting offers at strangers.

**Step 1.** Read the five stages in the table below. Label each live ad with the stage it speaks to.

**Expected result:** Every ad has a stage label.

**Step 2.** Check that cold audiences (people who do not know you) see early-stage ads. Warm audiences (visitors and people who engaged) should see later-stage ads.

**Expected result:** Each audience is getting ads made for its stage.

**Step 3.** Fill the biggest gap with one new ad in your next batch.

**Expected result:** The next batch covers a stage you were missing.

### Good to know

| Stage | What they know | What the ad should do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | They do not know they have the problem | Tell a story, spark curiosity, entertain, show a moment they relate to. |
| Problem aware | They know the problem | Name the pain. Show what it costs them not to fix it. |
| Solution aware | They know fixes exist | Show why your way is better. Compare. |
| Product aware | They know your product | Show proof, answer objections, make the offer. |
| Most aware | They are ready to buy | Offer, a reason to buy now, a reminder. |

## Lesson 5.6: Why people buy: the motives behind a purchase
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Buying motives are the deep reasons people buy. They include belonging, status, fear of missing out, curiosity, relief, getting better, ease and value. One driver per ad keeps the message sharp.

**Step 1.** Pick one driver for each ad in your next batch and write it on the brief.

**Expected result:** Each brief names exactly one driver.

**Step 2.** Match the feeling to the moment.

1. Sale ads: show the saving and the end date.
2. Launch ads: tease what is new and who it is for.

**Expected result:** Your sale and launch ads pull on different drivers.

## Lesson 5.7: Hooks (first 0 to 3 seconds)
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** The hook is the first line or first 3 seconds of an ad. Its only job is to stop the scroll. Most viewers leave in the first quarter of a video, so the hook matters most.

**Step 1.** Write 3 hooks (the first 3 seconds) for your next ad, each a different type.

- Movement or a surprising first picture.
- Calling out a group ("For runners with flat feet...").
- A bold statement, or the result first.
- A one-star review read out and answered.
- A price comparison, a question that makes people curious, or a gossip-style opener.

**Expected result:** You have 3 hook options of different types.

**Step 2.** Make one version of the ad for each hook, keeping the rest identical.

**Expected result:** You have 3 ads that differ only in the first 3 seconds.

**Step 3.** After 7 days, compare hook rate (3-second video plays divided by impressions) in Ads Manager.

**Expected result:** You know which hook stops the most people. Above about 25% is a good hook rate for most accounts.

**Watch out:** A bold headline on a still image can work as well as a clever video hook. Test statics too.

## Lesson 5.8: Script frameworks
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** A script framework is a proven order for the parts of an ad. Picking one stops rambling. It also makes sure every ad ends with a clear call to action: the line that tells people what to do.

**Step 1.** Pick one framework from the table below, such as "problem, make it worse, solve" or "N reasons why".

**Expected result:** Your brief names the framework.

**Step 2.** Fill in each part of the framework in one or two lines.

**Expected result:** You have a draft script of 20 to 45 seconds.

**Step 3.** End with a clear call to action: what to do ("Tap Shop now"), what they get, and why now. State the offer and any minimum clearly ("20% off your first order, free shipping over $80").

**Expected result:** The script ends with a call to action that has all three parts.

### Good to know

| Framework | The order |
| --- | --- |
| Problem, make it worse, solve | Pain, then why it gets worse, then the product, then proof, then what to do next. |
| N reasons why | Hook, then 3 to 5 reasons, then the offer. Make the reasons about the customer's life, not product specs. |
| Myth buster | "Everyone says X. Here's why that's wrong", then the truth, then the product. |
| Bold statement | One big claim, then 3 things that back it up, then what to do next. |
| Day in the life | Their routine, then the moment they need it, then the product, then the result. |
| Comparison | You versus the usual option. Be honest and specific. |
| Why now | A season, event or life moment, then the product, then a time-limited reason. |
| Founder opinion | A strong view, then a story, then an invitation. |
| Steps | "3 outfits, 3 ways, 3 steps" style how-to. |

## Lesson 5.9: Emotion arcs for launches and sales
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** During a launch or sale, feelings change day by day. Ads that match the mood of each phase feel natural and convert better.

**Step 1.** Before the launch, run teaser ads that build anticipation.

**Expected result:** Teasers are live 3 to 7 days before launch.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 2.** On launch day, swap to excitement ads; during the sale, add proof and reviews for reassurance.

**Expected result:** Ads change on launch day and again mid-sale.

**Step 3.** In the last 24 hours, run urgency ads ("ends tonight"); afterwards, post a thank-you with results.

**Expected result:** The final day and the day after each have their own message.

## Lesson 5.10: Native-feeling ads
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Native ads look like normal posts in the feed, not like ads, and they usually do better. A phone, real people and quick changes on screen are enough.

**Step 1.** Film on a phone with a real person in a real place.

**Expected result:** The footage looks like a post a friend would share.

**Step 2.** Put movement in the first 3 to 5 seconds. In longer videos, change something every 1 to 2 seconds: a cut, a new angle, text or a zoom.

**Expected result:** The edit never sits still for long.

**Step 3.** Add captions to every video.

**Expected result:** The ad makes sense with the sound off.

**Step 4.** Borrow a popular format from your feed and use it with your product and your own words.

**Expected result:** The ad follows a familiar structure but every word and shot is yours.

**Watch out:** Never copy someone else's content.

## Lesson 5.11: Macro and micro iteration
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Iteration means making new versions. About 30% of what you make should be brand new ideas (big swings). About 70% should be new versions of ads that already work (small tweaks). Writing down what you expect before each test is what makes it a test.

**Step 1.** Split your next batch about 30/70. 30% are new angles or pillars. 70% are new versions of winners with a new hook, first frame, creator, length or captions.

**Expected result:** Your batch plan shows the split.

**Step 2.** Write a guess for each ad. For example: "A review hook will beat the demo hook on hook rate".

**Expected result:** Every new ad has a written guess.

**Step 3.** As spend grows, move more budget to proven winners.

**Expected result:** Winners get the most spend, new ideas get enough to be judged.

**Watch out:** The biggest mistake is testing everything at once with no idea what you are trying to learn.

## Lesson 5.12: Content that shows the ad system who buys
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** When an ad is clear about who it is for, the platform can find the right people more easily. A clear niche, a clear use and a clear type of person on screen beat a generic message.

**Step 1.** For your next ad, name one clear group, like new mums, trail runners or home bakers. Show that kind of person using the product.

**Expected result:** Someone watching can tell in 3 seconds who the ad is for.

**Step 2.** Say the group's name out loud or on screen in the first line.

**Expected result:** The ad speaks directly to one group.

## Lesson 5.13: Reasons to buy now, without fake shortages
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** You can give people a reason to buy now without a discount, which protects your margin. There are six honest options.

**Step 1.** Pick one reason to buy now for this month. For example: the season ("before summer"), an event, a limited bundle, a free gift for a set time, or a delivery cut-off ("order by Friday for Christmas").

**Expected result:** You have one urgency reason with a real date.

<!-- do:calendar -->

**Step 2.** Add it to one ad and to the top of your product pages.

**Expected result:** Shoppers see the reason to act now in the ad and on the page.

## Lesson 5.14: Win over people who are still deciding
<!-- stage:convert -->

**Why this matters:** Warm audiences know you but have not bought yet. They need answers, not more awareness ads.

**Step 1.** Make one ad for warm audiences. Pick from: a comparison, reviews, an FAQ video, your guarantee, a "what you get" breakdown or a creator review.

**Expected result:** You have at least one answer-led ad.

**Step 2.** Run it to people who visited or engaged in the last 30 days.

**Expected result:** Warm visitors see an ad that answers their doubts.

## Lesson 5.16: Production tools
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** The right tools make production fast and keep ads looking right on every placement. A few settings checks before launch prevent ugly surprises.

**Step 1.** Make still-image templates in 4:5 (feed) and 9:16 (stories and reels) with text kept inside the safe zones.

**Expected result:** Text is never covered by buttons or captions.

**Step 2.** In your phone editor, cut silences, add captions and a text hook, and export at each platform's sizes.

**Expected result:** Each video is ready in both sizes.

**Step 3.** Let AI tools do the busy work.

1. Use an AI tool to resize your ad and make a few versions.
2. Add an AI voice-over if you want one.
3. Label AI-made people and voices where the platform's rules say so.

**Expected result:** You have more versions in less time, with labels where needed.

**Watch out:** Never fake reviews with AI.

**Step 4.** Before launch, check the automatic changes.

1. In the ad, scroll to the creative options.
2. Find the auto-enhance settings.
3. Turn off any that change your ad in a way you do not want.

**Expected result:** The ad shows exactly as you designed it.

## Lesson 5.17: Big brand moments
<!-- stage:attract -->

**Why this matters:** Large brands spend heavily on seasonal and cultural moments. Watching them gives you free ideas you can adapt to your size.

**Step 1.** Once a month, note 3 campaigns big brands run around a season or moment.

**Expected result:** You have a short list of ideas.

**Step 2.** Make one fit your size. Try a trend format, a team-up with another small brand, or a playful take on a trend.

**Expected result:** Your next batch includes one moment-led ad.

## Self-check

1. What share of your ads should be new versions of ideas that already work?
2. Which awareness stage suits a one-star-review hook?
3. Name three reasons to buy now that are not discounts.
4. What does it mean to borrow a format?
5. What should a creator brief always say about rights?

1. About 70%.
2. Product aware. It answers an objection about your product.
3. Any three of: seasonal timing, a free gift for a set time, delivery cut-off dates, limited bundles, events.
4. Using a popular structure with your own product and your own words.
