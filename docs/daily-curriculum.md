### Day 1: Tell Helix your costs

*Topic: Profit, not sales · Stage 1 · 10 minutes · SOP 02*

**Why it matters.** Sales show how busy you were. Profit shows what you kept. To work out your profit every day, Helix needs to know your costs. This takes 10 minutes and you only do it once.

**How to do it.**

1. Open Your numbers (the scorecard) and find the Settings box.
2. Type your monthly fixed costs. These are bills that stay the same, like rent, software, staff and your own wage.
3. Type a monthly sales target you believe you can hit.
4. Type a target MER. If you are not sure, use 30%.
5. Press Save.

**Want me to do it for you?** Fill in your costs from your store data (Free plan, after you approve).

**See the effect.** Update your numbers each morning. Over the next 7 days, watch: Profit at the top of your dashboard.

### Day 2: Add yesterday's numbers

*Topic: Your daily numbers · Stage 1 · 5 minutes · SOP 02*

**Why it matters.** Two minutes each morning tells you if yesterday made money. Doing this every day is the habit that makes everything else work.

**How to do it.**

1. Go to Today and find the 'Update yesterday' box at the top.
2. Type yesterday's sales and number of orders.
3. Check the ad spend. If Meta is connected, Helix fills it in for you.
4. Press Save.
5. Read the big profit number and the one line under it.

**Want me to do it for you?** Pull yesterday's numbers in for you each morning (Starter plan, after you approve).

**See the effect.** Update your numbers each morning. Over the next 7 days, watch: Profit, every day.

### Day 3: Find the most you can pay for a sale

*Topic: Break-even · Stage 1 · 10 minutes · SOP 02*

**Why it matters.** Every ad decision needs a line in the sand. Break-even is the point where a sale makes $0. Spend less than that per sale and you make money. Spend more and you lose money.

**How to do it.**

1. Open Your numbers (the scorecard).
2. Find 'Break-even ROAS' and 'Break-even CPA' in the channel box. Helix works these out for you.
3. Write both numbers down.
4. Write your target cost per sale too. Helix shows it next to the break-even number.
5. Stick the note where you will see it when you check your ads.

**Want me to do it for you?** Not for this one. It is quick to do yourself and you learn the most by doing it.

**See the effect.** Update your numbers each morning. Over the next 7 days, watch: Ad cost per sale (CPA) compared with your break-even.

### Day 4: Find the one thing holding profit back

*Topic: Find the bottleneck · Stage 1 · 15 minutes · SOP 03*

**Why it matters.** Profit usually stalls for one of three reasons: costs are too high, too few visitors buy, or not enough people visit. Fixing the wrong one wastes months. Today you find the right one.

**How to do it.**

1. Open Your numbers (the scorecard) and look at the coloured flags.
2. Find the number with a red flag. If there is none, pick the amber one.
3. Open Lesson 2.2 and find the matching situation.
4. Write down one fix to work on this week.

**Want me to do it for you?** Look at your numbers and tell you the bottleneck (Starter plan, after you approve).

**See the effect.** Update your numbers each morning. Over the next 7 days, watch: The flagged number on Your numbers.

**Why it matters.** Meta and Google both claim the same sale. Turning one off for a week shows what is truly bringing in sales.

**How to do it.**

1. Choose one campaign or channel.
2. Pause it for a week, or only in one area.
3. Compare total sales and profit with the week before.
4. Decide: keep it, cut it or change it.

**Want me to do it for you?** Not for this one. It is quick to do yourself and you learn the most by doing it.

**See the effect.** Update your numbers each morning. Over the next 7 days, watch: Total sales and profit.

### Day 63: Give one weekly job to AI

*Topic: AI helpers · Stage 7 · 20 minutes · SOP 18*

**Why it matters.** AI is good at repeat work: summing up reviews, drafting emails, writing ad versions and checking data. Pick one job, explain it clearly and check what it gives you.

**How to do it.**

1. Pick one weekly job, like a report, an email draft or a review summary.
2. Write the background: your brand, your customer, your rules and an example.
3. Run it and check every fact.
4. Save your instructions so you can reuse them.
