# SOP 17: Inventory, cash and planning

> **In plain words.** Growth should not break the business. Never run out of your best sellers, and do not get stuck with slow stock. Always know your cash for the coming months.

**Purpose.** Make sure growth does not break the business. Never run out of best sellers or drown in slow stock. Know the cash position months ahead.

**Copilot triggers**
- Any top-20 product drops below its reorder point or under 4 weeks of cover, or rises above 26 weeks.
- Scaling plans would sell through stock before the next shipment lands.
- Monthly review. Quarterly year-plan update.
- The user asks "should I slow down ads or air-freight stock?".

## A 10-minute weekly stock check

1. For each product: units sold per week (4-week average), units on hand, units on order and arrival date.
2. Weeks of cover = (on hand + arriving before stockout) ÷ weekly sales.
3. ABC grade: A = top products making about 80% of revenue, B = next 15%, C = the rest.
4. Flags: A products whose cover is less than lead time, shipping time and safety buffer combined (reorder now). Any product over 26 weeks (plan to clear with soft tools).
5. Purchase order timing: order date = stockout date - supplier lead time - shipping time - safety buffer. Start with a buffer of about 2 weeks, more for long sea freight.

## Stockout decision (scaling faster than stock)

Compare three costs: profit lost by slowing ads, extra cost of faster freight, and profit lost while out of stock. Being out of stock also stalls ads. Often the best answer is a mix. Shift ad spend to other products and take preorders with a clear dispatch date. Air-freight a portion. Helix models the options.

US preorders must ship by the date you state, or within 30 days if you state none. If you will be late, offer a new date or a refund (see [FTC: Mail Order Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule)).

## Cash

- Keep a 13-week cash forecast, one column per week, updated weekly (Lesson 14.4). Add monthly columns beyond that for big stock buys.
- Rows: opening cash, sales money, stock payments (deposits and balances), ad spend, fixed costs, tax and loan repayments.
- Set aside a share of profit for income tax each month. About 25% is a common starting point; confirm with an accountant.
- Also move GST or sales tax you collect into a separate account. It is not yours to spend.
- Supplier terms: ask for lower deposits, longer payment terms, or staged shipments. Small changes free up a lot of cash.

## Year plan

1. Revenue target from recent growth and planned events.
2. Back-solve monthly revenue curve (with peaks from SOP 15).
3. Back-solve stock purchases by month and marketing budget by month.
4. Update the year plan and marketing calendar every quarter.
