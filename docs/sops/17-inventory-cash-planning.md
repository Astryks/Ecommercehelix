# SOP 17: Inventory, cash and planning

> **In plain words.** Growth should not break the business. Never run out of your best sellers, do not get stuck with slow stock, and always know how much cash you will have in the coming months.

**Purpose.** Make sure growth does not break the business: never run out of best sellers, never drown in slow stock, and always know the cash position months ahead.

**Copilot triggers**
- Any top-20 product drops under 4 weeks of cover, or rises above 26 weeks.
- Scaling plans would sell through stock before the next shipment lands.
- Monthly review. Quarterly year-plan update.
- The user asks "should I slow down ads or air-freight stock?".

## Weekly demand snapshot (10 minutes)

1. For each product: units sold per week (4-week average), units on hand, units on order and arrival date.
2. Weeks of cover = (on hand + arriving before stockout) ÷ weekly sales.
3. ABC grade: A = top products making about 80% of revenue, B = next 15%, C = the rest.
4. Flags: A products under 6 weeks (reorder), any product over 26 weeks (plan to clear with soft tools).
5. Purchase order timing: order date = stockout date - supplier lead time - shipping time - safety buffer.

## Stockout decision (scaling faster than stock)

Compare: profit lost by slowing ads vs extra cost of faster freight vs profit lost while out of stock (plus ads that stop working). Often the best answer is a mix: shift ad spend to other products, take preorders with a clear dispatch date, and air-freight a portion. Helix models the options.

## Cash

- Rolling cash forecast 3 to 12 months: opening cash, revenue, product payments (deposits and balances), ad spend, fixed costs, tax, loan repayments.
- Set aside a share of profit for tax each month (about 25% is a common starting point; confirm with an accountant).
- Supplier terms: ask for lower deposits, longer payment terms, or staged shipments. Small changes free up a lot of cash.

## Year plan

1. Revenue target from recent growth and planned events.
2. Back-solve monthly revenue curve (with peaks from SOP 15).
3. Back-solve stock purchases by month and marketing budget by month.
4. Review the marketing calendar every 6 months.
