# SOP 09: Google Shopping, Performance Max and Search

**Purpose.** Capture people already searching for what the store sells, at a return the economics can afford, with a clean product feed and a simple structure.

**Copilot triggers**
- Google Ads or Merchant Center connected.
- The store is profitable on its main channel and wants a second channel (usually Stage 1 to 2).
- Weekly search-term review (Monday).
- Merchant Center errors or disapprovals detected.
- The user asks "is Google right for me?" or "why did my PMax drop?".

## Is Google right yet?

Good signs: people search for the category or problem; the product has clear names; the feed is clean; Meta or organic is already profitable; there is at least $30 to $50 a day to give a campaign enough data. Weak signs: brand-new category nobody searches for; very low price point with low margin.

## Structure (default)

1. **Brand search:** a small campaign (often $20 to $30 a day) on your brand terms. Use target impression share or manual bidding. Expect a very low cost per sale and do not let it inflate your view of Google overall.
2. **Shopping or feed-only Performance Max:** non-brand prospecting with brand terms excluded. Group products by best sellers or by margin tier (custom labels), not by every collection.
3. **Non-brand Search (optional):** high-intent terms you want control over. Up to 5 keywords per ad group (1 to 2 broad, the rest exact), up to 15 headlines with 1 to 2 pinned, shared negative lists. Do not over-segment.
4. **Demand Gen or YouTube (Stage 2+):** short-form video discovery. Judge it with view-through and platform-comparable metrics, not last-click.

## Conversion setup

- One primary conversion: purchase. Everything else secondary.
- Enhanced conversions on.
- Check monthly that no second purchase action has become primary by itself (this doubles reported conversions and misleads bidding).
- Segment new vs returning customers if the account supports it, and set the new-customer value goal deliberately.

## Bidding

- Breakeven ROAS = 1 ÷ margin after variable costs (for example 60% margin means 1.67).
- Target ROAS = breakeven adjusted for the profit you want to keep. New-customer campaigns can run nearer breakeven.
- Levers are budget and target. Change one at a time, by modest steps.
- During the first 1 to 2 days of a big sale, a temporary seasonality adjustment (about +50% expected conversion rate) helps bidding catch up. Do not leave it on longer.

## Feed optimisation

1. Fix every Merchant Center error and warning first.
2. Titles: put the words people actually search for at the front (use converting search terms). Include brand, product type, key attribute (material, size, colour).
3. Images: clean main image; test lifestyle images where allowed.
4. Price and shipping must match the site exactly.
5. Use custom labels for margin tier, best seller, seasonal.

## Weekly routine (10 minutes)

- [ ] Search terms: add negatives, move converting terms into feed titles or exact keywords.
- [ ] Spend split: brand vs non-brand.
- [ ] Merchant Center diagnostics.
- [ ] Return by campaign vs target.

## Testing rule

Give a new campaign about 2 weeks of learning plus 4 weeks of evaluation at no less than about $50 a day. Avoid major edits to a new PMax in its first weeks.

## 2026 changes to watch

AI Max for Search and for Shopping, query-matched text, final URL expansion and AI Mode placements change what you control. Keep brand exclusions, review AI-generated copy and landing pages, and check search terms more often after any auto-upgrade. See sources in the Trends feed section of the product design doc.
