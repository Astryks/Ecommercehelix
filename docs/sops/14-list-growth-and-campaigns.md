# SOP 14: List growth and campaign calendar

> **In plain words.** Grow your own email and SMS list so you rely less on paid ads. Then send to it at a steady pace: often enough to earn, not so often that people leave.

**Purpose.** Grow an owned audience (email and SMS) that lowers dependence on paid ads. Send campaigns at a steady rhythm that earns without burning the list.

**Copilot triggers**
- Pop-up sign-up rate below 3%.
- No campaign sent in 14+ days.
- 6 to 10 weeks before a big sale (list-building window).
- Friday retention day.

## Pop-up and forms

- Rule of thumb: target a sign-up rate above 3%, and 6 to 10% with a strong offer.
- Two to three fields at most, as a rule of thumb. Multi-step forms (a one-click question, then email, then phone) often lift sign-ups. Test them against a single step.
- Ask one zero-party question ("What are you shopping for?") to personalise the welcome flow.
- Offer options: dollar-off for high AOV, percent-off for low AOV, or a high-value free gift to protect margin. Early access or a quiz result also work.
- Separate mobile and desktop designs. Make it easy to close on mobile.
- Get consent for email and SMS separately. Text sign-ups need their own clear opt-in (SOP 13 has the rules).

## Other list builders

- **Competitions and giveaways:** run them when ad costs are lower (before peak season) to collect future buyers cheaply. Collaborations with complementary brands widen reach. Watch email quality: low-intent sweepstakes entrants buy less. In Australia, games of chance may need a state or territory permit, depending on prize value (see [business.gov.au: legal obligations of marketing](https://business.gov.au/marketing/advertising/legal-obligations-of-marketing)). US sweepstakes rules vary by state: New York and Florida need registration and a bond when prizes total over $5,000 (see [New York law](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/369-E)).
- **Lead ads** on Meta for list growth when CPMs are low.
- **Hype pages** before launches and sales (SOP 15).
- **Quizzes** for products with choice complexity.

An engaged subscriber keeps earning a small amount every month. So judge cost per subscriber against expected value per subscriber per month.

## Campaign rhythm

| Stage | Campaigns |
| --- | --- |
| Just starting | 2 a month, building to 1 a week |
| Stage 1 to 2 | 1 to 2 a week |
| Stage 2 to 3 | 2 to 4 a week, with segmentation |

Mix: product stories, education (Teach pillar), proof, founder notes, new arrivals, offers (keep offers a minority).

## Segments

- Engaged (opened or clicked in 30/60/90 days), purchasers, VIPs (top 10% by spend) and lapsed buyers.
- Window shoppers (viewed products recently, never bought).
- Send most campaigns to engaged segments. Mail the full list rarely.

## Win-back and clean-up

- Win-back flow for customers past their expected repurchase window.
- Sunset flow for profiles that never engage, then suppress them. A smaller engaged list delivers better.
